Alice — thanks for the clear feedback. Short answer: we can get to a compromise without exposing Redwood to open-ended credit risk. High-level proposal attached (SLA_Template_v3.docx). Key points: 

- Baseline uptime: propose a 99.90% production baseline with an option to upgrade to 99.95% for a committed pricing tier. The rationale: 99.95% materially increases reserved capacity and cost; we can include a priced uplift in the contract to cover that.

- Monthly credits: introduce a staged credit schedule rather than a single linear bucket. Example (percent below baseline -> monthly credit as % of invoice): 0.1-0.5% -> 5% credit; 0.5-1.0% -> 12% credit; >1.0% -> 25% credit, with a maximum cap of 80% of the monthly invoice. Also propose a soft-first-month taper for new customers (first 3 months cap at 50%) to avoid outsized early credits while we optimize routing and caching.

- Latency: commit to P95 <= 250ms (US hosted) and P99 <= 800ms. If latency breaches persist for >24 hours and materially affect request success, customers can elect a latency credit defined as 10% of monthly invoice per full 24-hour window beyond a 24-hour remediation window, subject to the overall monthly cap above.

- Maintenance carveouts: scheduled maintenance (published 72h in advance) is excluded. Emergency maintenance and force majeure follow standard carveouts but we will provide rollback and failover runbooks. We propose a maximum of 8 scheduled maintenance hours per calendar month across all regions.

I've looped in Laura and Monica to confirm the commercial language and to size the uplift for a 99.95% commitment. If you prefer, we can model two priced options in a short table in the contract: Standard (99.90%) and Premium (99.95%, higher commit).
