Ben / Karthik —

Looping you in with Aurora’s asks (below). They need concrete wording for: refill cadence, credit exhaustion behavior, and rollback procedure. I sketched three options we can present: 

1) Fixed bucket + periodic refill
   - Allocate N burst credits per 24h window; unused credits do not carry over. Refill at 00:00 customer local-time.
   - On exhaustion: requests are routed to the shoulder slab with enforced queueing; latency degrades gracefully.

2) Leaky-bucket refill (preferred for smoothing spikes)
   - Credits drip back at configured rate (e.g., 420 credits/hr) with a short prewarm allowance. Better for spiky telemetry workloads.

3) Credit buffer + hard failover
   - Maintain small pre-warmed reserve (30–60s GPU prewarm) for high-priority routes; beyond that, automatically fallback to a smaller model variant and emit a billing credit for time spent under fallback.

Rollback triggers (proposed):
- Quality regression: >5% failure rate on prompt set OR sustained drop in top-k BLEU/ROUGE proxy for 15m
- Cost shock: token/unit cost > 2x projected for 30m
- Latency break: tail latency (p99) > SLO for 5 consecutive minutes

If a trigger fires:
1) Move traffic to a pinned lower-cost model variant automatically (can be per-route).
2) Apply rate-limited throttling and enqueue excess traffic.
3) Create an incident and notify Aurora primary ops and Redwood on-call.
4) Postmortem + potential crediting reversal for the impacted window.
