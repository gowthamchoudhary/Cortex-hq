Hi Sara — thanks for the clear examples and the spreadsheet; that helps. Looping in Marissa (sales pricing) and Aditya (billing/product) so they can give concrete math and contract language.

Quick answers at a high level:
- Full prefix/KV cache hits: we do not bill for generation tokens produced entirely from the cache — you will see zero generation tokens for those responses. There is still a small per-request routing/ingress tier fee for API calls (listed as "request unit" on the ratecard) to cover networking/metadata, but it's a fixed low amount and waivable at enterprise tiers.
- Partial hits: billing is prorated. The invoice separates tokens served from cache vs model-generated tokens; you'll only be charged for the model-generated portion. We also expose a per-request breakdown in the Console so you can reconcile at token-level.

Marissa/Aditya — Sara asked about grandfathering/exception language for a signed enterprise committed contract. They want: 1) waiver of the request-unit fee once commit thresholds are met; 2) a retrospective credit path in case of meter mismatch; and 3) a clear example calculation to include in the SOW.

Sara — thanks for the spreadsheet. I built two short billing scenarios below based on your volume estimates. Please find an example invoice attached.

Scenario A — conservative (65% full-hit, 20% partial, 15% miss)
- Monthly incoming requests: 12,000,000
- Request-unit fee: $0.0001 per request (waived at commit >= $50k/mo)
- Cache full-hit: 65% -> 7.8M requests billed at request-unit only (if not waived)
- Cache partial: 20% -> 2.4M requests, of which average 40% of tokens require model generation -> billed tokens = model_token_rate * (avg_generated_tokens * 2.4M)
- Miss: 15% -> fully billed on generation tokens

Scenario B — enterprise commit (waived request-unit, 70% hit, 20% partial, 10% miss)
- Same incoming requests, request-unit fee waived at your committed tier
- We apply a tiered discount to generated-token rates starting at month 1 per the deal (see attached ratecard: 8% discount at $70k, 12% at $120k monthly commit)

Retrospective credit policy (proposed):
- If Nimbus identifies a meter mismatch within 90 days, we will investigate. If error is on our side, credits are issued for the affected invoice period up to the verified overcharge amount.
- If the issue is ambiguous, we do a joint reconciliation; if the difference exceeds 2% of billed tokens for the month, Redwood issues a pro-rata credit while investigation proceeds.

Hi Sara — a few product-level clarifications to make auditing straightforward:

1) Invoice breakdown: every invoice line includes: request-count, cache_full_hit_count, cache_partial_count, model_generation_tokens, model_context_tokens, and effective token_rate applied. Exportable CSV in Console.

2) Partial-hit accounting: we report the cached-token-count separately and the generated-token-count separately; the charge is model_token_rate * generated-token-count. For transparency we can add a brief appendix to the SOW showing the formula we will use.

3) Minimum bill floor: for hosted API customers below a $1,000/mo commit we enforce a small monthly minimum to cover baseline infra; for enterprise-tier SOWs this is removed and replaced with commit/discount language.

4) Performance SLA and credits: cache-hit behavior doesn't affect latency SLA, but if there is an operational bug causing stale KV cache returns or incorrect hit attribution, we will treat it as a severity event and offer SLA credits per contract.
