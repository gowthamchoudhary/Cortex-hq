Thanks, Elena — and thanks Kim for bringing this forward.

Quick legal assessment and a counter-proposal for Griffin's team:

- Redwood's baseline: Redwood will indemnify for third-party claims that the Service infringes a third party's intellectual property rights, subject to the standard conditions (notice, cooperation, and sole defense control). Traditionally Redwood limits total liability under the Order Form to a multiple of fees or a fixed cap. Carve-outs are narrowly drawn to avoid open-ended exposure.

- Counter-proposal on monetary exposure: carve-out limited to amounts actually paid by Redwood in settlement or judgment, and subject to an aggregate ceiling equal to 12 months of paid fees under the Order Form (the 'backstop cap'). However, to protect Redwood's business, we request that the backstop cap be applied only after Redwood has exhausted commercially reasonable efforts to defend the claim, and that the cap exclude statutory penalties that are not indemnifiable by law.

- Non-monetary settlements / licenses: Redwood cannot accept unilateral customer approval rights over settlement terms that would impose ongoing licensing or product modifications. We can agree to consult and obtain Customer consent for settlements that impose non-monetary obligations, but consent must not be unreasonably withheld — and if the parties disagree, the parties should be able to seek expedited arbitration limited to whether the proposed settlement materially increases Redwood's liability beyond the monetary backstop.

- Notice and cure: propose a two-stage window: prompt written notice upon receipt of a claim; Redwood has 30 days to assume defense; if Redwood declines, Customer may assume with Redwood's reasonable cooperation. If Redwood assumes defense, Customer may participate at its own expense. Settlement decisions require Redwood's approval unless the settlement includes only monetary relief that does not impose non-monetary obligations on Redwood.

- Insurance: Redwood will provide evidence of commercially reasonable liability insurance with IP coverage components where available; Redwood cannot be required to maintain specialized IP infringement policies as a condition precedent.

- Optional mitigant: an escrow mechanism limited to the backstop cap amount for high-risk customers; Redwood prefers insurance over escrow as the primary mitigation. If Griffin insists on escrow, we should add a 12-month review and automatic release if there are no claims.

I've included redline language inline in the attached revised draft (see exposure_summary_table.pdf for the numbers and fallback options). If Griffin accepts the monetary backstop + the notice/cure + limited consult rights on non-monetary settlements, we can present to finance and legal to approve an exception.
