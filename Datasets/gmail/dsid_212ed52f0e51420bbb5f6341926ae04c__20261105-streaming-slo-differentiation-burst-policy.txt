Quick tech take (summary) — I pulled the attachments and ran preliminary perf estimates with infra: 

- Baseline: hosted streaming endpoints (shared) typically see median TTFT ~220–320ms depending on model and concurrent connections; P95 often in 400–600ms range under mixed load.
- Non-streaming (generate): TTFT is effectively higher because we wait for chunking/finish, but tokens/sec after generation can be higher for batch paths.

Feasibility notes for Sara’s ask: 
- TTFT median <=180ms is attainable if we 1) pin a Dedicated instance pool for their traffic, 2) pre-warm model weights and KV cache, and 3) enforce a small max context for streaming calls. With these, P50 ~120–180ms and P95 ~300–400ms is realistic.
- Sustained 500 tokens/sec: achievable on provisioned A100-class instances with quantized model and worker-level parallelism for short bursts (<=30s). For 800 tokens/sec peaks the risk of transient request queuing increases unless we reserve burst capacity (additional nodes).

Recommendations: 
1) Offer them a Dedicated streaming pool with pre-warmed nodes (pin count based on their concurrency).
2) Define a pilot SLO bundle: Streaming TTFT P50 <= 180ms, P95 <= 400ms; sustained throughput 450 tokens/sec (guaranteed) with a soft-cap for bursts up to 800 tokens/sec best-effort.
3) Add an automatic fallback policy: if Dedicated pool saturates, redirect some traffic to a compatible lower-SLO variant (async/generate) with graceful degradation.
4) Capture expected concurrency & avg tokens per request from Acme to size pool and price.

Cost notes: reserving pre-warmed Dedicated nodes and burst reserve will add ~20% premium on committed monthly spend for pilot sizing we just estimated. We should present a pilot addendum (SLA + pricing + capacity plan) rather than a permanent hosted SLO change.

I can draft a short SLO summary for legal if Rishi signs off.
