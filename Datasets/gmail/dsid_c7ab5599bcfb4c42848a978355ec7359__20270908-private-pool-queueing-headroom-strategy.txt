Hi Anya — thanks for the concise profile, this is perfect to start with. Quick summary and recommended starting point below; follow-ups have the math and operational notes.

TL;DR recommendation (initial):
- Start with an 8-GPU pool using H100-80GB class nodes (4 GPUs per host x 2 hosts), staged as: 6 GPUs "active" for steady-state, +2 GPUs reserved as immediate headroom/spare (rolling upgrades, instant bursts).
- Configure prewarmed KV cache for typical user prefixes; use an aggressive prefix cache TTL for chat to avoid token regen on cold slots.
- Network: 2x25Gbps NICs per host (bonded) minimum; prefer 100 Gbps if within budget for inter-node NVLink-like traffic and fast model shards.
- Autoscaling policy: fast vertical scaling for queued short spikes (scale up within 90s), and a conservative horizontal policy for sustained >5-minute load.

Rationale and sizing notes:
1) Throughput baseline math (approx):
- Measured p95 latency target forces low batching size for user-facing chat. With our runtime, an H100 at modest batching (batch size 4–8) sustains ~25–40 generated requests/sec for median prompt sizes (800 tokens) with p95 under 120–140ms.
- For 200 QPS steady: 200 / 30 rps ≈ 6.7 GPUs => round to 7 and add 20–25% headroom => 8 GPUs recommended.

2) Queueing/fallback model:
- Short spikes: accept a small, bounded queue with SLA-aware prioritization (user chat > embeddings). Implement request queuing with token-budgeted timeouts and an early-fallback policy to a smaller-generation model if queue depth crosses threshold.
- Use a two-tier autoscale: immediate (fast start) to bring spare GPUs online via warm standby, and slower scale for sustained traffic. Keep 1–2 GPUs as warm standbys to avoid cold-starts.

3) Cache & warmup:
- Pre-warm KV caches for top-1000 user prefixes and warm commonly used model weights on node boot. For your mix, cache hits can shave 20–40% of p95 latency on bursty chat.

4) Networking:
- For single-host 4-GPU nodes: at least dual 25 Gbps NICs; prefer 100 Gbps for multi-host models or sharded variants. Turn on jumbo frames and ensure MTU consistency. Avoid oversubscribed TOR links.

5) Operational constraints:
- Place GPUs across two racks (see Marissa's note below) to survive a rack-level failure and allow rolling firmware updates without dropping capacity below target.
- Plan maintenance windows with explicit prewarming after updates.

Operational notes from ops/hosting side:
- Power & thermal: H100 racks at 4 GPUs/host typically draw ~12–14 kW per rack at full tilt with standard fans. Confirm datacenter rack PDU limits; recommend at least 20% spare power headroom per rack.
- Rack layout: do not colocate all 8 GPUs in a single rack. Spread across 2 racks (4+4) to handle rack-level network faults and PDU failures.
- Spares & procurement: Have 1 spare GPU node that can be swapped in within 4 hours. Consider an on-call escalation runbook for failed NVLink/NIC issues.
- Maintenance: schedule rolling firmware/driver updates with warm-standby strategy and prewarm periods post-update (30–45 minutes for large models).
