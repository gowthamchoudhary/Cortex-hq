Connor / team —

Read Acme's redline. Summary opinion and recommended verbatim language below. High-level: Acme's asks are reasonable commercial land, but we should retain tight guardrails on carve-outs and cap exposure. Key negotiation levers: make carve-outs narrowly tied to (i) direct remediation costs (defined and documentary standard), (ii) regulatory fines where liability is imposed on Redwood (not third-party fines), and (iii) indemnified claims. Keep the overall cap as a hard cap (higher of 12 months fees or $1M) but exclude indemnity and IP breach claims from that cap only if we take indemnity — otherwise indemnities should be limited too.

Risks to watch:
- Broad carve-outs for "loss of business" or "lost profits" will blow the exclusion and increase expected loss significantly.
- Vague remediation carve-outs without a proof-of-cost standard create open-ended exposure.
- Mutualizing exclusions is acceptable commercially but increases retained risk; ask for concessions (e.g., higher minimum commitment or shorter SLA credits).

Suggested redline (mutual exclusion, narrow carve-outs):

"Neither Party shall be liable to the other Party for any consequential, special, incidental, indirect or punitive damages, including but not limited to lost profits, loss of business, loss of data, or business interruption, except to the extent such damages arise from: (a) a Party's breach of its confidentiality or data security obligations resulting in regulatory fines or documented third‑party claims against the other Party; (b) reasonable and documented remediation costs actually incurred by the non‑breaching Party to restore or remediate data or systems where such costs were caused by the breaching Party's gross negligence or willful misconduct; or (c) liabilities for which a Party has expressly agreed to indemnify the other under Section [Indemnity]."

Negotiation posture: offer the above but limit (a) regulatory fines recovery to fines actually imposed on the customer (with evidence) and (b) remediation costs to those pre‑approved by Redwood in writing where feasible and capped by the overall liability cap (i.e., the greater of the fees paid in the prior 12 months or $1,000,000), unless indemnity applies.

Counter-proposals to request from Acme:
- Mutual exclusion as drafted
- If Acme insists on uncapped regulatory fines or uncapped remediation, require a higher cap (e.g., 2x ARR or $5M floor) or an annual onboarding fee of $25k to offset tail risk
- Carve indemnity claims out of the cap only where Redwood's indemnity is accepted and subject to standard indemnity limitations (notice, cooperation, defense control)

Ellie —

Thanks for the redline and the clear asks. We discussed internally with legal and risk and propose the following compromise to present to Acme:

1) Mutual exclusion: Redwood will agree to mutual exclusion of consequential, special, incidental, indirect and punitive damages (explicitly including lost profits and business interruption).

2) Narrow carve-outs (limited): the exclusion will not apply to (i) documented regulatory fines actually imposed on the customer to the extent caused by Redwood's breach of data security or confidentiality obligations, (ii) reasonable, documented remediation costs actually incurred by the customer to restore data or remediate systems to the extent caused by Redwood's gross negligence or willful misconduct, and (iii) liabilities the parties have agreed are covered under the indemnity clause.

3) Cap: the liability cap will remain the greater of fees paid in the prior 12 months or $1,000,000. Carve-outs (i) and (ii) above will be subject to the cap unless they fall within an accepted indemnity (in which case indemnity rules apply).

4) Proof standard & controls: remediation costs must be reasonably documented and pre‑approved where feasible; regulatory fines recovery requires evidence of fines assessed and a causal link to Redwood's breach. Redwood reserves defenses for contributory customer conduct.

We believe this gives Acme the protections they need for regulatory exposure and remediation while keeping Redwood's tail risk contained. If Acme requests uncapped regulatory fines or uncapped remediation, our alternative position is to accept mutual carve-outs only if the cap is increased to $5M or if Acme accepts a one‑time onboarding fee (we can discuss offsets).
