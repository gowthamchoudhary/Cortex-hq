Ben/Laura/Sofia/Tom —

High-level legal take and proposed approach below. I attached a one-page summary and a tracked-change Order Form (we'll upload the docx after internal review).

1) Regulatory fines/penalties: We typically resist unlimited exposure. A common compromise is to exclude government-imposed fines from the general cap only if (a) the fine is directly and solely caused by Redwood's breach of its obligations under the Agreement (e.g., failure to maintain PCI controls), and (b) Redwood had actual knowledge and failed to cure — or the breach amounts to willful misconduct or gross negligence.

Proposed clause (summary): "Notwithstanding the foregoing, regulatory fines or penalties directly imposed on Customer by a governmental authority that result solely from Supplier's grossly negligent or willful breach of security or privacy obligations shall be excluded from the limitation of liability up to the amount of actual fines levied, but Supplier's liability shall not exceed [X] per event or [Y] aggregate unless caused by willful misconduct."

2) Third-party claims (card networks, acquirer fees): We prefer to treat these as indemnifiable where Redwood's processing error or noncompliance triggers such claims. We can accept an indemnity for third-party claims arising from Redwood's negligence, but would like to carve in procedural protections (timely notice, control of defense, settlement consent thresholds).

3) Insurance/mitigants: Recommend referencing existing cyber/technology E&O coverage and offering to add an affirmative representation of maintaining coverage. If Acme requires higher exposure limits, we can discuss surcharge or an addendum to the Order Form for elevated limits.

Open points for Acme to consider: whether they need an unconditional exclusion (they likely won't get one) vs. a conditional exclusion tied to Redwood's willful or gross negligence, and whether they require a separate bucket/cap for regulatory fines.
