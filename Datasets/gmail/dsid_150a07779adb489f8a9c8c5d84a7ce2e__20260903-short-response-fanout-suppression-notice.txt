We’re sending an interim advisory because we’ve detected a degradation affecting short-response (<=128 token) generation requests routed to our US-West hosted inference pool. Symptoms: elevated tail of streaming responses, occasional truncated chunks, and a reduction in downstream fanout throughput for endpoints that fan out many small requests (e.g., classification/batch-scorer endpoints).

What we know so far:
- Start time: ~14:00 PDT (2026-09-03)
- A subset of requests experienced slower chunk delivery and occasional retries; overall success rate remained >97% for most customers but StallionTech saw increased retry amplification on your scoring pipeline.
- Impacted routes: /v1/generate (short-response traffic), /v1/score (fanout workers).

Immediate mitigations we’ve applied:
- We’ve temporarily disabled a recent kernel rollout that changed KV cache eviction heuristics for short sequences.
- Traffic routing policy adjusted to route affected tokens to neighboring pools (US-Central) for your account where compatible.
- We enabled additional prefetching on fanout workers to reduce small-request latency.

What we’re doing next:
- Infra on-call (Jonas) is running a targeted rollback verification and will monitor p50/p95/p99 for your routes.
- We’ll continue to scale prefetch and monitor for retry amplification; if we don’t see improvement in 30–45 minutes we’ll roll back the canary completely.

Subject: Post-incident summary: short-response fanout suppression (US-West) — resolution and next steps

Hi Lydia and Stallion ops team,

Quick post-incident summary and immediate next steps. TL;DR: root cause identified, mitigation applied, full recovery confirmed, and next actions for prevention.

Incident timeline (summary):
- 14:00 PDT, 2026-09-03 — elevated tail and retry amplification observed for short-response fanout traffic in US-West.
- 14:12 PDT — customer advisory sent; temporary routing and prefetch mitigations applied.
- 14:28 PDT — evidence that some nodes still had kernel v2.9.1 loaded; we initiated a full rollback across US-West.
- 15:07 PDT — p95 for affected routes returned to baseline; retry amplification subsided.

Root cause (short):
- A canary kernel rollout (v2.9.1) changed an eviction heuristic intended to optimize long-sequence memory usage. For very short sequences at high concurrency + heavy fanout, the change caused frequent KV cache evictions and host rehydration leading to extra small-tail latency and retry amplification in fanout scenarios.

Mitigations completed:
- Rollback of kernel v2.9.1 across US-West pool.
- Temporary routing to US-Central for affected keys while we validated stability.
- Rate-limit and prefetch tuning on high-fanout routes to avoid retry amplification during rehydration windows.

Customer impact and remediation:
- StallionTech: experienced increased retries and a short-term cost impact from 14:00–15:07 PDT; functional correctness was preserved.
- We will proactively open an SLA remediation ticket with Billing and Customer Success. If you’d like to pursue a credit, we’ll expedite evaluation — I’ll CC Billing and your account rep.

Next steps and prevention:
- Full postmortem is available here: https://redwood.sharepoint.com/:u:/r/incident-postmortems/2026-09-03-fanout-postmortem (draft).
- Engineering will add a short-sequence/fanout validation block to the kernel rollout gating checklist and add a synthetic that reproduces the fanout pattern for future canaries.
- We’ll run a joint verification with StallionTech’s engineering team next week if you prefer — propose Mon 2026-09-07 10:00 PDT?
