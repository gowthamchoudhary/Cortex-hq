Thanks — looping in some bench-level detail so everyone has visibility. I attached a snapshot (gpu_throughput_snapshot.pdf) of recent runs we did across a comparable model family. Below are concrete mapping steps and a recommended pilot config.

Quick mapping of Soteria targets to bench observations:
- p95 <= 200 ms (short responses):
  - H100 (fp16) can achieve p95 ~= 140–190 ms at 200–300 concurrent short requests (model/sequence dependent).
  - A100 (fp16) tends to sit at p95 ~= 180–260 ms under the same concurrent load for streaming-disabled workloads.
- Streaming tail p95 < 800 ms:
  - H100 gives more headroom for per-chunk throughput at high concurrency (sustained chunking for 2k-token sessions). A100 can hit it but with less margin and higher queuing when bursts arrive.

Recommended pilot config (minimal viable):
- Reserved pool: 2x H100 nodes + 4x A100 nodes (start) with autoscale burst pool + pre-warmed KV cache for common prefixes.
- Baseline reserved throughput = 250 concurrent short requests (this maps to ~1200 tokens/sec sustained given your average prompt sizes).
- Burst policy: allow up to 350 concurrent by pulling from burst pool (pay-as-you-go nodes) with automatic fallback to quantized A100 instances after 60s of sustained overload.

Bench plan (3 business days):
1) Day 1: FP16 runs on A100 and H100 with sample prompts (steady state).
2) Day 2: Streaming/long-session runs on H100; measure per-chunk latencies and KV cache effectiveness.
3) Day 3: Quantized runs (int8 / 4-bit where applicable) to measure cost vs. latency trade-offs and validate graceful degradation behavior.

Deliverables: throughput/latency matrix (per GPU variant), tail latency graphs, recommended reserve sizing and expected monthly unit cost estimate for the baseline.
