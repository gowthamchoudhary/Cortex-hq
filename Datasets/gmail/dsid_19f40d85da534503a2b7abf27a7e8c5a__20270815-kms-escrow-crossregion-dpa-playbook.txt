Hi Priya — thanks for the clear list. Quick summary up-front, then I attached a short draft ops playbook and diagram (PDF + PNG).

Short answers:
- No plaintext persisted outside the pin-region for customer-controlled data. During an emergency failover we may route/stream encrypted payloads through a secondary region for processing; decrypted material is held only in memory on the serving host and is never written to long-term storage outside the selected region. Transient memory residency is limited to the length of the request processing (sub-second to seconds depending on model), and we log the event.
- We do not export customer master keys to third-party escrow. For customers who require escrow, we offer a KMS-escrow option handled via an audited HSM partner under a contract addendum. Escrow access requires multi-party approval (customer token + Redwood legal approval) and is only used for narrowly scoped recovery scenarios.
- Safeguards: per-request KMS audit events, ephemeral host-level protections (sealed memory where supported), prefixed KV cache isolation by customer, and an ops runbook that includes post-incident key rotation and an independent audit. We include telemetry and searchable audit records tied to the incident ID.

Proposed DPA annex language (draft):
1) Transient Processing Window: Redwood agrees that, in the event of a region failover, any decrypted customer data processed in a secondary region will not be persisted to long-term storage and the maximum resident time in cleartext in memory shall not exceed 2 minutes per processing incident, measured from host decryption to process termination. Exceptional circumstances (e.g., extended recovery) will be escalated and require written approval from both parties.
2) KMS Escrow: Redwood will not disclose customer master keys to third parties under routine ops. For customers requesting escrow, the parties may execute a separate KMS Escrow Addendum specifying the escrow holder, access controls, and approval workflow. Escrow access requires: (a) customer-signed activation token, (b) a Redwood legal approval checkbox, and (c) an observable audit trail from the HSM provider.
3) Audit Evidence: Redwood will provide redacted KMS/audit event samples and an incident playbook extract as evidence during onboarding; full raw logs remain subject to retention and privacy rules.
