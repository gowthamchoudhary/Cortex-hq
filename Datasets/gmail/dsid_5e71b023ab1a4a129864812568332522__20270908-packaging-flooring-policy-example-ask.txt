Hi Alex —

Thanks for the clear list. I pulled together a short write-up and a sample invoice sheet (attached). TL;DR answers first, then a concrete example below.

TL;DR
- Billing unit: billed per 1,000 tokens consumed (we round up to the nearest 1k for each request).
- Long contexts: tokens are measured on the total request+response per call; a 32k context consumes those tokens in the call and counts toward the same 1k granularity.
- Monthly minimums: pilot has a $5,000 monthly minimum (applies to invoice subtotal before discounts). We can apply a one-time credit if usage is below minimum in month 1.
- Rounding: per-call rounding to 1k; invoice-level smoothing applies only for true-ups on committed plans (not on pure pay-as-you-go).

Concrete example (single month):
- Calls: 1,000 short calls at 120 tokens request+response (120k total tokens), 50 long calls at 18,400 tokens (920k total tokens).
- Raw token total = 1,040,000 tokens => billed units = ceil(1,040,000 / 1,000) = 1,040 units.
- List price per unit = $0.030 / 1k (pilot price shown in proposal) => pre-discount = 1,040 * $0.03 = $31.20.
- Monthly minimum (pilot) = $5,000 => invoice shows minimum charge applied: charge = max($31.20, $5,000) = $5,000.
- If we agreed a 20% pilot discount applied to list, monthly charge = $5,000 * 0.8 = $4,000 and your team would receive a one-time credit for the difference in month 1 so cost shows $31.20 (usage) + a credit to reconcile to the minimum.

Notes:
- The per-call rounding (ceil to 1k) is applied when we bill usage line items; the monthly minimum is a higher-level guard. For pilots we often show both: the actual usage line plus a minimum guarantee line in the invoice.
- We can map to a per-seat or per-reserved-GPU model if you need deterministic cost for execs — happy to convert.
