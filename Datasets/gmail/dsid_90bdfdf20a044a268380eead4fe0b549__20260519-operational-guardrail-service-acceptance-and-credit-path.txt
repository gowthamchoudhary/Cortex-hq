Hi Cassidy — great to hear and thanks for the clear list. Below is our standard hosted SLA language we typically propose. I also attached a short matrix for monthly credit calculation (uptime-credit-matrix.xlsx) and the draft SLA template (SLA_Template_v1.pdf).

High level:
- Standard uptime commitment: 99.95% monthly availability for the production API endpoint.
- Monthly service credits: calculated as (monthly invoice amount) * (percentage uptime shortfall / 100), with a maximum credit cap of 50% of that month’s invoice. Credits kick in only after a 0.05% tolerance band (i.e., uptime < 99.95%).
- Latency objective: P95 generation response <= 150 ms for single-request workloads under baseline commit (<=1M tokens/month). For customers with >1M tokens/month or burst/batched inference profiles, we set P95 <= 250 ms; we can map profiles to latency tiers in the template.
- Carveouts: standard maintenance windows (we provide 48 hr notice for any scheduled major upgrades), planned batched-inference degradation (we exclude brief hourly batching ramps from uptime calculations when customers opt into batched mode), and force majeure. We include a one-time monthly maintenance allowance up to 60 minutes without credit impact.

Additions/notes:
- For customers requiring stricter uptime (e.g., 99.99%), we handle via a reserved-capacity contract with higher commit and a lower credit cap.
- For latency, we recommend adding a short appendix that ties P95 to a specific request size/seq-length; otherwise the metric can be noisy.

If procurement wants, we can insert this language directly into your contract draft. I’ve asked Laura to confirm whether we can reduce the tolerance band for newly awarded pilots.

Hi Cassidy / Sean — quick note from Product & Legal ops: 

We reviewed Sean's proposed language and suggest one focused exception for batched inference customers. Many batch-heavy workloads see transient availability dips when large KV-cache flushes or quantization warmups occur. Our recommendation:

- Keep the 99.95% baseline for interactive/real-time tenants.
- For tenants who opt into 'Batched Inference Mode' (documented config), define a separate operational profile: 99.5% monthly uptime and a pro-rated credit formula with a lower cap (25% of monthly invoice). This avoids over-crediting when customers intentionally trade latency for throughput.

One more operational guardrail: include a 5-day remediation window before credits are applied for novel incidents tied to model rollout regressions; if we declare a rollback within 5 business days and remediation completes, credits are not applied. This helps avoid credits for incidents where we proactively revert a model causing quality or perf regressions.
