Hi Elena — thanks for the clear list. Attached is a concise playbook that maps our retention tiering model to legal-hold behavior and export mechanics (redwood-retention-playbook-v1.2.pdf). High-level answers below; Ben and Kim have added technical and SLA specifics in follow-ups.

Summary (short):
- Retention tiering: events are labeled at ingestion (event-type + sensitivity). Customers can configure retention policies per event-class via the Console or API. Default tiers: Ephemeral (30d), Standard (90d), Long-term (365d), Indefinite (customer-locked). Tier transitions are deterministic and timestamped in the audit ledger.
- Legal hold: holds are applied at account or resource scope and create an immutable preservation marker. Once a hold exists, normal deletion/expiry processes skip held records. Holds are visible in the Console (Holds tab) and available via the Legal Hold API (GET/POST/DELETE). Holds also add a tamper-evident flag to exported bundles.
- Exportability: bulk exports are delivered as NDJSON bundles with a signed manifest (SHA-256 checksums for each file, plus a manifest signature using our keying material). Exports can include raw event payloads and reconstructed session context; customers may request either a full raw export or a filtered, redacted export.
- Snapshot SLA: standard lead time for on-demand snapshots is 72 hours for datasets under 30TB. Accelerated options are available for Dedicated customers (24h or <6h with committed burst).

Elena / team — adding the technical specifics you asked for so the RFP reviewers have concrete items they can validate. Below is an export & hold workflow and manifest example (abridged).

Export workflow (typical evidence request):
1) Customer opens an evidence request with criteria (time window, event types, user ids). Legal holds are checked first; held records are flagged and included regardless of retention tier.
2) System composes a snapshot set from the immutable audit ledger and associated payload stores. Each file is written as NDJSON (one event per line) and given a deterministic filename: auditlines-YYYYMMDDTHHMMSS-<shard>.ndjson.
3) A manifest (manifest-YYYYMMDDTHHMMSS.json) lists files, per-file SHA-256, total bundle hash, and the applied retention/hold filters. The manifest is signed with Redwood's export signing key (ECDSA-SHA256).
4) Delivery: standard delivery is an encrypted archive to a customer S3 endpoint (HTTPS PUT). We support multipart delivery for large exports and provide a resumable transfer mechanism.

Retention enforcement details:
- Timestamps: every event carries ingestion_ts and policy_ts (policy assignment time). Transitions between tiers are logged as audit operations (policy_change events) and are queryable via the Audit API.
- Immutable snapshots: when a snapshot is requested, we create an immutable bundle reference in the ledger; underlying storage is tombstoned but not purged from immutable tier until the snapshot retention expiry.
- Tamper-evidence: manifests + per-file checksums + server-side signed envelope. For customers requiring an additional chain-of-custody, we can provide a signed chain-of custody packet including operator IDs and export job IDs.

Sample manifest entry (human-readable):
- file: auditlines-20261109T150000-00.ndjson
- sha256: 3b2f...a9c4
- events: 1,234,567
- applied_filters: {"types":["auth","model_request"], "legal_hold":true}

API & hooks:
- Legal hold API: POST /api/v1/holds {scope: "account" | "project", reason, expires_at(optional)}; response includes hold_id and immutable=true once created.
- Export API: POST /api/v1/exports {range, filters, include_payloads:true/false, destination:{s3:{bucket, keyPrefix, region}}}; returns export_job_id and ETA.

SLA examples:
- Standard: 72h ETA for exports <30TB, retries included, integrity verification upon delivery.
- Committed: 24h ETA (Dedicated) or 6h expedited for prioritized customers with burst capacity.

Attachments referenced: export-schema-samples.ndjson (contains field-level examples and a redaction matrix).
