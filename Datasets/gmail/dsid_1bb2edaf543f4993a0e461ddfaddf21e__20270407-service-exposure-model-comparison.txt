Hi Lydia —

Following up from our sync yesterday, attached is a concise comparison of three connectivity patterns for Redwood Private: VPC peering, interface endpoints (AWS PrivateLink style), and site-to-site VPN (IPSec). The spreadsheet has a quick-read matrix with latency, throughput, security surface, operational complexity, and compliance notes. High level summary below: 

- VPC peering: simplest packet-level connectivity, low latency, but requires non-overlapping IPs and transitive peering is not supported (cross-account routing limitations).
- Private endpoint (service exposure via interface endpoints): minimal routing exposure — only the specific service(s) are reachable. Good for multi-account security boundaries, easier IP overlap handling, slightly higher per-GB cost and requires NLB/Nginx fronting on our side.
- Site-to-site VPN: flexible, easy to set up for proof-of-concept, supports encryption in transit, but generally higher latency/MTU issues and less predictable throughput.

Thanks Lydia — great context. A few concrete notes and the action plan we recommend: 

Technical notes (quick):
- IP overlap: PrivateLink (interface endpoints) is the least brittle — it exposes only specific DNS/IPs from service side to your VPC via AWS ENIs, so your CIDR overlap is not an issue. We can also publish a fixed hostname for the endpoint with mTLS.
- Peering: works well for latency-sensitive streaming calls (lower per-GB egress), but requires a non-overlapping CIDR and careful L3 guardrails. Also note AWS peering does not support transitive routes; if Acme expects traffic to traverse third-party VPCs, peering won't fit.
- VPN: fastest to stand up for POC. Watch MTU — common mitigations: set DF clearing, lower MTU to 1400, or use TCP MSS clamping on your side. For token-based traffic we typically see a small throughput hit but acceptable for dev workloads.
- Cost/ops: PrivateLink has per-hour NLB + per-GB data processing costs on our side; VPN has lower infra cost (at least initially) but increases ops overhead for HA and monitoring.

Operational recommendation:
1) POC (1 week): stand up an IPSec tunnel to validate basic traffic flow and measure latency/MTU. Acme to provide tunnel endpoints + PSK/BGP details; Redwood to provision a temporary customer-dedicated gateway.
2) Parallel pilot (2–3 weeks): configure PrivateLink with an NLB in Redwood account; Acme to create interface endpoint(s) in their VPC. Validate p95 latency and connection stability under synthetic load (we can provide a stress script).
3) Production decision: if PrivateLink meets latency (<=80ms p95) and cost is acceptable, recommend PrivateLink for sensitive production traffic. For high-throughput low-latency streaming where CIDRs can be allocated, consider peering as an alternative (hosted in Acme account or via transit VPC).
