Folks — I pulled together an initial working hypothesis (see inline). I attached a small CSV with the per-model per-sku throughput estimates under our current kernel set.

Assumptions from Aurora profile:
- Short-chat model: medium decoder-only, average resp 60 tokens, 120ms p50 on L4 steady state, bursts up to 4x QPS.
- Long-context: large 70B family, sessions keep KV for the entire job; average session lasts 20 minutes but requires a per-session KV footprint equivalent to ~48GB.

Proposed reserve/burst mix (non-final):
- Reserve: L4 pool sized to cover baseline short-chat at 1.5x safety factor. L4 handles most short requests at best tokens/$ for baseline.
- Burst reserve: small A100 pool (warm) to improve mid-tier quality and handle sustained medium-length contexts without immediate H100 spin-up.
- H100: kept as on-demand dedicated burst (or pre-warmed in a small hot pool) for the long-context 48GB KV sessions and highest-quality model variants.

Rationale / tradeoffs:
- Memory headroom: H100 > A100 > L4 for large KV. If Aurora needs to keep full KV per session, tiling/eviction on L4 will significantly increase latency for long-context jobs and increase engineering complexity.
- Cost vs latency: L4 reserved -> lowest $/token for chat. H100 burst -> best latency and reduced orchestration for long-context but at higher $/hr. A100 is a middle ground enabling fewer H100 spin-ups if you accept some memory-constrained tiling.
- Operational simplicity: single-sku reserve is easiest for ops and procurement, but creates cost inefficiency. Mixed pool requires routing policies and cache-aware routing (we can provide those via Redwood Console).

Numbers (very rough):
- Baseline tokens/sec (short-chat) -> L4: 12k tokens/s per 16-GPU pool at 80% util.
- Long-context concurrency target -> each H100 can support ~6 concurrent 48GB-KV sessions (depends on model and quant).
- Suggested initial commit: reserve the equivalent compute of 48 L4 GPUs + 4 A100s as warm capacity. Plan H100 as a 6-node hot pool for predictable long-context SLAs.
