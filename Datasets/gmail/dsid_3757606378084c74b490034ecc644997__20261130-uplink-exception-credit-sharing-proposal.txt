Connor / Ben — attached is a short calc (SLA_credit_calc_v1.xlsx) and my recommendation. Summary below.

Recommendation (operationally safe, clearer to customer):
- Planned maintenance with >=72 hours notice: Redwood will treat the window as an "exception" with no service credits owed, provided Redwood provided timely notice to the customer and the event is demonstrably caused by a third-party uplink provider (log evidence required).
- Unplanned or extended outages caused by the uplink provider: credit = (degraded_minutes / total_minutes_in_billing_period) * monthly_service_fee, capped at 20% for the billing period.
- Degraded_minutes definition: minutes where p95 latency is > 2x baseline p95 or errors increase > 5% compared to baseline, measured on our standard telemetry.
- For scheduled windows longer than 4 hours, require a playbook execution and a post-mortem within 5 business days; absence of post-mortem may void the exception.

Why the cap: a fixed cap (20%) protects predictable exposure; the proportional formula keeps it fair to customers. It also encourages customers to accept early notice cadence.

"Temporary Uplink Provider Exception:
For planned maintenance events conducted by an upstream network or uplink provider where Redwood receives at least seventy-two (72) hours' prior written notice, Redwood shall treat the maintenance window as an exception and shall not owe Service Credits for performance deviations reasonably attributable to such maintenance, provided Redwood (i) notifies the Customer within twenty-four (24) hours of Redwood becoming aware, and (ii) documents the upstream provider as the root cause in its post-incident report.
For unplanned or extended service degradations attributable to the uplink provider, Service Credits shall be calculated as follows: (degraded_minutes / total_minutes_in_billing_period) * monthly_service_fee, subject to a maximum credit of twenty percent (20%) of the monthly invoice for the affected billing period. "
