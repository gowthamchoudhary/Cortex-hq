Following our run-through last week, attaching an initial proposal for throughput governance and sizing for the private VPC deployment. The goal here is to align on steady-state throughput targets, how we expose burst capacity, and what reserved vs. on-demand carveouts look like for billing and SLAs.

High-level asks:
- Confirm a steady-state target: your team indicated ~80 qps for text-generation workloads (avg payload). Is that correct as a 24x7 baseline?

What we propose (summary):
1) Sizing model (attached capacity_model_v1.xlsx): assume baseline of N GPU nodes (A100-class or equivalent) to meet steady-state throughput with 25% buffer for headroom.
2) Burst governance: a two-tier approach — (a) guaranteed reserved lanes for baseline (SLA-backed), (b) transient burst pool that is priced at a premium and throttled by a governance controller to avoid noisy-neighbor risk.
3) Network/topology constraints (see network_topology.png): colocate model-serving nodes with an egress aggregator to avoid per-node peering blowup; prefer a single tun/tap to your VPC to reduce open connections.
4) Scaling policy: goal-based autoscaling by sustained token-rate with an admission control window (90s) to prevent oscillation. Consider pre-warm snapshots for cached KV to minimize cold-start latency.

Operational notes:

- Compliance: please attach SOC2 / security questionnaire earlier in the thread (we noted some items missing).
