Thanks — I dug into the gateway logs for vpc-edge-08 from 07:50–09:00 PDT. Findings:

- At 08:13:24, the edge saw a route-change event where the best-path for 10.244.128.0/22 flipped to a secondary nexthop (local hardware failover).
- Immediately after, our NAT engine reported a translation-group checksum mismatch and demoted a set of entries to a fallback translation table (software path).
- While entries were on the fallback table, stateful sessions experienced asymmetric forwarding — responses were forwarded out a different egress with different SNAT mapping, which triggered TCP resets at the origin.

Hypothesis: an on-prem route-map or BGP policy is intermittently withdrawing the primary route, causing our gateway to flip nexthops and migrate NAT entries. That migration path doesn't preserve ephemeral port mapping consistently and exposes sessions to ASYM/translation mismatch.

Action items I propose now:
1) Acme to confirm whether there were any automated route-map changes, ECMP reweighting, or maintenance automation at ~08:13.
2) We'll add targeted packet captures on vpc-edge-08 for translations and enable a tracing flag to log translation table swaps.
3) Short-term mitigation: pin a static L3 route for the affected subnets on our edge and your border to stop nexthop oscillation while we investigate.

I can implement the static route; need confirmation from Acme that they can add the mirror on their side to avoid asymmetry.
