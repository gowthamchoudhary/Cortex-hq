Peter —

Appreciate the clear list. I compiled the answers below and attached our proposed acceptance matrix (Redwood_proposed_acceptance_matrix.xlsx). High-level summary first, then the detailed steps you can drop into the procurement/SOW.

Executive summary (TL;DR):
- We recommend a four-phase approach: 1) Discovery & baseline, 2) Pilot (shadow + limited production), 3) Progressive ramp (traffic shaping), 4) Full cutover & stabilization.
- Typical time box: 2–4 weeks discovery, 3–6 weeks pilot, 2–4 weeks progressive ramp, 1–2 weeks stabilization — adjustable by mutual readiness gates.
- Validation: automated eval harness (prompt sets + metrics), side-by-side A/B parity checks, sample human review for edge cases.
- Rollback / rollback-to-last-known-good snapshot supported at every stage; expected RTO for switching traffic is <5 minutes for hosted/dedicated routes, with config rollback orchestration in ~30–60 minutes depending on infra changes.

Detailed plan and decision gates:
Phase 0 — Preparation (pre-engagement)
• Deliverables: network allowlists, service account keys, compliance questionnaire answers (we'll provide templates).
• Duration: 3–7 days.

Phase 1 — Discovery & baseline
• Activities: workload profiling, seq-len distribution, baseline cost/latency measurement using a representative sample (we'll run a benchmark agent against a non-prod slice).
• Deliverables: baseline report, target SLOs, recommended batching/quantization profile.
• Decision gate: sign-off if latency and quality baselines meet agreed SLO band.
• Duration: 2–4 weeks.

Phase 2 — Pilot (shadow + micro-production)
• Activities: deploy private/dedicated endpoint per agreed topology; run traffic shadowing for N days, then route 1–5% production traffic to Redwood with feature flags.
• Quality validation: nightly automated evals (ROUGE/BLEU where applicable, classification accuracy, hallucination rate), weekly human review of 200 sample sessions.
• Safety: progressive rate limits, prefix/KV cache isolation, and token logging with masked PII per your retention policy.
• Decision gate: move to ramp if parity metrics within thresholds AND runbook-tested rollback completes successfully in staging.
• Duration: 3–6 weeks.

Phase 3 — Progressive ramp (traffic shaping)
• Activities: incremental traffic increases (5% -> 25% -> 50% -> 100%), with health checks (latency, errors, token-cost delta) at each step. We recommend minimum 48–72 hour observation windows per increment.
• Rollout controls: model variant pinning, autoscale thresholds, and automated fallback to a lower-cost or previous model if error or quality regressions exceed thresholds.
• Duration: 2–4 weeks.

Phase 4 — Full cutover & stabilization
• Activities: final config flips, extended monitoring for 14 days, runbook handoff to your SREs.
• Deliverables: production runbook, incident threshold sheet, audit logs export for your retention window.

Rollback and recovery specifics:
• Quick rollback: re-route traffic to prior endpoint/variant in <5 minutes for hosted/dedicated routes.
• Full rollback (schema/back-end changes): 30–60 minutes to execute orchestration plus verification.
• Data recovery: logs and audit artifacts are retained per configured policy; restoration depends on your retention window — we'll include a step-by-step in the SOW appendix.

Validation & acceptance matrix (attached):
• Functional parity thresholds (token-level KL divergence, top-k overlap), latency/D99 targets, error-rate caps, and sample-size criteria for human review sign-off.
• We included explicit test cases for auth flow, audit-trace validation with Splunk events, and telemetry hooks.

Integration checklist (highlighted items):
• Auth: OAuth2 or mTLS for private control plane; we support SAML/OIDC for console access and service accounts for programmatic calls.
• Logging: structured JSON token-level logs; direct Splunk HEC integration or forwarding via a collector.
• Networking: VPC peering options or IP allowlist + TLS; private deployments can run behind your egress controls.
• Compliance: SOC2 artifact bundles, audit log SLA, and optional HSM/KMS integration.
