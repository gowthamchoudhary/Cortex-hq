Hi Emily —

Thanks for the clear list. I've pulled together an executive one-pager and an appendix that map directly to your bullets. High level answers below; appendix attached (RI_SLO_RFI_Appendix_A.pdf) has example queries and a sample payload export.

1) Definition of availability
- Hosted API: availability = count(successful 200-range responses to authorized requests) / count(total authorized requests) over the measurement window. Requests impacted by customer-side rate limits or auth failures are excluded.
- Dedicated: same numerator/denominator but scoped to the tenant's dedicated pool endpoints; measurements are per-reservation.
- Private (VPC/on-prem): availability is reported from the customer's control plane heartbeat + endpoint health pings; we provide a measurement agent and guidance, but the final availability is jointly agreed (we sign off on exporter metrics).

2) Latency measurement
- Non-stream (complete-response): measured from request ingestion to final token emission for the top-level request. Reported metrics: P50/P90/P99 on tail (ms) with request-size buckets (<=512 tokens, 513-2k, >2k).
- Streaming: we measure first-byte latency (time to first token) and tail-completion latency (time between first token and final token). Streaming credit triggers are tied to first-byte latency unless otherwise requested.

3) Service credit detection + appeals
- Eligible incident = validated reduction in availability below guaranteed SLO for the rolling monthly window and sustained beyond our maintenance windows.
- Detection: automated alert + manual verification by SRE.
- Cutover windows: we use rolling 5-minute windows for incident detection and 1-minute sampling for rate-limited checks.
- Appeals: customer submits a support ticket within 30 days of the incident close; we commit to a 10-business-day investigation window and provide raw telemetry extracts for reconciliation.

4) Sampling / telemetry
- Default telemetry: continuous request logs (scrubbed of PII) shipped to our observability index with a 1:1 event retention for 30 days; after 30 days we retain aggregated histograms.
- Sampling: for high-volume accounts we apply statistical sampling for raw payload retention (configurable). Appendix contains the recommended sampling configuration and an example extraction query.

Adding the SRE perspective — a few practical points & a sample query you can share with Acme: 

- Partial degradations: we define per-feature impact flags (e.g., tokenizer_error, kvcache_miss_rate, compaction_backpressure). Availability calculations are additive; an incident that affects only autocomplete endpoints in a single region will be scoped to route-tagged metrics.

- Sampling and confidence: we recommend a 1% systematic sample for raw payloads once sustained request volume > 500k/day; this yields a 95% CI on P99 of +/- ~6% for typical request variance. For accounts below that volume we retain 100% for 30d.

- Example telemetry extraction (Postgres-ish pseudo):

  SELECT
    date_trunc('minute', received_at) as minute,
    count(*) as total_requests,
    sum(case when status >= 200 and status < 300 then 1 else 0 end) as success,
    percentile_cont(0.90) within group (order by latency_ms) as p90_ms
  FROM request_events
  WHERE tenant_id = 'acme-tenant-123'
    AND received_at >= '2027-03-01'
    AND route_tag = 'text-generation'
  GROUP BY 1
  ORDER BY 1 DESC;

Emily —

Below are the concrete items you asked for. I also attached a suggested service-credit matrix (service-credit-matrix.xlsx) and a short playbook we use to reconcile incidents.

A) Service credit formula (executive summary)
- Monthly availability A (percent). If SLO = 99.9% and A < 99.9%, credit = min( MaxCredit, floor( (SLA - A) / SLA * MonthlyFee * Multiplier) ).
- Our standard MaxCredit = 50% of the monthly recurring fee for the affected service tier. Multiplier is 1.0 for platform incidents; 0.5 for incidents attributable to third-party infra (we note provenance).

Worked example:
- SLA = 99.9%
- Observed availability = 97.2%
- MonthlyFee = $90,000 (example)
- Delta = 99.9 - 97.2 = 2.7 percentage points
- Credit = floor( (2.7 / 99.9) * 90,000 ) = floor(0.027027 * 90,000) = $2,432 -> apply Multiplier (1.0) -> $2,432. Apply MaxCredit cap (50% * 90,000 = 45,000) so credit remains $2,432.

B) Attribution for Dedicated pools
- We separate telemetry at the tenant level: pool-level metrics (isolated GPU nodes, scheduling logs) are used to detect noisy neighbors. If the incident scope is fully contained within the reserved pool (route_tag & node_id matching), credits apply to the Dedicated reservation. If platform control plane or region-level infra is implicated, it is a platform incident. We provide full diagnostic traces (KV cache miss rates, node-level CPU/GPU contention metrics) during the investigation window.

C) Private installs (proof-of-source)
- For Private we require: (1) signed extract bundle (GPG or HSM-based signing) of the agreed export schema, (2) hash manifest of exported files, and (3) a short attestation signed by an authorized customer operator. We accept SHA-256 manifests and will verify integrity prior to credit processing. We include a sample verification script in the appendix.

D) Timeline for payout
- Acknowledgement of appeal: within 2 business days.
- Investigation complete & preliminary findings: within 10 business days.
- Final determination and credit issuance or invoice offset: within 30 calendar days from final determination. Credits are issued as an invoice-credit or applied to next billing cycle per customer preference.
