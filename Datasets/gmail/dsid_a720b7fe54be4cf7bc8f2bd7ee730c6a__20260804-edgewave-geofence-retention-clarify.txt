Hi Sarah — thanks for the questionnaire and the clear asks. Quick summary up front, followed by the specifics we usually provide to security teams. I attached a one-page summary (redwood-data-retention-summary.pdf) for your reviewers.

High-level answers (short):
- Data residency: For hosted API customers we support regional tenancy. Production request payloads and request logs are pinned to the selected region (e.g., us-west1 or eu-west1) at rest.
- Deletion timeline: Active data removed within 48 hours of a verified erasure request. Backups/snapshots retained for up to 90 days in standard configuration; expedited deletion options available via contractual addendum.
- Encryption & controls: All data at rest encrypted with customer-key capability (KMS). Access is logged and restricted to the control plane and ops procedures described below.

Detailed answers: 
1) Are customer request logs and request payloads region-bound?
- Yes. For hosted API customers who choose a region, primary storage for request payloads and logs is kept in that region. Control-plane metadata (account-level routing and billing entries) may reside in the control plane in the US for operations but does not contain full payload content.

2) Deletion — what is removed and when?
- Primary storage: Upon a verified deletion request we mark the customer’s tenant data for immediate deletion and remove access within 24–48 hours.
- Cache & KV stores: KV caches and prefix caches are evicted as part of the request purge (typically immediate for active caches; some in-flight copies may persist for seconds-minutes).
- Backups/snapshots: Standard retention window for snapshots is 90 days. Snapshots are immutable by design for restore safety; they are indexed and can be scheduled for secure deletion earlier if the customer signs an addendum that allows expedited snapshot scrubbing.

3) Proof of deletion & attestation
- We provide an attestation email with a deletion token and timestamp once the active data deletion completes. For backup/snapshot removal we can provide timeline estimates and, with the addendum, a confirmation once scrub completes.

4) Regional compliance options
- EU geofencing: We can provision EU-only tenancy (data at rest and backups in eu-west1) for enterprise customers subject to capacity planning and a short onboarding window.

Hi Sarah — Irene from Security here. Adding a few operational clarifications so procurement has clear numbers to work with.

Quoted answers (operational):
- EU backups/snapshots: Yes — when an enterprise customer is provisioned in EU tenancy we ensure primary snapshots and backup artifacts are stored in eu-west1 by default. Our cross-region disaster recovery plan only kicks in under explicit DR activation; otherwise snapshots remain in-region.

- Expedited snapshot scrubbing (with addendum): We consider a standard SLA of 10 business days to locate and securely scrub snapshot copies for an EU-tenanted customer after signed request + proof of authorization. The process timeline includes discovery, validation, secure wipe, and verification.

- Logs & SIEM exports: Security audit logs that we forward to a customer-managed SIEM are considered out-of-band at the customer’s choice; Redwood does not retain copies of SIEM-forwarded logs beyond the configured retention. For logs kept by Redwood for forensic purposes (e.g., infra-level audit logs), we retain them for 30 days by default for incident response then purge unless a different retention is negotiated. Deletion requests for customer payloads do not retroactively remove entries from customer-managed SIEMs.

If procurement wants the 10-business-day expedited scrub SLA formalized we can include that in a short addendum and commit to an evidence packet once scrub completes. I’ve attached a short internal playbook excerpt that describes the scrub verification steps.
