Hi Alex — quick answers and added artifacts below.

On Mon, Oct 19, 2026 at 9:18 AM Alex Perez <alex.perez@tangenthealth.com> wrote:
> The p99 < 550ms requirement: is that achievable on rlm-7b-q4f at our concurrency? Do you have p99 numbers measured on 512-token prompts?

Short answer: Yes, with the right batching and KV-cache policy. From our Redwood-verified profile (attached PDF):
- rlm-7b-q4f (NVIDIA A10x2 pool, single-instance serving with continuous batching tuned): median ~210ms, p95 ~360ms, p99 ~520ms on our 512-token synthetic prompt set.
- At sustained concurrency ~18 TPS the model still hits ~p99 520–580ms depending on prompt variability; we recommend a max burst headroom and a 2-instance canary to keep p99 comfortable.

> For the sample notes: we can provide a de-id bucket (S3 link) by EOD today. Any formatting constraints we should follow?

Please: newline-delimited JSON with fields: note_id, timestamp_iso, text. If there are structured fields (diagnoses), include a diagnoses array. If not possible, send CSV and we'll transform.

> For the fallback path: how seamless is automatic routing between the quantized 7b and the 13b during a spike?

We route by policy. Simple options: 
- latency-first: route to rlm-7b-q4f, fallback to rlm-13b for higher-quality requests only on a per-request signal.
- cost-first: default to rlm-7b, route >X tokens or flagged prompts to 13b.
Either approach is transparent in telemetry and billing; we can add a canary that mirrors 5% of traffic to 13b for drift detection.
