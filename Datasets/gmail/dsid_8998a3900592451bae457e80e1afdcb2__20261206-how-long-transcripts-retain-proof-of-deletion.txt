Hi Lily — thanks for the clear list and the spreadsheet, very helpful. Short answers up front, then details below.

TL;DR:
- Default capture: we store request metadata (timestamps, routing), the raw prompt text (unless customer enables prompt-redaction at account level), and the final model output; intermediate token streams (streamed fragments) are not persisted by default except in rare debug-mode sessions (flagged and access-controlled).
- Residency: for customer region pinned to us-east we keep stored artifacts in that region; support access is audited and we do not copy telemetry outside the region for analytics unless explicit opt-in is granted.
- Deletion proof: we provide a deletion report (record ids removed, timestamps, and a signed audit assertion). For retained backups/tombstones we provide a retention-hold manifest and timeline. Typical purge latency: 24–72 hours for hosted accounts, with an evidence package delivered within 48 hours of completion.

Details:
1) What counts as a captured item?
- Prompts (customer-supplied inputs): captured by default. These are considered customer data. Customers may enable prompt-redaction at account-level to suppress storing raw input; this prevents prompt text from being written to long-term stores but still allows ephemeral processing for inference.
- Intermediate/streamed fragments: not written to persistent stores by default. If a customer uses streaming and enables debugging or attaches an "enable-debug-storage" flag, transient fragments can be stored in a separate debug bucket (explicitly scoped and billed).
- Final outputs/completions: captured by default for telemetry and quality monitoring; can be filtered or auto-expunged by account-level retention policies.

2) Residency and support access
- Region pinning ensures stored artifacts (prompts, outputs, logs) are kept in the specified cloud region (US East) for the lifetime they exist. Support engineers can request access via an audited workflow; such access is logged and available in audit exports. No analytics pipeline copies data outside the region unless you opt into cross-region analytics (we'll mark that in the SOW if requested).

3) Deletion and proof of deletion
- Customer-initiated erase: we run a targeted deletion job referencing persisted ids; job status and a signed deletion assertion are generated. We also include a list of object ids and the storage classes they were removed from.
- Backups and tombstones: backups are rotated per our backup policy; when immediate evidence is required (e.g., regulatory erasure), we provide a retention-hold manifest and removal schedule; in many cases we can accelerate a backup overwrite and provide a notarized assertion.
- Evidence package: includes a CSV of removed record ids, timestamps, job-run ids, screenshots of deletion job logs (if requested), and a signed assertion from Redwood InfoSec.

Hi all — quick legal/ops note:
- Contract language: standard SOW will state we provide "evidence of deletion" including object ids, job run ids, and signed assertion; we do not typically provide third-party notarization in standard enterprise SOWs but can include that as an add-on.
- SLA commitment: we can commit to producing an evidence package within 72 hours under standard hosting terms. We can escalate to 48 hours as a contractual commitment for customers on our Enterprise tier or with a narrow SOW addendum (this is typically included for procurements with high compliance needs).
- Backups: depending on the backup retention class, permanent removal from long-term backups can extend beyond the primary purge window; in those cases we provide a retention-hold manifest and a schedule for overwriting backup artifacts.
