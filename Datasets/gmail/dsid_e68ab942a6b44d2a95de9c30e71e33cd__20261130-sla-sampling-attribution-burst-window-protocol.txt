Hi Olivia — thanks for the clear list. Putting a concise answer together and looping in Dev and Markus for the telemetry and calculation examples. Attached: a summary of our SLA measurement approach and a small sample telemetry extract.

High level answers up front:
- Availability is measured at the request level (successful HTTP 2xx within configured timeouts) and aggregated over 5m windows for crediting.
- Retries are logged with a unique request_id; server-side retries (automatic fallbacks inside the Redwood runtime) are considered part of the single logical request and are de-duplicated for availability counting. Client-side retries that re-submit a different request_id are treated as independent attempts.
- Short bursts: we use a burst attenuation policy that ignores transient traffic anomalies under certain thresholds (default: any outage shorter than 30s and affecting <1% of requests in the minute is not immediately credited; the full 5m aggregation still applies). We can adjust thresholds in the contract.
- Batching: server-side batching increases per-request processing delay but we attribute latency to the model that served the request, including batch queuing time. We publish both end-to-end (ingress to response) and execution (runtime-only) percentiles so you can choose which aligns with your SLOs.

Hi Olivia — quick technical addendum to Kimberly's summary with precise rules we apply (note: shortened for email, full spec attached).

1) Timestamps and aggregation
- Ingress timestamp is assigned at the edge (first Redwood ingress) and stored with the request_id. Our canonical measurement uses ingress -> final-response timestamp for end-to-end latency.
- Aggregation window: 5-minute non-overlapping windows aligned to UTC. We also compute rolling 1m and 15m windows for observability.

2) Availability definition
- Success: final status code in 2xx (or 3xx for redirect workflows) and response seen before the configured request timeout.
- Failure: 5xx, network connection failure, or timeout. If a request is retried server-side (same request_id) and any attempt succeeds, the logical request is counted as success.

3) Retries and de-duplication
- For client retries that create new request_ids, we count each as a distinct attempt; however, for crediting we attempt to map related attempts by client-provided idempotency_key when present and coalesce.

4) Bursts and attenuation
- Short bursts are common. Crediting pipeline: (a) detect window with anomalous error rate relative to baseline, (b) require persistence over a minimum duration (default 2 minutes of sustained elevated errors) before automatic credit determination, (c) perform root-cause correlation (e.g., model overload vs network path) before final crediting.
- We provide a pre-claim report within 24 hours when a potential breach is detected; final credit decisions follow our dispute window (30 days) with evidence bundle.

5) Batching, KV-cache, and attribution
- Batching is considered internal scheduling. Latency reported in "execution" percentiles excludes client queuing time prior to ingress but includes batching queue time inside the runtime. KV-cache hits are flagged: we publish separate percentiles for cached vs uncached responses to aid attribution.

6) Sampling and telemetry export
- We store full logs for 30 days by default. For long-term retention we provide sampled exports (default: 1% random sampling plus targeted retention of incidents). Sampled telemetry in the attachment shows the fields we can export: timestamp, request_id, request_path, status_code, latency_ms, model_variant, region, cached (true/false), retry_count.

If you want, we can produce a one-week unredacted export under an NDA.

Hi Olivia — attached is a brief worked example and our standard crediting matrix. Summary below.

Worked example (cross-region failure, simplified):
- Scenario: Region-A experiences elevated errors from 12:04:00 UTC to 12:10:00 UTC (6 minutes). Traffic had a planned DR failover such that 70% of traffic remained routed to Region-A and 30% shifted to Region-B.
- Measurement: We compute per-region 5m windows (12:05-12:10 UTC) and global 5m window by aggregating all ingress records. Region-A shows 87% availability in the 12:05-12:10 window; Region-B shows 99.8% availability; global availability for that window is 95.6%.
- Crediting: Our standard contract defines availability SLOs per-region and a global availability SLO. If the per-region SLO was breached (Region-A), Region-A's customers can claim a regional credit under the per-region clause. Global SLOs are evaluated separately — if global SLO remains met, no global credit is automatically applied.
- Attribution nuance: If your traffic routing was under Redwood's managed routing policy and we failed to divert traffic appropriately, we accept responsibility for global impact. If you managed failover (e.g., custom DNS/edge policies at Atlas), crediting uses attribution logic that factors in your routing decisions. We document these attribution rules in section 3 of the attached matrix.

Key excerpts from our crediting matrix (attached):
- Measurement window: 5m UTC alignment for crediting
- Burst attenuation: outages <30s and impacting <1% requests are excluded from automatic credit decisions; sustained >2m elevated error rate triggers pre-claim review
- Credit tiers: 99.9-99.95% => 5% monthly service credit, 99.5-99.9% => 15%, <99.5% => 30% (per-region) — negotiable for large commitments
