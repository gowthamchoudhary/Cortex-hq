Sarah / Dev — thanks for the context. Below are proposed controlled edits we think will meet Novatek's risk profile while remaining operationally feasible for Redwood. I'll draft the redline and send as a tracked-change snippet by EOD. High-level bullets: 

1) Training definition and opt-in:
- Proposed definition: "Training" means the use of Customer Data or Customer Output as part of any dataset used to train, fine-tune, or otherwise adapt a model for general availability or for use by customers other than the Customer.
- Opt-in: A written, executed Training Addendum (attached as Exhibit X) is required for any Training that includes Customer Data/Output. Absent execution of Exhibit X, Redwood covenants not to use Customer Data/Output for Training.

2) Telemetry allowed for ops:
- Allowed telemetry (collectively, "Operational Telemetry"): anonymized latency and throughput metrics; aggregate error/exception counts; token consumption summaries by API route (not raw prompts or outputs); instance health metrics.
- Prohibited: raw prompts, raw responses, or any identifiable customer data.

3) Feedback license (narrow, time-limited):
- Redwood may use Customer Feedback for the sole purpose of debugging and internal feature improvements for the Customer's service instance for a period of 24 months from receipt. Redwood will not use Customer Feedback to train models for use outside the Customer's instance or for any commercial productization unless Customer signs Exhibit Y (a commercialization license).

4) Audit & proof language:
- If Novatek requests, Redwood will provide a redacted summary of telemetry categories used and a compliance attestation (no access to raw prompts/outputs). We can accommodate a 1x/year audit in a secure manner.

If that general approach works, I'll prepare tracked redlines to your document and a short Exhibit X/Y template for the opt-in and commercialization workflows. Sarah — does Novatek need any additional gating (legal counterparty, regional restrictions) in the addendum?
