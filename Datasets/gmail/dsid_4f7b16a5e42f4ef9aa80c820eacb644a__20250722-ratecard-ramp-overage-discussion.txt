Liz — thanks for the clear asks. Putting a concise summary below and attaching our working MAP + the Q3 rate card for reference.

Summary (short):
- Overage vs burst: We have two mechanisms — (A) committed capacity (monthly committed token spend) billed at committed rate; (B) burst/ondemand billed at on-demand rate. During the 90-day ramp we offer a blended "ramp buffer" where the first X% of overage is billed at the committed rate, next tier at on-demand minus 10%, and true overage beyond agreed burst ceilings billed at standard on-demand.
- Hard cap: Yes — we can gate billing with a monthly usage cap during the evaluation. If the cap is hit, requests are throttled/failover to a lower-cost fallback model (we can discuss specific model tiers). We can also auto-notify and route traffic to Dedicated reserved nodes if you want automatic capacity scaling (this would be additional committed spend).
- Billing treatment: Usage in month is billed as normal. For MAP milestone discounts (e.g., hitting 30/60/90-day usage milestones) we apply retroactive credits on the following invoice (not pro-rated mid-month).

Liz — quick technical note to supplement Marissa:

- The burst buckets are per-tenant and operate at the routing layer. We can configure a short-lived burst pool (90s window) and a sustained overage pool (daily aggregates). Billing tiers are based on aggregated token-slices, not per-request.
- On fallback behavior: if you opt to throttle at the hard cap we will return 429s for requests that exceed quota for the primary model. We recommend enabling our fallback to the Redwood-compact or Redwood-eco model for graceful degradation. That fallback will be billed at a lower tier; it will still count as usage but at the fallback rate.
