Ellie — thanks for the clear summary and the redlines. Looping in Marissa (sales lead) and Karthik (legal ops) so we can coordinate a single response. Quick answers up front:

- Output ownership: We generally grant customers broad rights to outputs for internal and product uses, but we reserve rights to use non-identifiable, aggregated feedback for platform improvements. Full assignment of IP in generated outputs is unusual and has downstream implications for model improvements and safety.
- Training & feedback: We do not train public base models on customer plaintext without a customer's explicit opt-in. For Hosted accounts we use aggregated telemetry and prompt signals to tune serving quality unless a customer opts out; Dedicated/private deployments can be configured to prevent any feedback capture.
- Opt-out: We can offer an explicit feedback opt-out clause; implementation differs based on deployment mode (hosted vs dedicated vs private).

We'll draft proposed language and a narrow set of carve-outs that protect Ridgewell's productization needs while preserving Redwood's ability to operate and improve the platform. Expect a marked-up order form and a one-page summary by EOD tomorrow.

Team — quick internal coordination note before we send to Ridgewell legal. Karthik, see my suggested tradeoffs below (I condensed legal feedback into customer-friendly bullets).

1) Output ownership proposal (acceptable compromise):
   - Redwood will grant Ridgewell a perpetual, worldwide, transferable license to outputs generated by Ridgewell-provided content and prompts for use in Ridgewell products, including derivative productization.
   - Redwood will retain a limited, non-exclusive right to use de-identified, aggregated output statistics for monitoring/performance improvements, not to reproduce Ridgewell product content.

2) Training and data use:
   - Hosted: Redwood will not train backbone models on Ridgewell customer data without explicit opt-in. Redwood may use aggregated telemetry to improve serving (latency, prompt formatting) unless Ridgewell opts out.
   - Dedicated/Private: We will support a no-feedback mode configurable at deployment. This should be contractual for Dedicated seats with a small implementation lead time.

3) Feedback license & opt-out mechanism:
   - Add explicit opt-out clause and an operational 14-day turnaround for applying the opt-out to customer data streams.
   - For hosted, this is subject to a pseudo-anonymization window; we propose a 30-day rolling buffer after the opt-out where telemetry is retained for safety but not used for model updates (can be purged earlier on request).

   - Offer a 10% credit on first 3 months for enabling no-feedback mode on hosted (to offset higher unit cost).

Ellie / Ridgewell team — attaching a concise redline and a 1-page summary of the proposed contract language. Key points:

- Outputs: Clause A.4 grants Ridgewell a perpetual, worldwide, transferable license to outputs generated from content or prompts provided by Ridgewell. Clause explicitly permits derivative works and commercial redistribution by Ridgewell.
