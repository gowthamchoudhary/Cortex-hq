Logan — draft below, keeping it narrow to limit legal exposure. I suggest two parallel options in the MSA addendum:

Option A (Preferred - Remote attestation + encrypted runbook escrow)
- Redwood will provide quarterly remote attestation reports summarizing control evidence relevant to the deployment (SOC2 Type II report where applicable; control mappings for key custody).
- Redwood will deposit an encrypted copy of operational runbooks and recovery playbooks with a mutually-agreed third-party custodian (custodian name to be agreed in Order Form). The deposit contains only runbooks, operational runbooks, and build artifacts necessary for recovery — explicitly excludes source code and proprietary model weights.
- Release trigger: the custodian will release the escrowed artifacts to the customer only upon a defined failure-to-service event (insolvency, sustained breach of SLA for X days, or contract termination without remediation) or following a binding legal order.

Option B (Fallback - Live attestations + ephemeral evidence snapshots)
- Redwood will support a remote, recorded security walkthrough by an independent auditor on an annual basis, plus provide ephemeral evidence snapshots (redacted logs, configuration manifests) sufficient to validate custody claims. No long-term deposit is stored.

Suggested MSA language (order-of-magnitude):
- "Custodial Escrow for Operational Continuity": Redwood shall deposit, with a mutually agreed neutral custodian, encrypted recovery artifacts limited to runbooks, procedural documents and recovery scripts. Redwood retains sole encryption key; release shall occur only per predefined triggers.
- "Audit Rights": For customer-managed private deployments, Redwood agrees to reasonable remote audit rights, including provision of attestation reports, redactable configuration manifests, and virtual walkthroughs arranged with at least 30 days' notice. On-site access limited to joint sessions at Redwood discretion and only for incidents requiring deep forensics.
