Team,

Short answer: we can issue an offer with equity "subject to board approval," but we should avoid promising a retroactive grant effective date unless the board explicitly approves that date. A few points:

1) 409A safe-harbor: our last appraisal on 2025-11-05 provides safe harbor for 12 months unless there have been material events (new financing, significant M&A activity, or material changes to cap table). As far as I know, we haven't had a financing event since then, so the November 409A is likely still defensible. I'll confirm with outside counsel (Clara) and Carta.

2) Backdating risk: the grant's effective date should be the date the board approves the grant or the date specified in the board resolution. If we state "effective on start date pending board approval," that creates ambiguity — we should instead state "grant to be approved by the board and, if approved, the grant’s effective date will be the date specified in the board resolution (not to exceed 30 days from start)."

3) Practical path:
   - Send offer now with equity language: grant subject to board approval within 30 days; exercise price to be set at the FMV provided by the most recent 409A (or updated opinion if one is obtained before approval).
   - Offer a sign-on cash instead of guaranteeing immediate grant.
   - If the team wants to accelerate, Legal can prepare a special board consent (written resolution) to be circulated and signed instead of waiting for the full board meeting; that reduces risk of a week+ delay.
