Thanks for the ping — I completed the technical sections and attached a draft Redwood_TIA_Response_Draft.xlsx. Summary of key responses below (high level; see attached rows for citations and evidence links):

1) Processing & regions:
- Hosted inference: request routing and model execution occur in customer-selected regions (we support GCP/us-west, GCP/eu-west, AWS/eu-central, and Azure regions; default for your EU tenant is eu-west).
- Short-lived inference artifacts (KV cache, ephemeral metadata) are kept in-memory where possible; when persisted for caching/optimization, they are stored encrypted at rest in the same region as the request.

2) Personal data and training:
- We do not use customer production request content for downstream model training by default. Any program that would use customer data for training requires explicit opt-in and legal controls.

3) Transfers & legal mechanism:
- Primary transfer mechanism: EU Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) + Redwood SCC addendum. We can sign MedLytics' SCC addendum or propose our redwood_scc_addendum.docx.

4) Retention & deletion controls:
- Default: 30 days retention for inference logs (configurable per-customer).
- KV cache retention: configurable; recommended default 7 days for PoC.
- Customers can request immediate deletion for specific datasets via API or support ticket; deletions are audited.

5) DPIA inputs & risk controls:
- Data minimization: recommend pseudonymization before send when feasible.
- Encryption TLS 1.2+ in transit; AES-256 at rest for persisted artifacts.
- SOC 2 Type II: available (document attached: SOC2_Statement_2025.pdf).

Open questions for MedLytics to confirm:
- Expected volume of EU-subject records and whether any identifiers must be preserved in request payloads.
- Are you planning to enable any long-term analytics on raw inference outputs?

I attached the draft response and the SOC 2 statement. If you want, I can convert the spreadsheet answers directly into MedLytics' PDF template before sending.
