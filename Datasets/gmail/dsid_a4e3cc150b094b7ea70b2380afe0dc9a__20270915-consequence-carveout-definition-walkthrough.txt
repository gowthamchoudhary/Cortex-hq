Linda —

Appreciate the clear summary and the redlines. Quick reactions from our side below to help prep for procurement:

1) Mutual vs one-way carve-out: Redwood prefers a mutual exclusion of consequential/special/incidental/punitive damages, with narrowly drafted exceptions that do NOT erode the exclusion (e.g., IP indemnity and certain statutory liabilities remain outside the exclusion). One-way carve-outs create an asymmetry in risk that our finance/legal teams can't accept at scale.

2) Definition fight: The term "Consequential Damages" as drafted by HyperGrid is fairly broad (includes loss of revenue/anticipated profits, third-party claims, downstream damages). We're open to tightening the definition to specifically list categories that are excluded (lost profits, loss of business, loss of customers, loss of data access) and include a bilateral materiality threshold for claims under the cap.

3) Caps: Redwood asks that the MSA cap remain Order Form-linked (e.g., 12 months of recurring fees for subscription services) and a separate carve-out cap for fees/indemnities tied to IP. We can consider a higher cap for specific professional services if HyperGrid commits to a Statement of Work that includes acceptance criteria.

4) Regulatory/Privacy: For consumer privacy/regulatory fines, Redwood cannot reasonably cap those in a way that would contravene applicable law. We propose language stating regulatory fines are not recoverable under the MSA to the extent such limitation is unenforceable, and for data breach costs we should keep a carve-out for third-party claims and mandatory remediation with insurance/indemnity backstop.

Thanks again for the call this morning — notes and proposed language below so we have something concrete for procurement. I tried to keep the structure narrow and operational. Please review and tell me if any bullet is a deal-breaker: 

1) Mutual exclusion (preferred): 
We propose: "Neither party will be liable to the other for consequential, indirect, incidental, special or punitive damages, including but not limited to loss of profits, loss of business, loss of data, loss of customers, or loss of use, except to the extent arising from (a) a party's fraud, willful misconduct, or gross negligence; (b) a party's breach of its confidentiality obligations; (c) indemnified IP infringement claims; or (d) amounts that cannot be limited or excluded by applicable law."

2) Caps: 
MSA cap: maximum liability for direct damages shall be the greater of (i) the amount paid or payable by HyperGrid to Redwood under the applicable Order Form in the 12 months preceding the claim, or (ii) $250,000. For professional services with a signed SOW, parties may negotiate a separate higher cap in the applicable Order Form.

3) Regulatory and privacy: 
We keep an explicit carve-out that regulatory fines and statutory penalties may not be limited to the extent unenforceable, and for data breach expenses Redwood's liability for third-party claims shall be governed by IP indemnity/insurance / breach response commitments.

4) Definition clarity: 
We recommend replacing a freeform "Consequential Damages" definition with a specific list (lost profits, loss of business, loss of use, lost data access, third-party claims for downstream disruption) and an express statement that the list is illustrative but the exclusion is a mutual allocation of risk.
