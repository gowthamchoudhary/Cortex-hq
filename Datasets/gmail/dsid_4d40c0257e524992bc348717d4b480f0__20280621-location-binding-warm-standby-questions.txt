Thanks for the detail, very helpful. Short answers up-front, followed by a recommended pattern and next steps.

Quick answers: 
- Warm-standby pattern: Primary remains in the US (we can pin to us-west-2). Warm-standby regions can be provisioned in EU/APAC but configured as passive; no live PHI processing occurs there unless you explicitly trigger failover. Backups and replicated state are encrypted at rest and in transit.

- KV cache & token cache: we do not automatically replicate in-memory KV or session caches across regions for pinned deployments. By default only persistent snapshots and append-only logs required for recovery are replicated (encrypted). This avoids accidental propagation of sensitive conversational context.

- RTO/RPO: typical warm-standby with pre-provisioned instances gives an RTO in the order of 10–20 minutes for request routing + model load if caches are warmed; cold failover (no warm VMs) is hours. We’ll provide a table with exact numbers per model class.

- Access to plaintext: Redwood’s control plane engineers do not have routine access to customer plaintext. During a failover we rely on customer-controlled KMS or our HSM integrations; we can produce a runbook that specifies who is allowed to perform key rotation or manual decrypt steps. We can also provide an inventory of roles that could be involved during incident response.

Recommended configuration for Horizon (minimum friction + compliance):
1) Pin primary processing to us-west-2.
2) Configure a warm-standby in us-east-1 (US warm-standby) rather than a non-US region — this satisfies the PHI-in-US requirement while keeping DR latency low.
3) If you still want an international DR copy, use encrypted snapshot replication to an EU project that remains locked under your control and is only used for cold restore.

Cost note: warm-standby in-region (us-east-1) increases committed capacity costs versus pure cold backups; we’ll run a cost delta for the options so you can present to procurement.
