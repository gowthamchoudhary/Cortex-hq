Hi —

Appreciate the clear asks. I put together an executive synopsis below (short paragraphs you can paste into the SIG) and attached a one-pager and a working SIG answer workbook. Key distinctions first: latency/cost control and network isolation drive most differences; contractual SLAs and how we host customer data are the practical outcomes.

Hosted (Redwood API, multi-tenant):
- Redwood responsibility: full runtime, model hosting, logging/metrics collection in our tenancy, SOC 2 controls for physical and logical security, key management for platform-level keys. We operate the control plane and the inference runtime in our managed environments.
- Customer responsibility: do not send regulated content unless contractually agreed. Customers control API-level access (API keys) and their application-layer data sanitization. For SIG answers: "Redwood operates a SOC 2 audited multi-tenant hosted service; customers authenticate via API keys and are responsible for data classification and client-side encryption where required."

Dedicated (reserved GPU pools for a single customer):
- Redwood responsibility: dedicated compute and network segmentation within our cloud tenancy, guaranteed capacity and SLA per contract, and operational support for scaling and incident response. We manage OS, runtime, and patching unless specified otherwise.
- Customer responsibility: configuration choices (e.g., VPC peering, IP allowlists), customer data in the dedicated pool is logically segregated; customers may request specific logging retention settings in their contract. For SIG answers: "Redwood provides dedicated GPU capacity with logical segregation and contractual SLAs; customers remain responsible for application-level controls and any additional encryption they require."

Private / VPC / On-prem (customer-managed network or on-premises):
- Redwood responsibility: control plane (optional) and delivering installation packages, hardening guidance, and supporting integration. When deployed in the customer VPC or on-prem, Redwood does not manage customer network infrastructure; we provide connectors and integration points with customer KMS/HSM.
- Customer responsibility: full network control, host-level hardening, and physical access controls (on-prem). For SIG answers: "In Private deployments, the customer controls the compute environment and network boundaries; Redwood provides installation artifacts, documentation, and optional managed control-plane services where agreed in contract."
