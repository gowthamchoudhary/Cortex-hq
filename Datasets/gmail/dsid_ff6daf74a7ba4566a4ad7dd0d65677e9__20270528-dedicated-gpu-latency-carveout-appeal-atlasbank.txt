Hi Darren (and Soojin),

Thanks for the profile — that file makes the request concrete. I reviewed the peak counts and token distribution. High level recommendations and constraints below; I tried to keep legal-friendly language where appropriate so Contracts can reuse it.

1) Queueing and reserved pool behavior
- Dedicated/reserved pools are tenant-isolated: workloads pinned to a Dedicated pool will not be preempted by other customers. That said, tail latency during sustained spikes is a function of concurrency per-GPU and headroom.
- Our queueing has two priority bands for Dedicated: "reserved" (customer pinned) and "burst" (shared burst capacity). Reserved requests are dequeued before shared burst requests. That provides deterministic dispatching when adequate headroom is provisioned.

2) Sizing guidance (based on your CSV sample)
- Baseline steady QPS: ~800 requests/min (avg). Peak month-end QPS: ~4,800 req/min for ~5 hours.
- Recommendation: reserve 4x baseline (to cover steady plus buffer) and add a dedicated burst pool sized to cover an additional 2x for the 4–6 hour window. Practically: reserve N GPUs (I estimate N=12 on A100-equivalent for your model family) and configure burst pool of 6 GPUs with automated warm-up 30 minutes before window.

3) SLA carveout language (draft for Contracts)
- For the scheduled monthly batch window (defined as the last business day of each month, 22:00–02:00 local), Redwood will make commercially reasonable efforts to maintain 95th percentile latency <= 300ms for responses <=512 tokens for workloads routed to the customer’s Reserved pool. The performance commitment is dependent on the customer provisioning the recommended Reserved and Burst capacity and enabling pre-warming.
- In the event the 95p target is not met for two or more scheduled windows in a rolling 3-month period, Redwood will provide a remediation credit equal to 5% of the monthly Dedicated fee for each breached window beyond the first.

4) Operational controls and fallbacks
- Pre-warm hooks: API to trigger KV cache warm-up and prefix caching 45 minutes prior to the window.
- Canary: we recommend a 2-week canary where Redwood will instrument and report a per-minute latency dashboard and runbooks for the on-call escalation.
- If Atlas elects to increase headroom during the window (manual or policy-driven), we will auto-provision burst nodes and route requests to the dedicated burst pool.

5) Risks and notes
- If customer runs very large single requests (>4096 tokens) during the window, observed tail latency can spike; recommend enforcing a per-request token cap for the batch job or sharding large inputs.
- Memory-constrained models may require more GPUs than compute-bound models; final count will be validated during canary.

I attached a short draft of contract wording and a redlined example for Contracts to start from. Let me know if you want a technical call to walk through pre-warm APIs and queueing behavior.
