Thanks, good questions. Attached is a concise three-option worksheet and a pricing breakdown for deal AT-4821 (pricing_breakdown_AT-4821.pdf). Summary below:

Option A — Conservative (+10 GPUs)
- Expected impact: reduces average queuing delays; estimated tail P95 latency improvement ~6–8% during moderate spikes.
- Monthly delta: +$13,200 (amortized on committed reservation pricing).
- Tradeoff: minimal cost increase, limited headroom for multi-day surges.

Option B — Growth (+30 GPUs committed) + 20-GPU burst pool
- Expected impact: P95 latency improvement ~15–20% for typical surges; burst pool handles multi-day spikes up to 20 GPUs elastically.
- Monthly delta: +$35,400 base + burst consumption billed on usage when used.
- Tradeoff: best for predictable growth and when you want to avoid frequent throttling/fallbacks.

Option C — Lean committed base + monthly top-ups
- Expected impact: similar to A at baseline; relies on top-up cadence to cover sustained demand.
- Monthly delta: lower committed cost but higher variability; we estimate blended monthly delta ~$22,000 if you top-up twice per month.
- Tradeoff: cheapest near-term with operational overhead for top-ups.

Technical note (from Dev Patel, cc'd): we can also route non-critical workloads to the quantized model variant during peak windows automatically to reduce per-token load; this typically buys ~12–18% effective capacity without additional GPUs. If you'd like we can include a routing policy as part of the amendment.
