Jordan — thanks for the clear list and the tracked document. High level: we can accommodate most asks with specific operational guardrails; a couple of asks need tightened scope or alternate phrasing (mainly the fallback mirror and the proof-of-delete timing). Below are proposed compromises we can put in the DPA. I attached our response with edits (RDW_DPA_response_trackchanges.docx).

Proposed language highlights (summary):
- Operational remediation: Redwood will initiate containment actions within 24 hours of confirmed exposure for ephemeral caches and provide a remediation report within 7 business days. For backup/archive plane issues, Redwood will begin prioritized remediation within 48 hours and provide a status report every 5 business days until complete.

- Archival carveout / notice: Redwood agrees to a 30-day advance notice before any change that would materially increase archival retention for data covered by this DPA. For newly-created archived copies created due to product upgrades or migrations, Redwood will provide a one-time purge option to the customer within 30 days of discovery where technically feasible.

- Jurisdictional fallback mirror (emergency): Redwood can conduct a temporary, tenant-scoped transfer to a non-EU mirror only when (i) EU capacity is unavailable for >12 hours and (ii) the transfer is required to meet a customer-critical SLO or to remediate an incident. Such transfers will be logged, limited strictly to the affected tenant data, encrypted in transit, and accompanied by a post-event audit delivered within 7 business days. Customer pre-approval will be sought for non-urgent transfers; for urgent incident response we will notify within 24 hours.

- Proof-of-delete: Redwood will provide a deletion certificate for on-demand erasures within 10 business days for ephemeral and primary data. Certificates for backups/archives (where deletion requires multi-step purging) will be provided within 30 business days.

Trade-offs: shorter remediation timelines for ephemeral data, longer windows for backups; fallback allowed but with narrower trigger conditions and stronger post-event auditing. If these look acceptable, we can fold final wording into the DPA and route for signatures.
