Thanks Ben — quick takes below.

1) Entity carve-out: we can accept invoicing to a non-contracting entity for a fixed period (12 months) provided: (a) there is explicit language tying payment to performance under the Order Form, (b) ClearSunset provides evidence of assignment/authority (TaxResidency_Evidence.pdf helps), and (c) the contracting entity signs the MSA and remains liable. We should add a short clause: "Invoices may be directed to a designated affiliate for the first 12 months; contracting entity remains the party to the Agreement and liable for payment."

2) Net-60: legal is okay with the term but finance must pre-approve from a credit perspective. No legal showstoppers, but we should include late-payment interest (per standard schedule) and require purchase order reference on invoices.

3) VAT/withholding: we cannot unilaterally assume third-party tax liabilities in jurisdictions where withholding is statutory. We can agree to cooperate and provide documentation, and where appropriate credit-back gross-up language, but we should not accept open-ended withholding obligations. Recommend: (a) Clarify that customer is responsible for local withholding unless Redwood is required by law to gross-up, and (b) include a process for evidence and reimbursement.

4) Commercial offsets: if finance approves net-60, request either (i) a 10% increase to Q4 minimum commit or (ii) a 25% advance invoice on signing for first quarter.

I’ve drafted suggested clause text and marked changes in the attached spreadsheet (see PaymentSchedule_Suggestion.xlsx). Marcus — can you confirm credit limits and whether we can waive advance invoice for this account if they provide corporate guarantees?
