Sam — thanks for the clear ask and the redacted request. Putting an initial high-level answer below; looped in Vanessa (DR/replication) and Karthik (security/compliance) to confirm numbers and any caveats.

High level:
- Acceptance -> tombstone creation: immediate (within seconds) on the primary write path.
- Tombstone propagation -> regionally replicated storage: typical roll-forward within 2–6 hours for same-region replicas, 6–36 hours for cross-region async replicas depending on backlog.
- Snapshot immutability: snapshots taken before the delete will continue to reference pre-delete blocks. Those snapshots are logically immutable — meaning the deleted data can be present until that snapshot is expired or explicitly removed.
- Offsite/archival copies: may persist beyond normal snapshot expiry windows (policy-dependent).

Implications for restores: if you perform a restore from a snapshot or copy that was taken before the deletion, the restored dataset may contain the deleted records. If you restore from a backup taken after the expunge propagation completes (post-expunge snapshot), those records will not be present.

Mitigations we can offer:
- Targeted restore filters (preferred): when feasible, we can run a targeted restore job that excludes the deleted user IDs — we will need the identifiers in discrete form.
- Playbook for legal holds: we can pause expunge for affected objects to preserve for eDiscovery, then resume once hold is lifted.
- Verify window: we will provide an expected maximum propagation SLA and an annotated timeline for your account.

Thanks — adding the technical detail so we can give GreenBank concrete numbers. Quoting Michael below for context.

Notes (technical):
- Tombstone generation is immediate; it marks records for expunge and prevents new reads from surface indexes.
- Local region streaming: tombstones are included in the change stream and consumed by local replica appliers within a median of ~45 minutes; 95th percentile ~3 hours under normal load.
- Cross-region async copy: we ship a compacted change log to cold-region replication every 4 hours. Typical arrival-to-apply is 2–18 hours; 99th percentile in current telemetry is ~36 hours during peak backlog.
- Archive/offsite snapshots: incremental snapshots and deep cold archives are on a separate cadence (daily for incremental, weekly for deep archive). Deep archives can retain blocks for up to configurable retention durations — these are the longest-lived carriers.
- Restore semantics: a point-in-time restore will rehydrate historic blocks referenced by the snapshot. If the snapshot references slate that contains deleted blocks, those records will be present after restore.

Edge cases and controls:
- Ongoing DR exercises can cause replication backlogs; during a DR event the cross-region tail can extend beyond the 99th percentile. We can flag a customer and mark their expunge as higher priority to accelerate tail processing.
- For GreenBank's legal requirement, the recommended approach is: (1) identify the exact object identifiers, (2) schedule a targeted scrub to run once cross-region appliers confirm tombstone applied, (3) if immediate removal is required, coordinate a snapshot invalidation/expiry + targeted scrub for any cold archives (this may require additional egress/work and a short outage for the affected namespace).

Estimated max windows (conservative, account-specific, US-only):
- Same-region finality: 3–6 hours
- Cross-region finality: 24–72 hours (we will validate for your shard map; typical observed 24–36h)
- Deep archive removal: depends on archive TTL — could be days to weeks unless an admin-assisted purge is run.
