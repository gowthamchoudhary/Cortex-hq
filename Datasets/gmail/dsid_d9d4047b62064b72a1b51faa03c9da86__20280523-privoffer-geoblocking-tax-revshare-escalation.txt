Thanks for the context — I reviewed with finance and legal this morning. Key points: 

- Marketplace settlements: AWS/GCP typically remit gross to the seller (us) but certain EU/EMEA tax authorities and local marketplace rules can result in withholding or VAT treatment differences. If a withholding is applied at the marketplace layer, that will reduce the gross remittance to Redwood and therefore the cash we can use to pay any partner rev-share or account credits.

- Options we can consider: 
  * Require customer to provide an EU tax residency/VAT certificate before the offer is activated. This reduces the risk of unexpected withholdings but doesn't eliminate marketplace mechanics.
  * Insert a clause in the private-offer that the listed prices and rev-share are 'net of marketplace withholding' — i.e., we do not gross-up for tax withholdings (sales standard).
  * Approve a temporary rev-share adjustment (down to account for likely withholding) and then true-up once we get the actual settlement records for the first month.

- Net45 via marketplace: Both AWS and GCP private offers can support net terms, but the timing of marketplace pay-outs vs vendor invoicing can create a lag. Finance prefers cash-on-delivery or net30 for marketplace deals to avoid AR exposure, but we can accept net45 if the deal economics (commit amount / credits) justify it.

Recommendation for approval path: 
1) Require tax certificate as gating item.
2) Approve the private offer with the requested discounts and onboarding credit, but add a 1.75% temporary rev-share holdback to account for likely withholding; true-up after first settlement month. 
3) Approve Net45 for this account given the strategic nature and commit size ($500k TCV estimate) — but require finance sign-off on AR guardrails.
