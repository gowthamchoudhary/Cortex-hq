Hi all — dug into the numbers and the ops path. Longer form notes below so procurement has a clear artifact to attach. I focused on three scenarios and included the quick math for Helium Health's reported monthly run-rate (MRR) of $120k across existing metered SKUs.

Attachments: sku_mapping_v2.xlsx (tabbed: before/after), invoice-flow-diagram.pdf

Scenario A — Conservative (no change to partner net):
- We consolidate SKUs only for display. Private-offer price commitments remain per-deal and net-of-discount stays identical.
- Reseller margin: 0% change relative to current net revenue split.
- Invoice path: reseller pass-through preserved (no ops change).
- Operational impact: minimal; mapping is bookkeeping only.
Estimated impact on Helium Health: $0 delta.

Scenario B — Aggregation rounding effect (small delta):
- Consolidation groups small metered SKUs into buckets. When aggregated, rounding/truncation across price tiers can create +/-0.4% effective delta in tokened metered billing for some usage shapes.
- Example math (monthly $120,000): +/- $480 per month in gross invoice variance pre-tax. Reseller margin moves proportionally (if reseller takes 10% of gross, their cut changes by ~$48/month).
- Invoice path: unchanged unless listing parity requested.

Scenario C — Listing parity requested (billing path flips to provider):
- If Helium requests listing parity and AWS/GCP change the marketplace listing, billing transfers to provider->customer. This changes how reseller margin is realized: reseller receives a commission from provider versus issuing the invoice directly.
- Typical outcomes: slight lag in reseller payout timing and potential withhold/commissioning fees depending on the marketplace. Margin impact ranges 0.5–2.0% of gross depending on reseller agreement.
- Ops note: we need Marketplace Ops to confirm specific commission schedules.
