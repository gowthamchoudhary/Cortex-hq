Hi Alice / Monica,

Below is a concise, source-of-truth style answer you can share with your compliance and procurement teams. I'm keeping language customer-facing and actionable.

1) Terminology we use - "Request/prompt": any user-supplied input (raw text, prompt fields, attachments) sent into the API.
- "Generated output/response": model-produced content (completion text, chat assistant replies, structured JSON outputs).
- "Streaming fragments": transient token buffers produced mid-stream before a finalization event.

2) Default capture windows (hosted API)
- Requests/Prompts: retained by default for 30 days. These logs are used for routing, debugging, rate-limiting analysis, and security incident investigation.
- Generated outputs (finalized): retained by default for 90 days. We retain outputs longer to support quality monitoring, evals, and model performance regressions unless a customer opts out.
- Streaming fragments (non-finalized): ephemeral by default; retained for up to 24 hours and then discarded. If a stream is finalized (i.e., session closed and final output emitted), the finalized output follows the "Generated outputs" TTL above.
- KV cache / session ephemeral caches: not stored as part of long-term logs; flushed at session end and not retained beyond session scope unless explicitly persisted by a customer API flag.

3) Why the difference?
- We separate prompts from outputs because they serve different operational uses: prompts are privacy-sensitive and often contain PII; outputs are necessary to monitor model quality and regressions. The asymmetric TTL balances privacy and operational needs.

4) Deletion & erasure evidence (customer-initiated)
- Customers may request account-wide erasure or per-record deletion via support ticket or the Console API.
- Default hosted erasure flow: raise ticket -> support validates ownership -> job scheduled -> deletion job ID issued -> propagation across replicas. Typical completion: 48–72 hours for standard projects; SLA-backed timelines are available under enterprise contracts.
- Evidence: upon completion we provide a signed, time-stamped deletion job record (job id, timestamp, affected buckets) and a short audit bundle (log of deletion steps). For regulatory audits we can provide additional metadata under NDA/contract.

5) Regional residency notes
- Hosted requests and outputs are stored in the region you select at project creation (US, EU, APAC). For the EU region we keep all primary logs in EU storage. Minimal routing metadata may traverse other regions for load balancing, but that metadata does not contain user text and is retained only for short-lived control-plane troubleshooting. For strict residency needs we recommend Redwood Dedicated or Redwood Private (VPC/on‑prem) and contractual residency guarantees.

6) Opt-outs & flags
- You can opt out of output retention via a project flag ("disable_output_retention"). When enabled: outputs are not persisted beyond ephemeral session state and are not available to our quality systems. Prompts retention opt-out is available under enterprise controls (requires contract) due to higher operational risk.

7) Attachments and artifacts
- Binary attachments referenced in requests (uploads) are treated like prompts for retention purposes. They are retained for the same TTL as prompts unless otherwise configured.

8) Practical examples (short)
- Customer sends a one-off prompt on May 1: prompt retained until May 31 by default. Model response generated May 1 retained until July 30 by default.
- Customer streams live audio on May 1 for 10 minutes and finalizes transcript: transient fragments up to 24 hours; finalized transcript retained 90 days by default.
