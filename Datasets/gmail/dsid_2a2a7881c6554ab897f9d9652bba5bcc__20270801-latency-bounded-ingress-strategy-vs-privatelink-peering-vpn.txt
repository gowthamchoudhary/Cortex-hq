Hi Emily — thanks for the clear constraints and the doc. I pulled the team together and sketched an initial recommendation. High level up front: PrivateLink as the primary connectivity pattern, with a controlled peering fall-back for intra-AZ low-latency workloads; S2S VPN only as a last-resort (DR/testing) due to jitter and throughput limitations.

I attached a simple diagram and a latency/cost spreadsheet. Below is a concise tradeoff table and the recommended next steps so you can share with compliance.

Short tradeoff summary (p99-sensitive, compliance-first):
- PrivateLink (recommended primary): No transitive routing, endpoint-specific NLB + interface endpoints, supports private-only access, good auditability (flow logs + endpoint policies). Expected additional latency: ~6–12ms overhead (NLB + ENI hop) inside region; consistent and low jitter.
- VPC Peering (recommended secondary when high-throughput intra-VPC traffic): Lowest network hop count and typically ~2–6ms extra latency intra-AZ, but requires cross-account route table entries and careful management to avoid accidental transitive routing. Harder to centralize per-endpoint access controls.
- Site-to-Site VPN: Easiest to stand up for initial testing but not suitable for p99 SLAs — packetization, MTU fragmentation, and variable jitter can add 20–50ms and throughput caps. Use as temporary DR path or for non-latency-critical flows.

Operational notes:
- Audit & logging: PrivateLink offers fine-grained endpoint logs plus VPC Flow Logs at the ENI level; we can push logs to your SIEM.
- Mutual TLS / mTLS: We can terminate TLS at the endpoint and require client certs for request auth.
- Failover: We recommend a canary route policy: prefer PrivateLink; if PrivateLink NLB health checks fail, route to a peered path in the same AZ (A/B fallback) — avoids routing across third-party VPCs.

Rajiv — thanks for the details. Quick answers ahead of the call:

- Overlapping CIDR: PrivateLink avoids VPC-level route table changes, so overlapping CIDRs are a non-issue for endpoint access. For peering, we would need non-overlapping CIDRs.
- Source whitelisting: With PrivateLink we can combine endpoint policies and security-group-level controls on the endpoint ENIs; we can also publish a short-lived allowlist token for app-level binding.
