Hi Marta — thanks for the clear list. High-level options below; I'll follow up with an implementation checklist and a sample signed manifest by EOD Monday.

1) Export formats and schemas
- Parquet (columnar) with a companion JSONL event index: best for large historical slices and efficient PII redaction.
- Single-file JSONL: easier for ad-hoc queries and streaming but larger.
- Each exported event includes: event_id, timestamp_utc, actor_internal (redwood_id), action, target_resource, ip_address_raw (masked if PII rule), kv_meta (operation context), and operator_session_id.

2) Incremental / delta delivery patterns
- Daily delta bundles (UTC day) + on-demand delta window (t+0..t+6h) for live QA.
- Each bundle carries a signed manifest (HMAC-SHA256) and per-file SHA256 checksums; manifest itself is signed with Redwood's KMS-backed key and timestamped.

3) Transport and attestation
- Preferred: Customer-owned S3 bucket (cross-account write) with an S3 prefix per day. Fallback: SFTP with signed manifest.
- Proof-of-delivery: We publish a delivery receipt entry in the manifest and provide a signed ack (JSON) with envelope encryption metadata (KMS key id).

4) PII & jurisdictional redaction
- Two-stage redaction: (a) field-level masking rules at export time (deterministic hash for emails, truncated IP blocks) and (b) tenant-slice redaction when requested (we can skip record exports for EU tenant ids).
- Redaction policy configurable per export request and recorded in manifest.

5) Legal holds and retention carveouts
- We honor RFC-constrained legal holds: we can produce a separate immutable bundle that preserves raw operator-event lines for a limited window (requires enterprise Private/Dedicated contract).

Open questions for you:
- Do you want deterministic pseudonymization (consistent mapping across exports) or irreversible hashing?
- Preferred transport (S3 vs SFTP) and whether you can provide cross-account write access.
