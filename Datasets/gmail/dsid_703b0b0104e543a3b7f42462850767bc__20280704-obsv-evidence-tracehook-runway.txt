Thanks Alicia — answers inline and a few technical notes.

- Throughput & sizing: expected baseline from Redwood for the Acme test will be ~120 events/sec (peaks up to 600 eps for heavy inference bursts). This assumes token-level telemetry is aggregated to an event per request (we won't stream per-token logs into the SIEM).

- Payload example (we'll attach a sample file):
  {"timestamp":"2028-07-05T15:00:12Z","event_type":"inference_request","service":"redwood-inference","trace_id":"4b7f...","span_id":"9d1a...","request_id":"req_abc123","model":"rwkv-14b-quant","response_tokens":42,"user_id":"acct-254"}

- TLS/certs: we'll perform mutual TLS client auth from the forwarder to your LogRidge endpoint. For the initial test we can use a short-lived client cert; in production we'll integrate with Acme's KMS-backed signing and OCSP checks.

- Tracehooks: tracehook_spec.yaml includes where in the call graph we emit span-level annotations (ingress, model_load, kvcache_hit, token_emit). We will only emit PII-free identifiers; raw user text will never be forwarded to the SIEM in the standard telemetry pipeline (it stays in encrypted object storage behind your VPC).

- Retention controls: evidence_proof_manifest.pdf spells out the lifecycle. For the handoff we will push a digest manifest and a zipped evidence payload to your S3 secure share if you prefer. Otherwise we will send individual events to the HEC.
