Quick update from the runtime team:

- Confirmed transient queue saturation on two host shards running thunderbolt-7b for the us-west-2 pool between 08:45–09:10 PDT. We observed a pattern of head-of-line blocking when several long-context (>1k) requests landed simultaneously and the scheduler attempted tight coalescing.
- KV cache eviction spikes correlated with a GC job that ran at 08:50; that amplified latencies for cache reloads and caused some scheduling timeouts (~250–400ms extra on affected hosts).
- No recent kernel code rollouts in the last 48h, but we did push a new autobalance policy to the scheduler two days ago; early hypothesis: policy interacts poorly with certain request-length distributions and burst arrivals.

Mitigation applied at 10:05 PDT: temporarily relaxed coalescing window and increased per-host concurrent request cap for thunderbolt-7b on the affected shard set; also disabled the GC compaction schedule for 30m. Immediate effect: 503 rate dropped to ~0.05% for the shard set.

Thanks for your patience while our team investigated. Below is a concise summary you can share internally.

What happened
- Between 08:45–09:10 PDT on 2027-08-31 a confluence of long-context requests and a GC compaction job caused KV cache pressure on a subset of us-west-2 hosts running thunderbolt-7b. That pressure increased scheduler latency during coalescing decisions and triggered request timeouts that surfaced as 503s for a small fraction of calls.

What we fixed
- Immediate mitigations (10:05 PDT): relaxed coalescing window, raised per-host concurrent caps, and deferred GC compaction. This reduced 503s from ~0.8% to ~0.05% for the affected shard set.
- Deployed a guarded scheduler flag and the patched autobalance policy to canary hosts (see RW-INC-28934 and attached thunderbolt-patch.diff).
- Added a KV-eviction alert driven by GC and a dashboard panel for long-context burst detection.

What we will do next
- Permanently add hysteresis to scheduling/coalescing logic (delivery ETA: 2027-09-08).
