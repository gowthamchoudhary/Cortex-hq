Evelyn/Marissa — quick summary of what I see after pulling runtime traces and host-level logs for the window around req_9f3a2f6c.

Findings:
- Server-side generation for the request completed (tokens produced) and our output buffer pushed chunks.
- Downstream: TCP-level keepalive and chunked frames for that stream terminated unexpectedly at the proxy edge for some connections. We see a correlated increase in 499/504 at the CDN layer for the same minute.
- There are signs of partial frame buffering — i.e., the client receives the last emitted chunk but then no more frames until the client times out.
- Spike aligns with autoscaler behavior: during the peak window a subset of dedicated instances hit short-lived GC pauses when swapping quantized kernels; that correlates with a temporary increase in jitter.

Hypothesis: a compound issue where transient host-level stalls (GC/kernel swaps) increase latency long enough that some intermediate proxies close idle chunked connections or the CDN's streaming time-window expires for long-running streams. The net result is the client sees a silent stall even though generation continues server-side.

Immediate actions taken:
- Increased socket keepalive aggressiveness on the serving fleet for the next 12 hours to reduce proxy idles.
- Pinned kernel selection for the model variants serving ShopFront to avoid in-flight kernel swaps during peak for the next 24h.
- Opened a CDN ticket referencing the timestamps and examples (HUBSPOT-68542).

Requesting exec attention: if we confirm an SLA impact >5% on peak conversion, DX/GTML leadership will need to sign off on interim credits and a formal postmortem timeline. I recommend elevating if we see continued repeats in the next 24h.
