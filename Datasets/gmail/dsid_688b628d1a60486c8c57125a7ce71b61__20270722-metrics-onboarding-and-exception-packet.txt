Elena — thanks for the clear list. Answers below are concise for the packet; I attached a one-page playbook (SLO_measurement_playbook.pdf) and will follow up with a Grafana invite link if you confirm the team and emails.

1) Uptime definition: Availability computed per-region as percent of successful 2xx responses from the public API endpoint(s), excluding scheduled maintenance windows explicitly published on the status page. We use a 30-day rolling window for contractual SLO evaluation; monthly SLA calculations use calendar-month aggregation.

2) Latency SLOs and sampling: Default public targets (hosted tier) — p50: 50ms, p95: 180ms, p99: 350ms for text-generation requests of <=512 tokens. Sampling: continuous sampling of all requests for percentiles; heavy-tail isolation uses reservoir sampling of large requests. Warm-up/maintenance windows are excluded from SLA calculations only when marked as scheduled maintenance in the control plane (we annotate the timeline).

3) Alerting and runbooks: Primary alerts (latency spike, error-rate increase, KV-cache pressure) fire at conservative thresholds (e.g., sustained p95 > 250ms for 5m). Pager escalation: on-call SRE -> senior SRE -> engineering manager -> platform lead. We attached the playbook section covering incident roles and expected initial response times (SLO_measurement_playbook.pdf, sec. 3).

4) Customer access: We provide read-only Grafana panels scoped per-account (dashboard slices for throughput, latency percentiles, error-rate, cache hit-rate) plus a short-lived Prometheus pull token for deeper queries when approved. Typical onboarding access: 14 days; for migration tests we can extend to 30 days on request. We'll grant view-only for your migration window if you provide the list of emails.

5) Retention: Request-level telemetry retained for 90 days; aggregated metrics and percentile rollups retained 13 months. Trace samples are retained for 30 days. Query limits exist for raw-export endpoints; we can provide a temporary higher quota for migration testing.

6) Temporary SLO exceptions: Standard process — customer requests exception with justification and timeframe; Product/Risk reviews and SRE approves operational mitigations; legal signs off on contractual language if SLA credits or formal deviations are needed. We track exceptions in our internal rollout tracker and communicate residual risk in the procurement packet. Marcus Lin (cc) manages exception approvals for Dedicated/onboarding flows.
