Evan / Camila / Ben,

Thanks for the context. Below is a pragmatic compromise we typically propose for customers with the concerns Novacorp raised. It addresses defend-vs-control, evidence, carveouts, and caps while keeping commercial risk manageable for Redwood.

Proposed points (high level):
- Defense approach: Redwood to have defend-and-control for third-party IP claims that allege infringement by Redwood's trained model artifacts or pre-packaged models (i.e., where Redwood's model output, independent of customer input, is alleged to infringe). For claims arising from customer-provided prompts or customer data/inputs, offer a reimburse-and-cooperate approach unless Redwood has clear reason to assume the claim arises from Redwood-controlled code or model weights.
- Cap: cap indemnity for IP infringement at the greater of (a) the Order Form fees for the preceding 12 months or (b) $1,000,000, subject to higher insurance limits if requested.
- Evidence & production: list required evidence types and a staged production plan: (i) claim notice and public complaint, (ii) specific allegedly infringing prompt/output pairs and timestamps, (iii) model version & execution logs (redacted where necessary), (iv) minimal request/response metadata (hashes + timestamps). Proposed SLA: initial acknowledgement within 48 hours of notice, first evidence bundle within 10 business days unless a court order prevents production.
- Carveouts: explicit carveout for open-source dependencies governed by their own licenses; carveout for customer modifications to Redwood-deployed models.
- Cooperation & subrogation: standard cooperation obligations; Redwood to reasonably assist Novacorp's counsel. Subrogation rights limited to cases where Redwood has paid settlement or judgment.
- Insurance: Redwood confirms it maintains commercial general liability and cyber insurance; can share certificate of insurance and SOC 2 Type II within a week.

Draft clause samples attached in plain text for review (legal_indemnity_proposal.txt). If Novacorp insists on vendor-not-controlled defence for customer-input claims, we can discuss a narrow allocation (e.g., Redwood will reimburse defense costs up to the cap and reserve the right to assume defense if it believes it can control a better outcome).
