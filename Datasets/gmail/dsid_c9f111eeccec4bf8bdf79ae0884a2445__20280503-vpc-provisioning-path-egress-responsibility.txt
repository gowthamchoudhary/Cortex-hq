Alex — great questions. Quick recap and proposed next steps below. I pulled in Aditya (solutions/networking) and Ben (sales ops) to help with specifics and procurement cadence.

Recap answers (short):
- VPC ownership: two supported patterns — (A) FinPay provisions a customer VPC and we instruct on routing/security (fastest for security teams), or (B) Redwood can provision a dedicated VPC in your AWS account via our deployment scripts (we need IAM role access). Either works; we’ll document both flows.
- PrivateLink vs Peering: PrivateLink is recommended when you want explicit service endpoints and tighter control (no route table changes). Peering can be simpler if you already allow peering and don’t mind changing routing. For cross-account isolation and ease of IP allowlisting, PrivateLink is usually our suggestion.
- IPs for allowlist: If PrivateLink, you only need to allow the endpoint ENIs in your account — we'll provide a short list of interface IPs per AZ. If Peering, you need to allow our advertised CIDR ranges.
- Ports: we'll provide a definitive port matrix, but essentials are 443 for control/data, 8080/metrics (optional) for scraping if you opt in, and a few ephemeral ranges for internal runtime sync.
- Egress ownership: typically FinPay owns egress for calls originating in your VPC; Redwood only makes outbound calls from our control plane when explicitly configured. We can also route certain third-party callbacks through Redwood-managed egress on request (this adds procurement work).
