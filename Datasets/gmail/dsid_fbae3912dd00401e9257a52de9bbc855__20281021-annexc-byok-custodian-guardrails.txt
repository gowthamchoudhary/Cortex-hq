Emma — thanks, and appreciate the clear checklist. I've sketched a short Annex C that maps responsibilities and the proposed guardrails; summary below and full Annex attached as AnnexC_BYOK_Matrix_v1.pdf. Key points:

- BYOK model: Customer holds primary CMK (created and managed in their KMS). Redwood can hold an encrypted escrow copy only if the customer signs an Escrow Addendum and enables hardware‑backed WRAP/KEK; escrow material is sealed and released only under mutually agreed triggers (court order / data destruction / customer request).

- Operator access: Redwood operators do not have persistent access to unwrapped customer CMKs. Temporary access for triage is allowed only under a three‑party approval (customer SRE contact OR named security approver + Redwood infra oncall + recorded breakglass ticket). All sessions are session‑limited, MFA protected, and recorded as audit events with a signed approval artifact.

- Telemetry & minimum evidence: We will produce a triage packet containing redacted low‑level logs, a timeline, and a snapshot of request ids (no plaintext keys). Telemetry can be delivery‑scoped; by default we collect connection metadata, ops commands, and anonymized error stacks. If you require stricter telemetry minimization we can suppress debug payloads for the private deployment.

- Subprocessors: We will list any third‑party cloud providers or managed‑ops partners used for the Private control plane. For BYOK deployments in your environment we restrict subprocessors to those approved in the DPA annex; any change triggers a 30‑day notice and option to object.

- Cross‑border transfers: Control plane metadata may traverse regions for routing/failover. We propose standard SCCs + an annex clause that customer keys and plaintext never leave customer VPC/on‑prem boundary; only telemetry & anonymized metadata may cross borders.

Karthik — nice draft. A few suggested edits from legal/privacy perspective:

1) Make the escrow release triggers explicit and narrow: addition of 'court order' should be coupled with a customer‑notice carve‑out and a right to seek a protective order; add a requirement that any escrow release requires a verifiable out‑of‑band authorization from the customer's named approver.

2) Operator session artifacts: require immutable audit hashes (SHA256) of session recordings and store those hashes in our immutable log retained per DPA retention schedule. This helps Aurea verify tamper integrity without us sharing raw recordings.

3) Telemetry minimization: add an opt‑in toggle in the deployment plan for debug payloads (default off). Also include a clause that specifies only anonymized identifiers are shared in support tickets unless explicit consent is granted by the customer's security approver.

4) Subprocessor emergency clause: we can permit temporary engaged partners for incident response but only under the same approval flow and with a documented subprocessor contract addendum.
