Laura, Ben — quick technical proposal and example math. High level: we currently compute p50/p95 over calendar-day epochs defined in UTC (00:00:00 to 23:59:59 UTC). That keeps calculation deterministic across regions. For DST days:

Proposal (what we can commit to):
- Base behavior: UTC-anchored 24h epoch. Each epoch contains N requests where timestamps are normalized to UTC. Percentiles computed over per-request token latencies for that epoch.
- DST handling: on days where local time changes, the UTC epoch still has 24h; the local wall clock may show 23h or 25h. Under UTC anchoring the p95 will be computed from the same 24h worth of events and there is no automatic carveout.
- Optional carveout: we can include a one-day temporal carveout for customers that request local-time semantics. Implementation detail: mark the DST transition epoch as "excluded_epoch" for SLO calculations and provide an example remediation credit calculation (pro rata daily credit).

Worked example (fall-back, 25-hour local day):
- UTC epoch: 2027-11-06T00:00:00Z - 2027-11-06T23:59:59Z (24 hours).
- Local PST: on fall-back the clocks repeat one hour; local requests that map to the repeated hour have distinct UTC timestamps and are included normally in the UTC epoch. p95 = 95th percentile over all requests in the UTC window. No double-counting occurs because all events are unique by UTC timestamp.

Spring-forward (23-hour local day): similar logic — fewer local-wall-clock hours map into the UTC epoch, but the epoch length is unchanged. p95 computed over the observed request set for that UTC window.

Suggested contract language snippets:
- Option A (default): "Redwood measures latency SLOs over UTC calendar-day epochs (00:00:00 to 23:59:59 UTC). Percentiles (p50/p95) are calculated over all recorded requests in each UTC epoch."
- Option B (DST carveout): "Notwithstanding the foregoing, for customers that elect local-wall-clock SLO semantics, Redwood will exclude the UTC epoch that overlaps a local DST transition day from SLO measurement for that customer for one (1) day, and the parties will apply a pro-rated credit mechanism for any eligible SLO violations."

Operational note: the carveout requires a tagging mechanism per-customer and a small reporting pipeline change to mark excluded epochs — estimated dev effort: 1-2 sprints for a tested release. Billing/legal impact: credits would be calculated against daily SLO targets.

Happy to draft a short addendum. Which option does Sales/legal prefer?

Option A (Recommended, simpler):
- Measurement anchor: UTC calendar-day epochs (00:00-24:00 UTC).
- DST: no special carveouts. p95 is computed over the UTC epoch as observed; this is deterministic and lowest operational complexity.

When to pick A: customers who prefer operational simplicity and deterministic billing; no engineering change needed.

Option B (Optional local-time semantics with DST carveout):
- Measurement anchor: UTC for primary calculations, but Redwood will exclude the UTC epoch that overlaps a local DST transition day for Summit9s designated production regions and treat it as a one-day carveout. Any violation credit tied to that day will be pro-rated per the daily SLO credit method in the addendum.
- Impact: requires tagging and a minor reporting pipeline change (1-2 sprints).

Example: imagine a fall-back day where local clocks repeat an hour. Under Option A that repeated hour results in additional recorded requests within the same UTC epoch and p95 is computed over the full set. Under Option B we would exclude that UTC epoch from SLO computations for Summit only.
