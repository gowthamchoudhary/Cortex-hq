Sean / team — reviewing Storify's proposed language and context. Summary of legal view and recommended compromise below. Attachment: legal_redline.docx

1) Risk profile: the ask is reasonable as a commercial starting point, but open‑ended carve-outs tied to "third-party models" are risky unless we define boundaries. Major risk drivers: (i) unsandboxed user uploads (hard to trace provenance), (ii) cross-customer model reuse, and (iii) indemnity demands that morph into broad product liability claims.

2) Recommended compromise language (high-level):
- Carve-out scope: only claims for which Vendor's hosted inference (not customer-hosted integrations) is the proximate cause of the alleged infringement, and the claim relates to a third-party model or content that Vendor knowingly distributed or knowingly made available to Storify as part of the integration.
- Damage type: limited to "actual direct damages" (no punitive, exemplary, or consequential).
- Sub-cap: aggregate cap for IP carve-outs = $1,000,000 per rolling 12 months (subject to change for very large enterprise deals).
- Defense mechanics: Storify must provide prompt written notice; Redwood may assume or share defense; Redwood's indemnification obligations require that Storify (i) allow Redwood to control defense and settlement, and (ii) not accept obligations that materially prejudice Redwood.

3) Operational asks (what we need from Sales / Product / CS to accept this):
- A short MSA schedule or order form attachment that confirms Redwood-hosted inference only and documents how Storify will isolate/label third-party models.
- Confirmation on who is responsible for model provenance logs for incidents.

4) Suggested fallback if Storify refuses sub-cap: accept carve-out but limit recoverable remedies to court-awarded direct damages with a notice/control requirement and require Storify to carry insurance that covers such claims.

We'll put the suggested clause into legal_redline.docx and can iterate. Marissa, please chime on whether $1M is acceptable commercially for this account size.
