Hi Laura — thanks for the clear ask and the attachment. Short answer up front: we can model both approaches, but our standard practice for private offers is to treat the seller fee as an external marketplace deduction (so the list-price floor is the anchor for revenue recognition and the seller fee is reconciled as an onboarding/platform deduction). Folks — attaching a concise workback and two scenarios. High-level position:

Scenario A (seller fee deducted first):
- Gross list price: $100,000
- Marketplace seller fee (assume 10%): $10,000 (deducted by marketplace)
- Remaining pool: $90,000
- Reseller passback (we can contractually reserve 20% of gross or 20% of remaining pool — typical is 20% of gross to keep reseller economics predictable)
- If 20% of gross: Reseller = $20,000, Redwood net = $70,000
- If 20% of remaining: Reseller = $18,000, Redwood net = $72,000

Scenario B (list-price floor honored first):
- Gross list price: $100,000
- Reseller guaranteed passback (20% of gross) booked as contractual line: $20,000
- Marketplace seller fee (10%) applied to listing revenue; engineering/finance treat as third-party deduction: $10,000
- Redwood net = $70,000 (same final net in this simple example if passback is defined on gross)

Recommendation: Contractually define reseller passback as a percent of gross list price (gives partner certainty). Treat the marketplace seller fee as an operational deduction; show it as a separate reconciliation line in the invoice/settlement. That avoids double-dipping and makes audit easier.
