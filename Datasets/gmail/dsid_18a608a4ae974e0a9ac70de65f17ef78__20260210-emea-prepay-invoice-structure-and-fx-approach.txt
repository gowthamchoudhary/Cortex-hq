Hi Anika — appreciate the detailed questions. Below are three pragmatic options we typically offer for EMEA multi-year prepays; I'll attach the term sheet, a sample EUR invoice, and our WHT guidance. Happy to jump on a quick call if you prefer.

Option A — USD invoice from Redwood Inc (no FX risk for Redwood):
- We invoice in USD from Redwood Inc (US), customer pays in EUR and bears FX risk.
- Simple commercial treatment; AP will need to manage FX.
- VAT: Redwood Inc does not charge EU VAT — reverse-charge typically applies if you have an EU VAT number (confirm with your tax team).
- WHT: If local WHT applies, customer should provide certificate; otherwise, standard withholding applies and invoiced amount would be net. Redwood does not gross-up by default for WHT.

Option B — EUR invoice from Redwood EU Ltd (recommended for EU-registered customers):
- Redwood EU Ltd invoices in EUR and is VAT-registered in the EU.
- We can offer a 12-month fixed FX lock for EUR pricing with an annual true-up on FX delta thereafter. For a 3-year prepay we typically do a yearly true-up to align to realized FX.
- VAT: If you provide EU VAT numbers, we can apply reverse-charge or issue net invoices where applicable; otherwise VAT will be charged at point of invoicing.
- WHT: For most intra-EU payments WHT is not applicable; for payments from outside EU subsidiaries we can accept WHT certificates to avoid gross-up. If WHT is applied and no certificate is provided, Redwood would need to invoice gross-up if explicitly agreed in deal terms.

Option C — Dual approach (master USD contract + local EUR invoices for tax):
- Master commercial agreement quotes in USD with a USD-denominated commitment; Redwood issues localized VAT invoices from Redwood EU Ltd for local AP processing.
- This reduces legal complexity while satisfying local tax/VAT requirements.

Commercial mechanics (short answers):
- Ramp schedule: We can structure prepaid credits to allocate 50% Year 1 / 30% Year 2 / 20% Year 3 by default, with an option to front-load more if you expect heavier usage upfront.
- Discount: for 3-year upfront commitment we typically provide ~15% term discount; 2-year prepay is ~10%.
- Refunds / early termination: refunds are pro rata against remaining prepaid value minus the applied term discount (standard approach); legal can provide an early-termination schedule if needed.

Diego — quick responses below from Finance (Ben) and Legal (Monica).

Ben (Finance):
- Invoicing entity: For EU-registered customers we prefer to invoice from Redwood EU Ltd (Ireland). That avoids most WHT exposure and simplifies VAT handling for your AP. We can issue EUR invoices and show VAT fields as required.
- FX: Finance can support a 12-month fixed EUR price with an annual true-up. We do not recommend locking for more than 12 months without hedging — the true-up mitigates long-term exposure. For a 3-year prepay we would do yearly reconciliations.
- Gross-up: Our default is not to gross-up. If WHT is applied and the customer can provide an official WHT exemption/certificate, we can issue net invoices. If no certificate is available and law requires withholding, we do not automatically gross-up unless commercial approval is granted (we will escalate if needed).

Monica (Legal):
- Refunds / early termination: Include pro rata refund clause for unused prepaid credits, calculated on net usage value with the initial term discount amortized over the term (standard approach). Example: a 15% term discount is amortized monthly, any unused months are refunded at the amortized net rate.
- WHT language: Insert clause requiring customer to provide tax residency/WHT certificates prior to payment or commit to gross-up if taxes are imposed and a certificate is not provided (commercial to confirm).
- VAT: If you invoice from Redwood EU Ltd and customer provides valid EU VAT numbers, apply reverse-charge. If not, charge applicable VAT and follow local reporting.
- DocuSign/contract: legal has redlines on the term sheet (see Redwood_Term_Sheet_Redlines.docx).
