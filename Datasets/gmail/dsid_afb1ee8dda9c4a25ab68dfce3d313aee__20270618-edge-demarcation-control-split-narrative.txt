Carl —

Thanks for the clear ask. We're preparing a short narrative and a diagram set. High level answers up front:

- Demarcation point: customer tenant traffic terminates at the inference gateway inside the customer's Dedicated pool or Private VPC; control plane (model management, telemetry, orchestration) traverses Redwood-managed control subnets that are logically separated and encrypted in transit.
- Control-plane split: management/control channels use a distinct TLS-authenticated mTLS channel over a dedicated peering/management VPC; only metadata and model manifests flow over this channel; KV cache and runtime tokens remain in-customer tenant boundary unless customer opts into shared cache feature.
- Edge modes: for Private (VPC) we push a lightweight control agent into the customer VPC that establishes an outbound-only VPN to Redwood control endpoints; for on-prem air-gapped customers we provide a signed bundle and offline approval flow.

Team — quick technical clarifications to include in the narrative before we send to Veridian:

- Explicitly call out that tenant inference traffic uses ephemeral compute in the Dedicated pool; compute hosts never receive KMS plaintext keys. We use envelope encryption and KMS references; HSM-backed keys can be used for Private on-prem customers.
- The management/control channel authenticates using per-customer mTLS certs issued by our CA; cert rotation is automated via our control-plane CA service.
- Egress: by default we restrict outbound egress to a small allowlist (CRLs and software updates) and enforce egress proxies in Dedicated; in Private deployments customers can provide NAT/egress endpoints — we document required ports in the matrix.
