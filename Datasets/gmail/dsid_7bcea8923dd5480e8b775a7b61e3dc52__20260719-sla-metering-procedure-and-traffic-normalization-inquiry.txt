Thanks for the clear list. High-level answers below and I've attached a sample metering workbook (sla_metering_workbook.xlsx). Dev will follow up with the low-level telemetry and normalization rules that your compliance team is likely to want.

- Measurement points: primary measurement is at the ingress gateway (edge LB) for availability; latency SLOs are measured end-to-end from ingress to response completion, with a secondary confirmation at the model-serving layer for internal diagnostics.

- Percentiles & windows: latency SLOs are defined on p99 measured over a rolling 30-day window by default (can be 7-day or 90-day per contract). We exclude client-side retries from p99 calculations (we count successful first-attempt latency for SLO purposes) and flag cache-hits when caching is explicitly enabled in the route config.

- Maintenance & bursts: scheduled maintenance up to a defined monthly allowance (default: 4 hours/month) is excluded from uptime calculations when scheduled through the console and publicly notified. Emergency maintenance is handled case-by-case and requires post-hoc validation. Traffic bursts are normalized using a baseline hour-of-week model (we compute the 95th percentile of historical traffic for matched hour-of-week and scale thresholds accordingly). Dev has the full normalization algorithm attached.

- Service credit formula (high level): credits are computed monthly as a percentage of the affected month's service fees, capped per contract. Example worked numbers are in the workbook.

Hi Anika — thanks for the follow-ups. Below is the detailed, contract-ready language you can drop into the RFP. I've included worked examples and edge-case rules.

1) Telemetry & measurement points
- Primary availability measurement: ingress gateway (edge load balancer) response success/failure at 1s granularity. A request is considered "successful" if a 2xx/3xx HTTP status is returned to the client within the configured timeout.
- Secondary telemetry: model-serving (worker) success/failure and per-token generation latency for internal diagnostics; used for root-cause but not for published uptime percentage unless otherwise contracted.
- Data sources: LB access logs (ingress), internal request traces (sampled), and aggregated metrics from the serving runtime. All measurements are retained in tamper-evident storage for 12 months by default.

2) Latency SLO calculation
- Metric: p99 end-to-end request latency (ingress -> response completion) computed on successful first-attempt requests only. Retries initiated by client or SDK are excluded.
- Windowing: rolling 30-day window by default; alternative windows (7/90 day) are configurable via contract.
- Caching: if a route explicitly enables prefix/KV caching, cached response latencies are annotated and can be excluded from the p99 calculation per the SLO definition.

3) Availability definitions and counting rules
- Downtime (counts toward availability failure) is defined as ingress-level request failure (non-2xx/3xx) for a continuous bucket >= 1 minute, measured in 1-second resolution buckets. Transient spikes shorter than 60s are not considered sustained downtime unless they cause the monthly availability percentage to drop below the contracted SLO when aggregated.
- Application-level errors (model runtime exceptions) are recorded and may be excluded from availability if an upstream network failure is shown not to be the causal factor; all such exclusions require joint review and evidence.

4) Traffic normalization & DDoS handling
- Scheduled maintenance: up to 4 hours/month (default) are excluded when scheduled >=48 hours in advance via the console. Additional scheduled maintenance may be excluded by mutual written agreement.
- Emergency maintenance: excluded only after a post-incident review and documentation; excluded duration is limited and requires evidence.
- Burst normalization: we compute an hour-of-week baseline from trailing 90 days of traffic. For any 1-hour analysis bucket, if observed traffic > baseline_95th(hour_of_week) * 1.25, we apply a normalization factor that scales the observed failure counts back to the baseline level before computing availability. This is intended to avoid penalizing customers for legitimate traffic surges that exceed historical norms.
- DDoS & malicious traffic: if traffic is labeled as malicious by either Redwood's automated protection (DDoS scrubbing) or by a mutually accepted third-party mitigator, those labeled requests are excluded from availability calculations. Labeling and exclusion require an audit trail (scrub logs) and must be agreed in writing within the post-incident report.

5) Clock synchronization & audit evidence
- We run NTP with verified stratum configurations across our control and data plane; for latency-critical zones we additionally use PTP where available. All telemetry events are stamped with UTC and include a signed hash for tamper-evident retention. We can provide sample log extracts in JSONL or CSV for one month on request.

6) Service credit formula (contract language + example)
- Contract text (plain): "If monthly availability for the Service falls below the Contracted Availability (A_target), Customer will be eligible for a Service Credit equal to: min(Max_Credit, Max_Credit * ((A_target - A_observed) / A_target))."
- Example: A_target = 99.5% uptime; Max_Credit = 10% of monthly fees. If A_observed = 98.0% then Credit = 10% * ((99.5 - 98.0) / 99.5) = 10% * (1.5 / 99.5) ≈ 0.151% of monthly fees (example numbers are illustrative; workbook contains monthly-billing examples).

7) Evidence artifacts we provide
- One-month audit extract (CSV/JSONL) containing: timestamp, request_id, ingress_status, latency_ms, region, route_id, label_flags (cache_hit, ddos_label), signed_hash.
- Measurement diagram (measurement-diagram.png) showing ingress/serving instrumentation points.
