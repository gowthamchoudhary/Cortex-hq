Samira/Olga —

I dug into the numbers and added a couple of practical adjustments from an ops perspective. Key points below (I attached a quick calc excerpt into the sheet):

1) Tokens/sec baseline per GPU: 120 t/s is our measured steady-state for RedOpt-13B on A100-80GB equiv with continuous batching and a mix of sequence lengths. However:
   - For p95 <= 250ms at 320 tokens: we need to bound batch sizes and introduce QoS (priority queues). Effective t/s drops to ~95 t/s to keep tails short under multi-tenant load and background maintenance windows.

2) Revised GPU count (conservative):
   - Peak tokens/sec required = 350 * 320 = 112,000 t/s.
   - Safety-adjusted per-GPU = 95 t/s → GPUs = 112,000 / 95 ≈ 1,179.
   - Add 18% operational reserve (for canary/cold-cache, scheduling jitter, rolling upgrades) → total ≈ 1,394 GPUs.

3) Slab proposal & phasing (practical):
   - Phase 1 (initial commit, 6 mo): reserve 6 slabs (6 * 64 = 384 GPUs) — covers base and short-term peaks with burst capacity from shared pools.
   - Phase 2 (expand, +6–12 mo): add 8 slabs (512 GPUs) after validated canary and first 90d usage patterns — brings committed total to 896 GPUs.
   - On full steady-state (post-12 mo) convert burst to committed / add +7 slabs to reach conservative target ~1,392 GPUs.

4) Canary plan (24h): we can run in staging with the same model/config: two runs — a) diurnal replay (24h) b) focused 10-min spike at 400 qps. We'll instrument KV cache hit rates, p50/p95/p99 latencies, and packet-level timing. Requires a short IP/CIDR allowlist and a staging API key.

5) Cost/latency tradeoffs:
   - If Veracity can accept a p95 SLA of 300ms (instead of 250ms) we can operate at ~110 t/s per GPU, reducing eventual committed capacity by ~10–12% (savings on committed GPU count).
   - Quant changes (to int4) could double t/s but need repro/QA on hallucination/quality — not recommended for initial commit.
