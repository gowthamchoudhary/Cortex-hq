Camila / Hannah — quick tech summary for the customer follow-up:

What we saw (prelim):
- Control plane scheduler mis-evaluated a burst policy during a short spike, delaying allocation of additional GPUPool capacity by ~90s.
- Simultaneously, KV cache pressure on a subset of nodes increased tokenization latency for some concurrent requests, which made certain requests hit enqueue paths.
- Net effect: higher p95 for ~25 minutes with several requests experiencing timeouts at the application layer.

Actions taken (done):
- Manual autoscale override to force immediate capacity slices active
- Targeted KV eviction on 3 nodes to clear cache pressure
- Rolling restart of the affected scheduling agent (to clear a stale policy evaluation state)
- Increased short-term retroactive capacity for the pool (3 nodes) to avoid regressing if a second spike occurs

Next steps (in progress):
- INF-2483 created (https://redwood.atlassian.net/browse/INF-2483) to track root cause and a fix to the scheduler evaluation logic
- We will add a guard in the scheduler that fails open to the burst policy when enqueue rates exceed threshold, to prevent allocation stalls
- SRE to run a 72-hour soak to ensure no recurrence in us-east; monitoring dashboards updated with alert thresholds
