Sam / team — detailed technical tidbits below so legal can slot in wording:

1) Storage topography / geo-locks:
 - Tenant-level geo-tagging: data and metadata are pinned to the specified cloud region (EU-west-1, us-east-1, etc.).
 - Backups: nightly encrypted snapshots are stored in the same region by default; cross-region replication is opt-in and requires an explicit change request + audit log.
 - DR copies: If a customer requests cross-region DR, we require a documented business justification and sign-off; otherwise no replication.

2) Retention defaults and categories (we'll attach retention-matrix.xlsx):
 - Primary conversation payloads (user-visible content): default TTL 90 days (configurable 0-3650 days).
 - Model telemetry (inference logs, perf counters): default 180 days; aggregated telemetry retained longer for product analytics but is pseudonymized.
 - Ephemeral KV / session cache: TTL default 1 hour; not persisted to backups.
 - Backups/snapshots: full snapshots retained 90 days by default; incremental snapshots retained 30 days. Archived snapshot storage is available with custom SLA and retention.

3) Proof of deletion workflow:
 - Primary: customer requests an erasure; we run a tenant-scoped purge, truncate indexes, and run a validation pass. Certificate: delivered within 45 days including a checklist of objects removed and audit log references.
 - Snapshots/backups: backup deletion has a separate pipeline. For capacity/consistency reasons we mark snapshot objects for garbage collection and run the deletion job in the next retention cycle. We can provide a deletion manifest referencing snapshot object IDs and the GC job run id.
 - Edge cases: backups already exported off-platform (customer pull) are out of Redwood's ability to delete; we note that in the DPA.

4) Legal hold mechanics:
 - We flip tenant retention to "hold" flag; this prevents GC and snapshot pruning. We provide a hold token and ticket number; release only after an authorized release notice.

Sam — following Ben's technical notes, proposed legal compromise language below for §4.2 (redline-ready):

"Data Residency and Backups: Redwood will store Customer Data in the geographic region(s) elected by the Customer at onboarding. Unless the Customer provides prior written authorization, Redwood will not replicate Customer Data or Customer Backups to regions outside of the elected geographic region(s). In the event Customer requests cross-region replication for disaster recovery, such replication will be performed only after Customer provides a documented request and written authorization specifying target regions, retention settings, and any applicable export controls."

Deletion certification (proposed):
"Upon Customer request for erasure, Redwood will: (i) execute a scoped purge of the Customer's primary storage and indexes, (ii) initiate deletion of associated incremental and archived snapshots subject to applicable retention windows and technical constraints, and (iii) provide a written Deletion Certificate within forty-five (45) days describing the objects targeted, the deletion job identifiers, and links to corresponding audit-log entries. For backups that have been exported by Customer or otherwise placed outside Redwood's control, Redwood will provide reasonable assistance but cannot certify deletion of such external copies."

Legal hold (proposed):
"If Customer issues a lawful hold, Redwood will pause deletion and GC workflows for the affected Customer environment and provide confirmation of hold initiation within three (3) business days. Redwood will resume normal deletion workflows only upon receipt of an authorized release notice from Customer."
