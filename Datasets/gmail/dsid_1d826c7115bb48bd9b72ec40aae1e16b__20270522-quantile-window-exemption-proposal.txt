Alex — thanks for the clear ask and the template. Draft proposal below; attaching a PDF with formal wording and an example measurement run. High level summary first, then details.

Summary (proposal):
- P50 target: 80ms measured as a rolling 30-second quantile (captures steady-state user-facing latency).
- P95 target: 450ms measured as a 5-minute sliding-window quantile, aggregated into hourly buckets for reporting (this reduces noise from transitory bursts).
- Exception carve-outs: scheduled maintenance windows (pre-notified >24h), scheduled large batch imports (customer-initiated, max 2/hr windows), and operator-triggered model cold-starts (documented in diagnostics).
- Remedies: for monthly breach beyond agreed threshold (more than 1% of hourly buckets in month exceeding p95 target), Redwood proposes a billing credit of 2% of the monthly invoice for that calendar month and a joint post-mortem.

Measurement details (brief):
- Telemetry: request-level latencies measured at the ingress proxy (front-door) with RFC3339 timestamps and a sampling of full traces (1/1000) for root cause.
- Quantile computation: streaming t-digest per window to avoid heavy storage; we will publish hourly rollups as CSV with timestamped buckets.
- Reporting: hourly CSVs retained for 90 days and exported to a shared Drive link for audit.

Next steps: if this aligns, Marissa (legal/product) will proposer final contract language. Ben can attach the measurement example and a small script you can run to reproduce the bucketed reporting.

Alex / Neha — technical note and reproducibility script attached. A couple of implementation points we wanted to flag:

- The 30s rolling quantile uses a micro-bucket approach (six 5s sub-windows) to reduce jitter for p50. That helps represent UI latency during interactive sessions while still being resilient to 1–2s blips.
- For p95, the 5-minute sliding window is computed using t-digest with max compression 100; we then materialize the hourly aggregate as the max of all five-minute quantiles within that hour (this biases slightly toward penalizing sustained elevated latency rather than single-sample outliers).
- Cold-start detection: we annotate requests where model container up-time < 30s; those are eligible for discretionary carve-out review if they correlate with autoscaling events.
