Hi Samantha — appreciate the clear asks. High-level answers up front, with attachments and an internal note to the team below.

- Recommendation: hybrid approach (automated detection + operator-confirmed cutover). Automated health checks and traffic-steering are used to detect and stage failovers; final production cut is operator-confirmed during business-hours incidents or for high-impact customers. For lower-severity events we can enable safe-automated fallback policies.

- Sequencing (short): 1) automated detection & alerting -> 2) staging (prefix cache freeze, KV sync checks) -> 3) canary traffic reroute to standby region -> 4) operator confirmation -> 5) global DNS cutover (TTL-aware) or load-balancer promotion -> 6) verification & telemetry validation -> 7) rollback or finalize. Attached: concise YAML playbook snippets and a PDF rehearsal artifact.

- Rehearsals: quarterly full failover rehearsals recommended (off-hours weekend blocks, typical maintenance window 3-6 hours depending on dataset), monthly smoke drills (15–45 mins). We include a dry-run mode that exercises routing and KV cache warming without taking production traffic where possible; when true cutover is required we schedule with a 4-week notice.

Samantha — thanks for the prompt. Per Ben's note we've added explicit technical thresholds and a short RPO table to the PDF; summary below and full artifacts are attached/linked.

Key clarifications:
- Health checks and hysteresis: p99 latency > 2.5s sustained for 3 consecutive windows (60s windows) or error rate > 2% for 2 minutes triggers automated staging. KV replication lag > 5s (measured by ack latency) will prevent automatic final cut until sync completes; operator must acknowledge. Node-level OOM or sustained GPU memory pressure triggers immediate evacuation to staging cluster.

- DNS vs LB promotion: preferred approach is load-balancer promotion for immediate regional failover when customer requires sub-2min RTO for control-plane calls. For large-edge populations we combine LB promotion with DNS TTL 60s. Document explains tradeoffs and rollback flow.

- RTO / RPO: Typical configured RTO target in our Dedicated/Private offering is 5–30 minutes for control-plane operations (can be as low as 2 min with pre-warmed standby) and 15–60 minutes for full data-plane warmup depending on model size and KV footprint. Worst-case achievable RPO is 0–5s for hot KV sync (synchronous ack mode) and up to 15 minutes for eventual-sync policies; we recommend synchronous mode for regulated workloads.

- Rehearsals & artifacts: Quarterly full failovers (3–6 hr windows) + monthly smoke drills; we provide a rehearsal report template and recorded telemetry used for postmortems (included in the PDF).
