Megan — thanks for the clear brief and the traffic profile. High level recommendations first, then a compact pricing/throughput matrix attached.

Summary recommendations:
- Baseline commit: L40S-focused baseline + small H100 headroom. L40S gives the best perf/$ for medium-length sequences and distilled models; it’s significantly cheaper per-hour and has good FP16 inference characteristics.
- Memory-sensitive workers: reserve a small H100 pool for the 60GB+ kv model (we suggest 6–8 H100s depending on replication and hot-cache needs). H100s offer ~1.9–2.2x perf on large-context workloads vs A100 but cost ~35–45% more per-hour at list price.
- A100s: avoid as primary unless you need the specific memory-sized A100 80GB configuration at a lower price point; for your mix they don’t hit the perf/$ sweet spot vs the other two.
- Hybrid approach: commit to a baseline of 40 L40S equivalents for steady state and 8 H100s as reserved burst capacity. Add a 20% monthly burst credit pool to cover short spikes; autoscale policies route first to cached L40S instances, then to H100s for cache-miss heavy or large-context requests.

Throughput & perf/$ notes (ballpark, normalized):
- L40S (48GB-class): baseline perf metric = 1.0 perf-unit, list hourly price = $1.0 (normalized). Best perf/$ for medium models and high qps when requests are cache-friendly.
- A100 (40GB/80GB): perf-unit = ~1.1–1.3 vs L40S for many models, but memory/kv handling falls behind H100. Cost per hour often similar to or slightly higher than L40S in our vendor pool; perf/$ is marginal.
- H100 (80GB): perf-unit = ~2.0 vs L40S for large-context transformer runs; list hourly price = ~1.4x–1.5x L40S. For memory-heavy sessions it’s substantially better, but for your distilled model traffic it’s overkill.

Reserve commit structure we recommend:
- 12-month committed baseline (monthly billing): 40 L40S-equivalent capacity with a 28% discount vs hosted list (this covers steady-state qps).
- Dedicated H100 block: 8 H100 reserved with 22% discount.
- Burst credits: equivalent of 8 L40S for short bursts (replenished monthly); additional bursts billed at reduced overage rates.
- SLA: 99.5% pool availability with automatic region fallback and a throughput credit of up to 8% of monthly committed capacity for missed SLO windows (formalized in contract).

I attached a quick pricing matrix and an example cost-run for your current weekly profile. Key trade-offs you should validate internally:
1) How often those 60GB+ context queries occur (they drive the H100 sizing).
2) Whether you can quantize some models (INT8 / FP16 mixed) to reduce memory and push more work on L40S.
3) Appetite for multi-year commit (we can improve discounts materially for 24–36 month).

Looping you all in — got questions from procurement around discount cadence and burst economics. My suggested variant to present: an elasticity-first bundle that reduces committed baseline by 25% but adds a predictable burst bank and a 10% lower H100 block to make headroom cheaper.

Sketch: 
- Baseline: 30 L40S-equivalents committed (12 months) at 32% discount.
- Headroom: 12 H100s on a 12-month reserved (20% discount) + a burst bank sized to cover 1.5x baseline for 4 peak hours/month.
- Overage: reduced overage rate for any additional usage during spikes (we invoice at a blended effective rate).

Rationale: Atlas cares about peak economics more than always-on. If we shift to a smaller baseline and larger burst bank we can offer better effective costs during big events while reducing their on-the-book committed spend. We should model two scenarios: Behavior A (spikes < 10% of monthly time) and Behavior B (spikes > 35% of monthly time) to show sensitivity.
