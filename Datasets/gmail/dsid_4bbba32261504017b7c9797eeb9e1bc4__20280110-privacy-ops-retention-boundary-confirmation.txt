Hi Emma — thanks for the clear list. Short answer: Redwood does not use customer prompts, uploads, or outputs to train or fine-tune our production models for our hosted service. Below is a concise statement you can include, followed by a brief breakdown of retention nuances. Happy to jump on a call if you want deeper technical detail.

Concise statement (suitable for questionnaires):
"Redwood Inference does not use customer prompts, uploads, or generated outputs from customer workloads to train, fine-tune, or improve our production models. Customer data may be processed for serving, caching, and short-term debugging, but is excluded from model training pipelines and training datasets unless explicit, documented consent is provided for a separate data-sharing agreement."

Retention & nuance bullets: 
- Inference logs (request/response): default retention 30 days for hosted accounts; can be configured shorter via account settings or enterprise contracts. Logs are used for billing, metrics, and anomaly detection.
- KV/prefix cache: ephemeral in-memory caches persist only for active instances and are evicted based on LRU and workload; we do not materialize cache contents into training datasets.
- Debugging traces/sampled captures: we occasionally capture redacted samples when customers opt into support captures. Captures are redacted, stored for a maximum of 90 days unless a customer agrees to extended retention for troubleshooting.
- Backups/snapshots: backup retention depends on the deployment mode — hosted backups are retained for 90 days for disaster recovery; Dedicated and Private deployments follow customer-configured policies (on-prem backups remain under customer control).
- Private deployments (VPC/on-prem): by default, no data leaves the customer environment; training is not performed by Redwood unless contractually agreed.
- Edge cases: the only way customer content enters training pipelines is via an explicit, documented opt-in agreement (separate from standard terms). Internal research experiments never use identifiable customer content without legal approval and customer consent.
