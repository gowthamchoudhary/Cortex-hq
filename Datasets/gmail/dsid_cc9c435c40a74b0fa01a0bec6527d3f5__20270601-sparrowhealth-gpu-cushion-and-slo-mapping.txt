Quick assumptions we used for the draft:
- Planning model: rw-13b-v1-q4 (production-quality tradeoff between latency and quality). We also show a fallback path with rw-3b-q4 for heavy bursts.
- Avg tokens per response: 128
- Target p95 latency: 1.5s for full response (streaming end-to-end)
- Peak concurrency for planning: 120 concurrent streams

Raw throughput math (simplified):
- Required tokens/sec to meet p95: (concurrency * avg_tokens) / p95_seconds = (120 * 128) / 1.5 = 10,240 tokens/sec

Model throughput (empirical runtime profiles):
- rw-13b-v1-q4: ~400 tokens/sec per GPU (single-stream, conservative with minimal batching)
- rw-3b-q4: ~1,600 tokens/sec per GPU (handles more streams concurrently due to smaller memory footprint)

Sizing options:
- If committed model = rw-13b-v1-q4: raw GPUs needed = ceil(10,240 / 400) = 26 GPUs.
  - Operational recommendation: commit 18 GPUs (predictable baseline) + 2x burst buffer (automated burst to 36 GPUs) to cover spikes and maintenance windows. That yields ~14,400 t/s burst capacity which gives ~1.4x headroom above target.
- If committed model = rw-3b-q4 (lower-cost option): raw GPUs needed = ceil(10,240 / 1600) = 7 GPUs.
  - Operational recommendation: commit 8 GPUs + burst to 16 GPUs (2x cushion).

Tradeoffs + notes:
- Quality: rw-13b gives better clinical-safe completions; rw-3b is cheaper but we recommend a fallback policy that routes lower-risk queries to 3B and clinical-critical queries to 13B.
- Cache/prefix reuse: enabling prefix caching for common triage prompts can cut effective tokens generated by ~10–25% depending on UX — we'll show sensitivity in the spreadsheet.
- Batching: continuous batching can increase measured tokens/sec per GPU, but it can also increase p95 for single-stream requests. We're sizing conservatively with minimal batching to preserve latency SLO.

Folks — attaching the detailed spreadsheet and a short ops note (PDF) that walks through the math I used. Highlights and operational recommendations below.

Detailed sensitivity (spreadsheet attached):
- Baseline scenario (no caching, minimal batching):
  - rw-13b-v1-q4: 26 GPUs needed to meet raw t/s requirement (10,240 t/s). With a 1.25x safety factor for maintenance and noisy neighbors, recommend commit = 32 GPUs.
  - rw-3b-q4: 7 GPUs raw; recommend commit = 10 GPUs with autoscale to 20 for bursts.
- Cache sensitivity: 15% effective token reduction reduces required GPUs by ~15% for both models. E.g., rw-13b goes from 26 -> ~22 raw GPUs.
- Warmup/steady-state: pre-warming KV caches prior to expected peak window (we can orchestrate a scheduled pre-warm) reduces the p95 delta during ramp and allows lower burst buffers.

Recommendation matrix (what to include in the contract):
1) Preferred (quality-first): Commit 24 GPUs (rw-13b) with guaranteed burst to 36 GPUs for 3 monthly windows. Implement request-route policy: clinical-critical routing to 13B pool; all other traffic to 3B or cached responses.
2) Cost-first: Commit 10 GPUs (rw-3b) + autoscale to 20 GPUs with a policy to promote to 13B on timed canary for critical hours.

Operational guardrails:
- Set max concurrency per pod to avoid tail latency spikes; enable short-lived prefetched KV caches for the triage prompt set.
- Implement a graceful fallback policy: when 13B pool is saturated, route low-risk queries to 3B and surface truncated previews to users while full generation queues (if clinical safety allows).
