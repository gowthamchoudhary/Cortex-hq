Team — quick diagnostic notes after ingesting the logs and an initial trace:

What we see
- Chunks are emitted by the runtime (token generation traces show steady output).
- Packets carrying chunk frames are observed by our egress proxies but for affected sessions there's a long pause before the downstream ACKs arrive. TCP-level retransmits are minimal; the socket remains ESTABLISHED.

Suspected root cause
- There's evidence of a broken interaction between client-side proxy buffer management and our chunked transfer framing: some proxies group small frames into larger TCP segments but fail to flush immediately when application-level chunks arrive, producing client-side "quiet windows" despite an open socket. This behavior is exacerbated by intermediate CDN or proxy keepalive settings that aggressively coalesce writes.

Immediate mitigations (we can implement now)
1) Temporarily enforce a streaming-to-poll fallback flag for SprintWave (we can flip it from our console within 5 minutes). This will avoid the problem entirely at the cost of higher token latency.
2) Add periodic 1-byte heartbeats on streamed responses (we can deploy a small runtime change as a hotfix to push keepalive frames every 10s while we fully fix behavior).
3) Work with SprintWave to adjust their proxy TCP_NODELAY / flush frequency — Elena's team indicated they control the edge fleet.
