Diego / Lauren —

Reviewed Catalyst's redlines. Below is proposed language we can offer as a compromise; it keeps Redwood's broad limitation of liability but carve-outs for confidentiality breaches and trade secret misappropriation are carved out from the cap. Suggested wording (to insert into Section 7 / Limitation of Liability):

"Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this Agreement, the limitation of liability set forth in Section 7 shall not apply to (a) either party's breach of its confidentiality obligations under this Agreement, including the unauthorized disclosure or use of the other party's Confidential Information; or (b) any claims arising from the willful misappropriation or theft of the other party's trade secrets. For the avoidance of doubt, the foregoing carve-outs shall apply to claims for actual direct damages arising from such conduct; consequential, special or punitive damages shall remain excluded to the fullest extent permitted by law."

Notes / risk assessment:
- Trade-secret carve-outs are commonly requested. Customers typically want an unlimited carve-out; we can accept carve-outs that preserve the "direct damages" limitation but exclude those categories from the monetary cap. That mitigates exposure while meeting the customer's ask.
- If Catalyst insists on a truly unlimited remedy (no cap and no quantification) we should either: (i) require proof of insurance that covers trade-secret claims (cyber/e&o/topside?) or (ii) negotiate a very high sub-cap (eg. $10-25M) tied to account size.
- Also recommend we add a reciprocal obligation: Redwood's confidentiality breaches should be treated analogously. Avoid one-sided carve-outs.
