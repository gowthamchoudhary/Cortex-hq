Thanks for the quick clarifications — very helpful. I updated the worksheet and added a short economics annex to show reserved vs on-demand deltas across corridors. Summary below (full numbers in sheet/Drive link):

Corridor recommendations (US-first):
- Baseline (steady): A100-40GB reserved pool — good cost-performance for mixed batch workloads, predictable tail at typical sequence lengths.
- Corridor (sustained peaks 06:00–10:00 PT): H100-80 reserved pool sized to absorb 70% of your peak Q1. H100 gives stronger single-request throughput and lower p95 for long sequences, which maps to your binding SLA.
- Burst (short spikes): small cluster of A10/Ryzen-class preemptible instances for cost-effective burst when queues exceed the corridor threshold.

Tail-latency guidance:
- H100-80: expected p95 improvement of ~15–30% vs A100 for long-context generation in our benchmarks at your token lengths (table in sheet).
- A100-80: slightly higher p95 but lower reserved price; choose if cost is primary and your prompt patterns are shorter on average.

Quantization & precision:
- We recommend INT8-like strategies for embedding-heavy routes (no quality hit in sem search).
- For generation-critical routes, try Q4_0 mixed precision on A100 for a 10–18% cost saving with negligible subjective degradation; H100 supports a more aggressive Q4_0+ pathway in our internal tests.

Phased reservation lane (proposed):
- Phase 1 (Q1): Reserve 40% of projected peak on A100 baseline + 30% corridor H100 capacity (trial run to validate tail).
- Phase 2 (Q2–Q3): Increase corridor fill to 70% of peak, reduce burst reliance.
- Exit clause: 60-day review with rollback option for any corridor band that exceeds latency budget or cost thresholds.
