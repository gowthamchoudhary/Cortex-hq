Karthik / team — I reviewed the redline. High-level legal concerns:

- 99.995% uptime is materially stricter than our standard 99.9% for hosted. That increases exposure for us (more frequent/sizeable credits) and operational burden (additional regional redundancy guarantees).
- The customer’s proposed credit schedule removes our typical aggregate cap language and replaces it with a per-incident formula tied to monthly fees — exposes us to outsized payouts during multi-day incidents.
- The latency P95 <= 80ms is achievable in certain regions with dedicated capacity, but not guaranteed across mixed hardware. We should only accept a latency SLO if paired with Dedicated (or an explicit hardware/instance profile) and a narrower scope of traffic.

If we were to agree, my recommended counter:
1) Offer 99.99% uptime (not 99.995%) for a 24-month committed Dedicated deployment or a dedicated pool with reserved capacity.
2) Cap annual service credits at 15% of ARR (absolute cap).
3) Explicit exclusions for force majeure, scheduled maintenance (48h notice), and customer-configured integrations that affect availability.
4) Latency SLO: P95 <= 120ms with measurement window and region-limited scope; P95 SLA only applies to requests routed to the dedicated pool.

Legal will need exec signoff on any deviation from our standard cap (we typically do not go below 10% without finance ok). Please include Finance for the commercial tradeoffs.

Folks — quick finance POV and pricing sensitivity analysis:

- Revenue math: $1.8M yr1 ARR (commit). At 35% off list our realized ARR drops to ~ $1.17M/yr. If we additionally accept a 15% annual credit cap, worst-case payout exposure is ~$175k for the year.
- Margin: With reserved capacity and priority routing (Dedicated), our incremental infra cost rises ~15-18% vs standard shared hosting.

Proposed commercial guardrails to mitigate risk:
A) Approve a maximum 25% one-time booking discount (not 35%) tied to: 24-month minimum committed spend and a quarterly usage floor (>=85% of forecasted tokens). If usage falls below floor, discount steps down retroactively.
B) If Legal insists on tighter uptime, cap total annual credits at 15% of ARR (equal to Steph’s upper suggestion), but require a reconciliation clause: credits reduce future invoice amounts only after an operational RCA window and up to the cap.
C) Require a minimum $300k upfront payment to cover initial dedicated capacity provisioning.

If Sales wants the higher discount to win the deal, propose we route to exec for CRO/CFO signoff with explicit conditions and a deal-level reserve (escrow/credit hold).
