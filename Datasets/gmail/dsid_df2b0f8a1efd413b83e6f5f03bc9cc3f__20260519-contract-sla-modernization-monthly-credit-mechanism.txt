Sofia — quick operational analysis below.

Summary: moving baseline from 99.9% to 99.95% halves the tolerated monthly downtime (99.9% = ~43.2 minutes/month; 99.95% = ~21.6 minutes). That materially increases frequency of credit triggers for small incidents. With our current multi-region routing + automated fallbacks, most incidents are localized and short-lived; however, incidents that affect KV cache warming or regional capacity (e.g., a noisy spike on a dedicated pool) could easily exceed the 21.6 minute threshold if we don't allow carve-outs.

Proposed operational guardrails we can support:
- Measurement: availability computed at the customer-account-model-route level, aggregated hourly and rolled up to calendar month.
- Remedy formula: credit = (monthly_invoice * downtime_minutes / total_minutes_in_month) * weighting_factor, where weighting_factor = 1 for unplanned downtime, 0.5 for partial degradation (P95 latency breach) — and a monthly cap.
- Cap: For hosted customers on pay-as-you-go we cap at 12.5% monthly invoice. For customers with reserved/dedicated commits (Streamline has a dedicated pool), cap at 20% for the first 90 days of the amendment (pilot), dropping to 10% thereafter. This gives Streamline some protection while limiting Redwood exposure.
- Latency carve-outs: accept scheduled maintenance windows (>=72 hours prior notice for planned rolling maintenance) and post-deploy warm-up window (60 minutes per deployment per pool). For burst windows without notice, require customers to register endpoints as 'high-burst' during onboarding: we then apply a higher remedial threshold for those routes.

Operational notes: we cannot accept a 30% unlimited cap. Also, we should avoid subjective language like 'predictable' — opt for objective triggers (scheduled notice, per-deploy timestamp, request rate thresholds).
