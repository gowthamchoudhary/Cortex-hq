Numbers from the runtime lab (summary):
- Micro-benchmark: 34B int8 on H100 profile -> 7200 tokens/sec per GPU (sustained, prefetched KV cache disabled); with our RPC/IO overlay measured in our stack that maps to ~6200 tokens/sec effective per GPU for a cold request.
- Effective tokens/sec with KV caching: assuming 70% hit rate and cache reduces generated-token compute 40% on hits, weighted effective tokens/sec per GPU becomes ~7900 tokens/sec during steady-state (more subtle math in workbook).

Assumptions and recommended slab math (conservative):
- Peak concurrent requests: 250 sessions * 1.5 rps = 375 rps.
- Average tokens per request = 150 -> peak tokens/sec = 375 * 150 = 56,250 tokens/sec.
- Required GPUs (raw) = ceil(56,250 / 7,900) = 8 GPUs.
- Add 1.5x headroom for sustained peaks and operational safety -> 8 * 1.5 = 12 GPUs (recommend committing 12 GPUs).
- For pilot (3 months) recommend committing 6 GPUs with a 6 GPU burst pool (on-demand) to handle traffic spikes above the committed slab.

Latency SLO validation (300ms p95):
- Median per-token compute mapped to latency: at 7,900 tokens/sec per GPU -> average response (150 tokens) compute time = 150 / 7,900 = 0.019s = 19ms compute; adding RPC and tokenizer overhead (~40ms in lab) -> p50 ~60ms.
- p95 tail driven by queuing when slab saturates. With committed 12 GPUs and the peak tokens/sec estimate, utilization at sustained peak is ~56,250 / (12 * 7,900) = 59% — leaves enough headroom to keep p95 under 300ms with standard queuing.

I attached the workbook with detailed rows for varying KV hit rates (50/60/70/80%) and alternate model path (13B quantized) to show sensitivity.
