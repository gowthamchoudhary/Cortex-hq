Samira —

Good questions. I pulled in Karthik (cc) from our infra team and summarized below to make the distinctions concrete. We'll follow up with a short tech workshop if you want deeper verification.

Short answers first:
- Hosted (multi-tenant): best for non-PHI, high-volume routes where cost and time-to-market matter. We offer elevated SLOs and configurable batching but no hardware reservation — noisy-neighbor scenarios are mitigated by autoscaling and request-level throttles, not physical GPU isolation.
- Dedicated: reserved GPU pools for your tenancy. Guarantees on GPU capacity, no neighbor interference at the GPU level, isolated KV caches, and your routes get predictable tail latency because you own the capacity. Recommended if more than ~30% of peak throughput needs guaranteed p95.
- Private (VPC / on-prem): full control over networking and data plane; you manage provisioning or we deploy into your cloud account. Best for PHI routes that must not touch any shared control plane resources.

Karthik's notes (high level):
- Noisy neighbor protections: Dedicated = physical reservation; Hosted = QoS but shared hardware; Private = fully within your environment.
- KV cache and model state: KV cache can be per-customer on Dedicated and Private. On Hosted we use per-request token isolation with shared cache layers (configurable TTLs, but not physically isolated).
- SLO enforcement and rollback: we can attach model-level p95 SLOs to Dedicated capacity and run active latency alarms with automatic fallbacks to lower-cost variants. For Hosted we provide monitoring and retry policies but weaker hard guarantees.

Suggested next actions:
1) If you have 1–3 PHI-critical routes: mark them "private" in the matrix and we'll scope a Private/VPC engagement.
2) If ~3–8 high-priority routes need strong latency predictability: mark those as "dedicated" and we'll propose a capacity profile and SLA.
3) For everything else, mark as "hosted" and we'll provide guidance on batching, caching, and rate limiting to keep cost low.
