Summary of changes based on your feedback:
- Primary region switched to us-east-1; secondary (failover) = us-west-2. We will provision a small warm standby in the secondary region for regional failover.
- Autoscale policy proposal: target concurrency-based scaling with the following knobs: min_pool = 4 VMs (each with 8 x A100-equivalent GPUs), scale_step = +2 VMs, cooldown = 45s, max_pool = 20 VMs. Burst policy: allow short-term burst up to +30% above max_pool for up to 120s using preemptible capacity where available.
- Routing & fallbacks: primary-routing to us-east-1; if queue depth > 200 and estimated wait > 200ms, requests will be routed to the secondary region. Where models are not pinned between regions, we will route to a compatible quantized variant (cost delta ~ 20%).
- Failure modes: 1) capacity exhaustion — automatic cross-region failover to secondary region, 2) degraded model variant — fall back to quantized variant with monitoring and alerting on quality drift, 3) control-plane outage — local cached policy allows continued serving for short intervals, 4) billing/overage control — automated throttle and notify.

Quick infra notes from my end (summary for the walkthrough):

- Cross-region model sync: We'll surface two options — active sync (near real-time replication) vs periodic snapshot sync. Active sync has higher bandwidth and storage cost but reduces cold-starts on failover. Recommend periodic snapshots + on-demand pre-warm for the first 15 minutes after failover.
- Burst economics: preemptible instances for bursts save ~45% cost but come with preemption risk. For critical queries (clinical decisioning) we should reserve non-preemptible capacity. Suggest policy tags so requests can opt into lower-cost burst paths.
- Quotas & throttles: we'll expose a per-route concurrency quota and a global account throttle. We'll preconfigure a conservative throttle at launch and ramp after week 1 based on observed p95.

I'll prepare a short slide (5-6 bullets) for the 30m walkthrough covering: sync options, burst tradeoffs, throttles, monitoring hooks.
