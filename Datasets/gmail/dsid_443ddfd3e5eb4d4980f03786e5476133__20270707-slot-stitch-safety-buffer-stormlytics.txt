Hi Elena, Sean —

I pulled a quick model for both approaches. High-level numbers below (happy to drop into a one-pager):

1) Convert + pack (recommended pilot):
- Current: 40 mixed slots (baseline throughput = 100k tokens/s effective).
- After packing + quantization profile: equivalent throughput = ~122k tokens/s (approx +22% effective).
- Expected monthly committed spend: +3% (small increase to account for packing overhead during peak).
- Expected per-token cost: down ~9% due to higher utilization.
- Risk: minor (compatibility checks for KV cache and batching).

2) Straight add (add raw H100 capacity):
- Add 24 H100 slots (effectively increases capacity by ~60%).
- Expected monthly committed spend: +28% (material uplift).
- Per-token cost: similar or slightly improved depending on utilization.
- Risk: low; operationally simple but more expensive long term.

Recommended rollout:
- Canary (10% traffic) for 14 days with metrics collection (p95 latency, error rate, cost per 1M tokens).
- If stable, ramp to 50% over 48 hours, then full switch after 7 days.
- Auto-rollback thresholds: p95 increase >25% or error rate >0.5% sustained for 2 hours.

Attachments: I exported a short latency projection and cost delta table for both options (attached as a CSV to this thread). Also included a stub of the runway playbook and an internal flagging checklist for the canary.

If StormLytics is sensitive to month-to-month cashflow, the convert+pack approach gets most of the upside at low incremental commit. If you need headroom for sustained higher peaks, straight add is safer but costs more.
