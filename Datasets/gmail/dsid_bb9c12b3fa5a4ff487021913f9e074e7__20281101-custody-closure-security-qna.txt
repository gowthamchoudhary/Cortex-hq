Hi Ellen —

Thanks — quick answers below, and I attached a short PDF with the same points geared for auditor review (pelagos-data-residency-answers.pdf). Ben and Neha are looped in for any technical/contract clarifications.

1) Default location: customer data for the hosted API is colocated in the cloud region that corresponds to the customer’s sign-up preference (we currently offer US (us-west-2), EU (eu-west-1), and APAC (ap-southeast-1)). Under default onboarding we place data in the chosen region on AWS; we also support GCP/Azure marketplace customers—region selection is available during provisioning and can be enforced via account-level lock.

2) Deletion timeline: our policy is designed to remove customer data from primary stores within 24 hours of a verified deletion request, but full removal from backups and asynchronous replicas may take up to 30 days. We document and timestamp each deletion event; short-lived cache entries are purged immediately. The attached PDF includes the stepwise timeline we provide to auditors.

3) Logs & traces: application logs and request-level traces are retained for operational debugging and quality monitoring. By default, request logs that include model inputs are kept for 7 days for debugging, then are either aggregated or redacted depending on the customer’s retention profile. We provide PII-scanning tooling during ingestion and can set stricter defaults (zero retention) for customers who require it.

4) Audit artifacts: we maintain deletion receipts, S3 lifecycle manifests, and an audit log bundle that contains timestamps and storage object ids. We can produce an evidence package (signed) on request — see the KB link below.

Folks —

Adding a more granular engineering view for the packet we’ll send to Pelagos. Quoting Sofia’s note for context: 

> 2) Deletion timeline: our policy is designed to remove customer data from primary stores within 24 hours of a verified deletion request, but full removal from backups and asynchronous replicas may take up to 30 days.

Notes:
- Primary store purge: deletion is a two-step process (mark-for-delete + compaction). Mark-for-delete occurs immediately on confirmation, compaction to physically remove objects runs in the next scheduled maintenance window (within 24 hours in most regions).
- Backups & snapshots: incremental backups are retained for 30 days by default. For snapshot-based backups, object pointers are removed immediately but underlying blocks are zeroed/garbage-collected on the provider cadence — we represent this as up to 30 days to guarantee full irrecoverability.
- Replication: cross-region replicas are deleted after primary deletion is acknowledged; the replica garbage collection process can add latency up to 14 days for edge-case replication queues.
- For incident response and legal holds: we have a documented exception workflow that requires executive sign-off and appears in the deletion receipts; it is rare but necessary.
- PII scanning: we redact high-risk tokens and surface a report to the customer as part of the audit bundle — we do not perform manual inspection without customer consent.
