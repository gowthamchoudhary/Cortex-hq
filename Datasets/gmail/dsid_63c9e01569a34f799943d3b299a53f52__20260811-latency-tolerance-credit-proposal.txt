Hi Alicia / Finline team,

Thanks again for the productive call this morning. Per our discussion, I attached a short draft SLA amendment that tries to balance predictable uptime guarantees with realistic latency windows for noisy batch workloads. High-level summary below so your legal and procurement teams can skim: 

- Baseline uptime (hosted): 99.90% monthly availability
- Baseline uptime (dedicated/pinned capacity): 99.95% monthly availability
- Latency SLO: p95 inference latency target of 300ms for interactive routes (best-effort for heavy-batched endpoints)
- Exception window: scheduled maintenance + 2 hours/day regional burst carveout (detailed in section 3)
- Remedy (monthly): graded credit tiers tied to percentage of monthly invoice (see attachment):
  * availability >= 99.90%: no credit
  * >= 99.50% and < 99.90%: 10% of monthly invoice for affected service
  * >= 98.00% and < 99.50%: 25% of monthly invoice
  * < 98.00%: 50% of monthly invoice (max)

A couple of notes we called out in the draft: credits are the sole financial remedy, regional fallbacks are allowed if capacity is constrained, and we include a 30-day dispute window on incidents and measurements.

I'll leave Marissa and Ben copied — Marissa to confirm ops wording for the exception carveouts, Ben to confirm the credit cap math. If this looks directionally correct please flag any redlines and we'll push a DocuSign-ready version.

Thanks — happy to hop on a quick follow-up if it helps.

Alicia / Vanessa —

Thanks — copying in Ben to confirm the finance side on the cap. On the technical points:

- 99.92% hosted baseline: engineering team flags that moving from 99.90 -> 99.92% increases the expected monthly capacity reserve by ~12% for multi-tenant pools; that's feasible but will change committed throughput (we can either: raise price slightly, or restrict telemetry-heavy routes to dedicated pools). We should present Finline both options.
- Credit cap: Ben, can you propose a conservative cap given the current forecast for DL-4729?
- Measurement appendix: we'll add an Appendix A describing the measuring method. Proposed approach: regionally-scoped p95 samples at the model-inference layer using signed timestamps, 1-minute rolling windows for p95 calculation, with a clock-skew tolerance of ±50ms; we will publish both raw probe and end-user measurement and use the latter for billing only if there's a >5% delta. This will be written into the draft (engineering to sign off).

Using Finline's expected monthly run-rate (from the HubSpot deal) and conservative loss scenarios, a monthly cap at 40% of the monthly invoice gives predictable tail protection for both sides and keeps worst-case exposure below the $200k number Alicia mentioned in 90%+ scenarios.
