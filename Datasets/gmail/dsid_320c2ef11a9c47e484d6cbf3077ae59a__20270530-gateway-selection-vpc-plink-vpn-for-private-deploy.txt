Anita —

Thanks for the diagram and constraints. Quick summary up front: for most Medigene workloads I recommend PrivateLink for control-plane and API traffic (low latency, secure interface endpoints) plus a negotiated S2S IPSec connection for bulk data transfers and large model syncs. VPC peering is attractive for single-region, same-account/link use cases but has routing and transitive limitations that make it brittle for mixed VPC/Dedicated patterns.

Below are concise tradeoffs and practical recommendations (we can expand into a decision memo if you want):

- PrivateLink (Redwood-hosted interface endpoints)
  * Pros: private connectivity without route table changes, supports least-privilege access, easy to expose only the API/control ports. Predictable latency and you avoid exposing your on-prem CIDR to our VPC. Works well for the low-latency RPC paths (token streaming).
  * Cons: per-hour/per-GB endpoint charges (AWS model), not ideal for very high-volume bulk model syncs; requires endpoint service setup and IAM/endpoint policy work.

- Site-to-site VPN (IPSec + BGP)
  * Pros: straightforward for customers who want their traffic to traverse their firewall, supports large bulk transfer, preserves on-prem access controls. Good when you must egress through your perimeter for compliance.
  * Cons: higher latency/jitter vs PrivateLink, possible throughput limits on appliance-based VPNs (watch MTU and fragmentation), operational overhead (BGP/BFD tuning), and NAT traversal complexity depending on customer firewall. We recommend HA VPN (two tunnels across AZs) and optional SD-WAN for better throughput.

- VPC Peering
  * Pros: low-latency L3 routing inside AWS, no endpoint hourly charges.
  * Cons: non-transitive (doesn't naturally scale across many peers), requires route table exposure of customer CIDRs to Redwood VPCs — raises blast-radius and complicates cross-account set-ups. Not our default recommendation for multitenant Dedicated/private control plane mixes.

Practical default for Medigene:
1) API/control traffic: PrivateLink (interface endpoints) to expose only the Redwood API & streaming endpoints.
2) Bulk/model syncs & admin traffic: HA S2S VPN with BGP; enable parallel SCP/s3-multipart uploads over the VPN.
3) Optionally, for single-VPC, high-throughput internal-only deployments, consider peering but only after negotiating route exposure and security review.

Anita — quick numbers to inform your TCO calculation:

- Latency: PrivateLink p95 observed in our Palo Alto/AWS customers is ~12–25 ms (US-west), dependent on on-prem egress & colo fiber. RPC streaming latency typically sits ~15 ms p50, p95 under 30 ms when tunnel-less.
- Throughput & costs: for ~6 TB/month (approx 200 GB/day) expect interface endpoint data processing + data transfer charges — rough ballpark $0.06–0.14/GB depending on region and AWS pricing tiers. VPN egress via site-to-site will incur NAT/egress costs on both sides and likely a lower per-GB vendor charge but may require appliance scaling for peak windows.
- Operational: PrivateLink simplifies firewall rules (allow only endpoint ENIs) and reduces blast radius vs peering. VPN is cheaper at high volumes but carries operational overhead and variable latency.
