Vanessa / Kim — draft answers for the RTO/RPO + failover sections below. These are the operational targets we publish and the engineering guarantees we run to. Put these in the consolidated doc unless you want different wording.

RTO / RPO (by component) — Redwood Inference (published targets):
- Control plane (management API, deployment control): RTO target = 4 hours (full management functionality restored in failover region); RPO = 1 hour (snapshot of config/state replicated).
- Model serving / inference path (stateless request handling): RTO target = 5 minutes (automated traffic cutover to a warm replica region for production-critical endpoints); RPO = near-zero for in-flight tokens (we attempt seamless redirection but in-flight generation may be lost; clients receive a 503 with retry guidance in that window).
- KV/prefix cache and ephemeral GPU state: RTO target = best-effort within 10 minutes to warm cache; RPO = loss accepted (we rehydrate from model weights and cached prefixes as available).
- Telemetry / analytics pipeline: RTO target = 8 hours; RPO = up to 1 hour for buffered telemetry (we use durable buffering to minimize loss).
- Customer-controlled backups (S3 snapshots of persisted customer artifacts): RTO target = 4 hours for restore into a new tenant workspace; RPO = 1 hour (hourly incremental snapshots).

Failover approach (summary):
- We operate cross-region active-passive model serving for most customers: warm replica clusters are kept with pre-loaded models and warmed KV caches depending on tier.
- Automated monitoring triggers region failover for SLO-impacting incidents (based on latency/error thresholds). For other outages, we perform manual planned failover following the DR runbook.
- DNS / traffic cutover: we use weighted DNS + global load balancer; automated failover reduces weight on failed region and shifts to warm region. TTLs are kept low (60s) for critical endpoints. Additionally, clients are encouraged to implement exponential retry; our SDKs include retry guidance and status codes for transparent reconnection.

Evidence / artifacts I can attach:
- prod-architecture-repl-diagram.png (cross-region replication + warm cluster callouts)
- summary of recent failover test (2026-09-21) and outcome (passed, mean cutover 3.8 min) — attached in the consolidated PDF.

Notes / caveats: RTO for inference is best-effort and varies by plan (dedicated pools have lower expected recovery times due to reserved capacity).

Adding the security specifics around backups, encryption, and restore testing so we can include this verbatim in the SIG artifact. Happy to redact low-level ops logs and provide attestations.

Backup & retention
- Primary backups: incremental snapshots of customer data taken hourly and stored in encrypted object storage across multiple availability zones. Full snapshots taken daily.
- Retention policy: default 30 days for incremental + 1 year for weekly full snapshots (configurable by enterprise contracts).
- Encryption: at-rest encryption using AES-256; customer keys are supported via KMS (we integrate with customer-managed KMS or Redwood-managed KMS). For enterprise on-prem deployments, HSM-backed key management is available.

Access & auditing
- Backup access limited to designated SRE and Security roles via role-based access controls (RBAC). All backup/restore actions are logged and retained in audit logs for 12 months. Audit logs are immutable and include operator identity and justification.

Restore and test cadence
- Restore tests: quarterly restore drills for critical customer data in production-similar environments; documented post-mortems are archived.
- Last successful restore test: 2026-09-21, test type: cross-region restore into warm cluster, time-to-restore (to accept traffic): 3.8 minutes median across test replicas.

Compliance notes
- SOC 2 controls cover backup integrity and change control; snapshots and restore procedures are part of our control set.
- We can provide redacted runbook excerpts and a signed attestation of the last DR test for SIG evidence.
