Hi Sarah / team,

Thanks for the clear ask — I suggest the following redline language as an operationally achievable compromise (high level here; I'll attach a marked-up PDF):

Proposed (high-level):
"Upon a Customer's verified deletion request or contract termination, Redwood will: (a) initiate deletion of Customer Data in primary production locations and any active replicas within thirty (30) calendar days; (b) for non-rewritable backups or snapshots maintained for disaster recovery by Redwood or its subprocessors, Redwood will use commercially reasonable efforts to purge or render inaccessible such backups and will provide Customer a written attestation that deletion efforts have been completed within ninety (90) calendar days. In the event of lawful hold, litigation, or regulatory requirement, Redwood shall delay deletion and notify Customer with the applicable basis and estimated timeframe.

Alternative tighter language (if Acme requires):
- Combine the two timelines into a single 60-day window if Acme prefers one SLA, but Redwood will need explicit carveouts for subprocessor-controlled immutable backups and for DR processes that require coordinated rollback with cloud providers.

Operational notes for Acme:
- We can provide an attestation certificate that lists the replica locations and the steps performed (delete API calls, snapshot purge requests, confirmation from subprocessor APIs).
- We cannot guarantee immediate purging of immutable snapshots held by a subprocessor beyond our control; we will, however, escalate with the subprocessor and provide evidence of purge requests and their confirmations.

See attached: marissa_redline_v1.pdf (proposed DPA text) and internal impact notes (redwood_operational_impact_notes.txt).

Quick technical summary from infra:

- Active replicas (running VMs / persistent disks in other zones/regions): we can schedule synchronous delete jobs once the legal/ops ticket is opened; 30 days is workable.
- Cloud provider immutable snapshots / long-term backups: depends on provider SLA — some providers only allow snapshot retention until the next lifecycle truncation and may require up to 90 days to fully purge depending on snapshot chains.
- K/V caches and ephemeral stores: typically cleared within 48-72 hours once the deletion job runs.

Operational suggestion:
1) Create a single SLO sentence for the contract: "Redwood will initiate deletion of all active and replicated copies within 30 days and complete purge of backups and snapshots within 90 days, subject to lawful hold and subprocessor constraints."
2) Add an Appendix describing the purge playbook (steps, what evidence we provide, expected subprocessor confirmation windows). I uploaded a short ops playbook here: https://redwood.internal/ops/geo-rollback-playbook (internal).

We should be explicit about what "attestation" contains (API logs, ticket references, subprocessor confirmations).
