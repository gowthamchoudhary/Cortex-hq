Vanessa / team — initial triage notes below. I attached a trimmed pcap and a CSV with p95/p99 series for 05/17-05/19.

Summary:
- Correlation between elevated TTFT and a ~200-400ms increase in TLS handshake time on a subset of client->edge flows. Handshake time here = time between TCP handshake complete and TLS ClientHello->ServerHello completion for the first TLS flight.
- Affected flows are concentrated in a handful of edge POPs in us-west and us-east, all using the same upstream CDN provider (third-party edge carrier).
- No recent cert rotation in our control plane. No config pushes from infra since 05/15.
- Upstream BGP flaps noted in provider status logs around 05/17 03:00-07:00 UTC that align with the worst spikes.

Hypothesis: intermittent degradation at the upstream edge carrier causing delayed ALPN/TLS processing or queueing before our edge terminators. This increases time-to-first-byte / token because the token generation can't start until the TLS handshake completes and we've received the initial bytes.

Immediate mitigations we can do:
- Reroute affected customer traffic to an alternate POP path (manual policy override) — low-risk, requires short config push and will need coordination with traffic team.
- Offer the customer to route their requests to a dedicated region (Dedicated or Private) while we investigate — may require sales/ops approval for credits.
- Ask customer to enable 2nd-tier client retries with jitter to hide transient handshake delays for non-critical requests.
