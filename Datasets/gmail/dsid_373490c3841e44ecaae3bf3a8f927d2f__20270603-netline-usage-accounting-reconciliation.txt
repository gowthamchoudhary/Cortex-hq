Hi Liam — thanks for the heads up.

Short answer: we use a rolling attribution window aligned to the request arrival time and an internal session-join heuristic that aggregates short-lived sessions under a parent session when we detect rapid client reconnects. That can cause apparent spikes on the route if many reconnects map to the same parent session.

I'll pull the raw session attribution export (CSV) for May 18 and include the transaction_id, route, request_ts, attributed_tokens, and sample_kv for the rows that rolled up to `search-api`. Would you prefer a GDrive link or a compressed CSV in email?

Liam — attaching our export and a short explanation of how the join works. See the attached `redwood_sample_session_logs.gz` (contains the columns you requested plus the join_key we used).

Key summary (short):
- Attribution window: we assign tokens to the route for the parent session whose first seen ts is within a 30s build window. Rapid reconnects from the same client IP + user-agent are merged under a single parent session to avoid double-counting short-lived handshakes.
- What happened on May 18: a client-side upgrade caused a flurry of reconnects from the same client pool. Our merge heuristic considered them the same parent session and rolled the tokens to the `search-api` route where the parent session's first request landed.

What we included: `redwood_session_export_May18.csv` (rows that contributed to the invoice), mapping to your `tx_id` where present.

Next steps proposed:
1) We will correlate our mapping to your `tx_id` in the attached CSV.
2) If there is a mismatch attributable to our merge heuristic, we can propose a one-time pro-rated credit for the over-attributed tokens on May 18.
3) We'll propose a config toggle for Netline accounts to reduce the merge window from 30s to 5s for environments with aggressive reconnect behavior.
