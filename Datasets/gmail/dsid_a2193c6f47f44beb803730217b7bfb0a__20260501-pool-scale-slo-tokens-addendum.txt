Rachel / Tom — thanks for the bandwidth. Based on Tom's traffic numbers and our capacity planning, attached is a draft addendum and a capacity spreadsheet. Key items we propose (high level):

1) Capacity commit
- Redwood will reserve a dedicated pool equivalent to 4x A100 GPUs for Atlas Health starting on the renewal effective date. Pool may be isolated or co-located with existing reserved pools by mutual agreement.

2) Throughput SLO (tokens/sec)
- Sustained throughput guarantee: 150,000 tokens/sec measured as a 5-minute rolling average per reserved pool.
- Burst allowance: up to 200,000 tokens/sec sustained for up to 60 consecutive seconds without being considered an SLO violation. Bursts longer than 60s will be measured against the 5-minute rolling average.

3) Measurement & telemetry
- Throughput will be calculated using the platform token-metering pipeline at the ingress/dispatch point. Metrics are available via the Redwood Console (per-pool token/sec timeseries) and via an ingestable webhook to Atlas's monitoring endpoint.

4) Availability & credits
- SLO target: 99.9% monthly compliance for the Sustained Throughput SLO (measured as percentage of 5-minute windows during the month that meet or exceed sustained throughput).
- Service credit: for each 0.1% below 99.9% in a given month, Atlas will receive a credit equal to 2% of that month's Dedicated reserved pool fee, capped at 40% for a single month. Credits are applied to the next invoice.

5) Escalation & remediation
- If Atlas experiences three consecutive months below 99.9%, Redwood will assign a dedicated SE for remediation and offer a one-time operational burst (subject to capacity) while we implement fixes.

Laura / Rachel — technical confirmation from SE side:
- Measurement point: we'll use the dispatch token counter (pre-KV cache expansion) so reported tokens/sec reflects work the GPUs perform. This avoids inflating numbers when cache hits short-circuit inference.
- The 5-minute rolling average is straightforward and aligns with our existing SLO framework.
- Webhook: we will emit per-pool token/sec datapoints at 1s granularity to Atlas's provided endpoint; we can also stream aggregated 60s buckets to reduce noise.
- PagerDuty: we'll stand up an integration that triggers a high-priority alert if sustained 5-minute average drops below 140k tokens/sec or if bursts exceed provisioned limits for >120s.

Suggested small tweak to legal language for clarity: replace "measured as percentage of 5-minute windows" with "measured as percentage of non-overlapping 5-minute windows in the monthly calendar period" — this prevents ambiguous windowing in edge cases.

I think everything here is implementable without further infra changes. Happy to go to legal when Laura gives the green light.
