Thanks Sam — great questions. High level answers up front, followed by the operational details and a small proposed canary we can run with you next week.

High-level:
- Billing: We bill for tokens consumed up to the point of eviction. For interrupted work we provide two remedies depending on root cause: (1) automatic retry routing (free for first retry within 2 hours) when eviction is due to transient spot reclaim; (2) pro-rated credit in cases where eviction was caused by Redwood-side scheduling misalignment (rare, escalated).
- Credit windows: We have a 24-hour reconciliation window for transient preemptions — during reconciliation we identify eligible interrupted workloads and apply credits to your next invoice as line-item adjustments.
- Escalation: For priority batch jobs we recommend a canary route with pinned shoulder capacity and a documented fallback to a cheaper model variant. If a customer experiences repeated >3% job failure due to preemption in a rolling 7-day window, we trigger an operations review and potential credit.

Operational detail (ops folks cc'd):
- Eviction etiquette: Ephemeral pool nodes are best-effort; we expose an eviction signal (SIGUSR1) and also provide a 30s drain window where possible. Customers should checkpoint at that boundary where possible.
- Burst admission: Burst requests are admitted into an ephemeral pool in FIFO order. We reserve a small headroom buffer for pinned shoulder commitments to avoid cold-start evictions for critical runs.
- Retry routing: The first retry is replayed to the same region and, where feasible, to an identical model/quantization profile. That retry is not billed. Subsequent retries follow standard billing.

Adding ops specifics and a conservative sizing suggestion.

Camila (ops) notes:
- Recommended config for Helio's anticipated peak pattern (from Sam's cron notes):
  - Pinned shoulder slab: 4 x A10-equivalent GPUs as a hot buffer during 00:00-06:00 PST
  - Ephemeral burst pool: capped at additional 12 GPUs; eviction TTL policy set to 15m for that pool
  - Preemption windows: staggered evictions in 30s waves to allow graceful checkpointing; we only do full node reclaim outside 03:00-04:00 PST where Helio runs critical nightly summarize.
- Autoscale policy: continuous batching enabled; scale-up threshold at 75% queue depth, scale-down cooldown of 90s. This limits thrash while still admitting bursts quickly.
- Headroom math: For Helio's 200 concurrent streams peak, the above config yields ~1.2x headroom on median token latency. We recommend starting there for the first 30 days and measuring actual tail latency for SLO adjustments.

Operational caveats:
- If bursts systematically fill >80% of ephemeral pool during scheduled maintenance windows, we will look to negotiate temporary shoulder increases (timeboxed) rather than frequent credits.
- Credits are intended as a safety-net, not a substitute for pinning in contract language for truly critical runs.
