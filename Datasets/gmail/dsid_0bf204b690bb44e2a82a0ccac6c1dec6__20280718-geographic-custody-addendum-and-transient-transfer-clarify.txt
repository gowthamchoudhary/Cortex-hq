Hi Elena — thanks for the clear list. Quick summary of where we stand and next steps:

Summary answers (high-level)
- Primary custody: yes — for your account we can bind inference request routing and storage of ephemeral per-request artifacts to a US region. That means model execution and KV caches will run on US-hosted capacity by default.
- Transient cross-region processing: permitted only for three operational modes — (1) scheduled maintenance (short window, typically <4h), (2) burst failover when US capacity is exhausted (expected window depends on load; usually <2h but can extend), and (3) emergency disaster recovery (longer, documented).
- Temporary reprocessing: we do not, by default, export queued raw request payloads to another region for persistent storage. Retries/replays may touch ephemeral compute in another region for a short period; we can limit that to <24h and require scope-limited KMS access.

Irene / Marissa / Elena — technical notes from infra/ops: 

- KV cache / ephemeral artifacts:
  * We can bind cache residency to US regions for a customer ID namespace. Cache TTLs and eviction policies are configurable per account. Default is 6 hours TTL for inference prefixes; we can reduce to 1 hour for Catalyst if requested.
  * During a burst/failover we may instantiate compute in nearby region pools; ephemeral caches there are deleted upon failback. We can guarantee deletion and a 24h maximum persistence for any ephemeral copy, with logs showing creation/deletion timestamps.

- KMS scoping:
  * We support customer-managed keys (CMK) scoped to a region. If Catalyst supplies a US-scoped CMK, keys never leave the US. For platform-managed keys, we can provide attestations of key access events.

- Transient reprocessing / queued replay:
  * By default we do not transfer queued request payloads to a non-US region for persistent storage. However, replay for model debugging may temporarily route a sample to a non-US test cluster for <= 12 hours; this is not enabled without an ops ticket and the customer explicit approval.

Open question for legal/compliance: do we require explicit DPA language that caps all transient hops to <24h per occurrence, or would you accept scenario-based caps (e.g., maintenance <4h, burst <12h, disaster recovery up to 72h with notification)?
