Eve / team — technical summary and proposal below. I'm copying the suggested defaults we use for large async runs and where we can bend for Polaris.

Context: Redwood's hosted batch pipeline treats a single job as the logical unit. For large workloads customers often submit a single job id with internal shards. Our standard completion SLA (customer tier dependent) is written around median completion time under expected load (typically 1–3 hours for tens of millions of tokens across small shards). This workload is atypical because of size and bursty concurrency.

Proposal (technical):
- Completion window: offer a dedicated SLA carveout of 8 hours for the nightly full-run (compromise from Polaris's 12h ask). We consider 8h achievable with reserved dedicated pools and burst capacity.
- Idempotency: require a client-provided job_id and a per-shard idempotency token. Redwood will persist job_id->result mapping for 30 days (configurable) to prevent duplicate billing and duplicated outputs. On re-ingestion we will dedupe by job_id first, then by shard token.
- Retries: server-side: exponential backoff with jitter; default max retries per shard = 5. Client-side: recommend a single orchestrator retry with incremental backoff to avoid thundering herd during recovery windows.
- Failure visibility: per-shard status stream (started/processing/succeeded/failed) in the Console and via the Jobs API. We can emit progress percent and ETA estimates.
- Fallback behavior: two options for Polaris to pick in the contract addendum: (A) automatic failover to Polaris pull-mode (Redwood persists partial outputs to an S3 export and notifies Polaris to ingest), or (B) switch execution to a lower-priority shared pool without strict completion guarantees. Option A minimizes duplicate compute on Redwood side; Option B keeps compute in-platform.
- Credits/penalties: for an 8h carveout we would propose a lower credit multiplier than for standard SLA breaches; specifics require procurement sign-off (Marissa).

Operational caveats: enabling the 8h carveout for sustained nightly runs requires Dedicated or Reserved capacity sizing; on Hosted we cannot guarantee predictable completion under high cross-customer contention without reserve. If Polaris wants to avoid reserved capacity, we'd need to document a best-effort mode (no strict SLA).

2) Dev to prepare a simple API contract change: job_id header requirement, shard_token field, and per-job export hook for fallback.
