Forwarding Brightline's profile. Initial take below (notes + recommended pilot mix). I also attached a short playbook (sku_reco_playbook.pdf) with the decision rules.

Notes:
- p99 250ms (clinical) is the strictest constraint and drives per-request latency design. Those routes have fairly short contexts (~2k tokens) but require consistent low tail latency.
- Conversational UX has higher token variance and some long-context spikes (~12% of requests >8k tokens). Those are memory-sensitive.
- Embedding nightly batch is large volume and very cost-sensitive — fine to place on quantized L4s.

Recommendation (pilot allocation):
- H100 (memory-first): 30% of reserved slots — used for long-context spikes (>8k) and any large-batch RL/ft training jobs Brightline runs during off-peak. Rationale: H100's larger HBM and NVLink support provide headroom for checkpoints and long KV-cache.
- A100 (latency-first): 40% of reserved slots — pin for clinical inference routes that require p99 250ms. A100 has strong fp16 perf at tight latency with predictable PCIe topology.
- L4 (cost-first, quantized): 30% of reserved slots — for embeddings and bulk async inference. Use quantized 4-bit models with caching and batching.

Autoscale + fallthrough: configure burst-to-hosted policy for spikes outside reserved capacity (billing-visible) and fallthrough to a slightly larger L4-backed pool for degraded but available inference (lower quality). Also recommend a prefix cache for popular prompts to reduce H100/A100 tail demand.

Estimated committed capacity (back-of-envelope):
- Using Brightline's peak RPS and token mix, a 240-slot reserved pool with the pilot mix above should meet p99 targets with ~15% headroom. That maps to roughly 17,280 GPU-hours/month (assuming 24/7). We can provide a more precise tokens/hr -> GPU-hour estimate once Aditya runs microbenchmarks on the exact model variants they plan to use.
