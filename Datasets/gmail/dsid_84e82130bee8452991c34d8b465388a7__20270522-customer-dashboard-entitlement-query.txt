Hi Alice — adding a few technical specifics that often come up in procurement/architecture reviews.

Metrics we surface in Console and via API:
- Request success rate (1m/1h/24h rollups), failure categories (timeout, model error, validation error).
- Latency percentiles (p50, p90, p95, p99) for both cold+hot path; we annotate p99 spikes with concurrent-load context.
- Throughput: tokens/sec and requests/sec per model/route.
- KV-cache hit ratio and effective latency delta when KV hits occur.
- Resource telemetry: GPU utilization, queue-depth, instance restarts (for incident correlation).

How measurements are computed:
- Percentiles use t-digest sketch with 1m bucket rollups; contractual p95/p99 are recalculated from the full 30-day rolling t-digest to avoid windowing artifacts.
- Availability is computed at the region level and then weighted for multi-region contracts based on configured traffic percentages.

Alerting and runbooks:
- Default alert set: availability < 99.5% over 1h (P1), p95 latency > SLO + 40% over 10m (P1/P2 depending on customer tier).
- Alerts go to our alerting pipeline (PagerDuty, Opsgenie integration) and create an incident with a pre-populated runbook. We provide a read-only incident timeline in the Console for customers to review post-incident.

Temporary exceptions and mitigations:
- For planned maintenance we support pre-approved exception windows up to 8 hours per month, recorded in the portal. Anything beyond that requires an SLA amendment (DocuSign).
- For short-term traffic surges or unexpected errors customers can request an ad-hoc exception. If approved, we credit the impacted billing period per the contract schedule or apply a patch rollback.

Data retention & proofs:
- Raw telemetry retained for 90 days; aggregated (hourly/daily) retained 2 years. We can provide signed CSV extracts for audit requests.
