Sara — thanks for the detailed notes and the attachments. Helpful. High level responses below; I'll follow up with a short playbook and proposed wording.

1) Territory split: We can support per-customer routing (EU vs US) without an MSA exception if the Order Form includes a binding locality table and you accept the subprocessor list for each region. The key is documenting the routing policy and whether Clearwave requires data residency guarantees (e.g., no cross-border fallbacks). If you require no fallbacks, that becomes an Order Form SLO/exception.

2) Joint controller: That changes a few items in the DPA exhibits — primarily the clause that assigns purposes and lawful bases. We typically add a short exhibit (DPA Exhibit C) that enumerates joint-controller operations and responsibility split. That will also require updating the SCCs section to reflect the controller-controller references in clause 3.

Sara — quick operational clarity from my side (copied Jonas for technical specifics):

- We can route at tenant or customer-id level. Routing by tenant is simpler operationally; customer-id routing requires request metadata and tagging but is supported.
- Fallback behavior: by default we allow cross-region fallback for resilience; marking a customer as "no-fallback" will pin their traffic and require capacity reservation (Dedicated or private). That impacts pricing.

Jonas — could you sketch a table with columns: Customer_Alias | Preferred_Region | Routing_Level (tenant/customer) | Fallback (allowed/no) | CapacityImpact (low/medium/high) — we’ll attach in the Order Form.
