Looping in a short technical plan we can share with Acme ops. I propose we break verification into two phases: connectivity checks and perf validation.

Phase A — connectivity & baseline:
- Allowlist IPs: we will provide a small set of ingress IPs for traceroute/tcp-connect and an egress IP range if we initiate connections (we prefer allowlist-inbound to a private proxy inside their VPC).
- Tests: ICMP traceroute (where permitted), TCP connect latency sampling to our endpoint port, and an MTU probe.
- Success criteria: median connect latency to edge < 25 ms from their gateway, no MTU fragmentation on the path.

Phase B — perf SLO verification:
- Load profile: 1 minute sustained ramp to 400 req/s with 512-token typical payloads, repeated 3x with 2-minute cool downs.
- Concurrency check: 80 simultaneous streaming sessions for 5 minutes at ~1–2 tokens/sec per session (to model typical streaming sessions).
- Latency targets: P50 < 40 ms, P95 < 120 ms, P99 < 220 ms (end-to-end measured at client after TLS/TCP handshake).
- Instrumentation: capture per-request timing (client-first-byte, model-first-token, total completion), and KV cache hit rates if enabled.

Operational notes for the Acme team to be aware of:
- If they plan to terminate TLS at a LB inside their VPC and forward plaintext to Redwood's private agent, we need to coordinate cipher suites and handshake reuse to keep handshake overhead low.
- For IPSec fallback, ensure NAT traversal (NAT-T) is enabled and log the peer MTU during handshake.
