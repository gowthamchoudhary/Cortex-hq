Sarah — thanks for the clear list. Draft answers below (high level) plus attached capacity workbook and a network topology sketch. Will follow with a concise mutual action plan after we sync internally.

Summary (high level recommendations)
- Slab sizing: recommend a 40-GPU slab (A100-equivalent) as the initial committed unit for regulated customers; this gives strong isolation and predictable amortization for pre-warm/caches.
- Sustained throughput: ~110k–130k tokens/min per slab depending on model/quantization; for conservative planning use 110k tokens/min.
- Latency: p95 ~120–220ms (single-turn) at sustained load; p99 can push up to ~400ms under tail events unless admission control is applied.
- Burst handling: we operate a warm-buffer pool + short-duration burst lanes. For 2x bursts we recommend a 15–20% warm-buffer capacity and a 5–10s queueing window before shedding.
- Graceful degradation modes (in order): 1) short queue with ETA, 2) model fallback to a smaller variant (quantized or distilled), 3) rate-limit with explicit 429 and retry-after, 4) cross-region fallback to hosted Redwood with reduced privacy guarantees (opt-in).

Operational knobs we propose for your runbook
- Admission gate thresholds: at 75% slab concurrency begin request queuing, at 90% trigger pre-warm and enable model-fallback policy.
- Queue policy: FIFO with per-tenant priority tiers; max queue time 8s for latency-sensitive lanes, 20s for batch lanes.
- Cache warmup: recommend a 10–15min warmup window after slab activation for common prefixes; we provide a warmup API and tooling.
- Network: expect TLS overhead ~1–2ms per connection at scale; recommend keep-alive and connection pooling on clients.

Attachments: capacity_calcs_v3.xlsx (token math + slab burn-rate scenarios), network_topology_v2.pdf (peering & egress notes).
