Folks — forwarding Erin's note and my reply. TL;DR: Stratus wants private pool sizing for a pilot: peak ~2k r/min, target p95 150ms, 60% kv-hit.

Quick capacity math / operational notes (for our sizing exercise):
- Traffic units: 2,000 req/min = ~33 rps peak; but bursts are very spiky (they said short 5-min surges).
- Convert to GPU throughput: with avg 512 tokens and our A100 perf vector (approx 90 tokens/sec per GPU for mixed batch patterns), one GPU can handle roughly ~1.5–2.0 rps at target latency without batching. With batching (1.6x), effective rps/GPU ~= 2.7–3.2.
- Warm cache effect: 60% kv-hit reduces decode time ~35%; factor that into per-GPU capacity to bump throughput ~20–25%.
- Safety margin: aim for 30% headroom at p95 to absorb jitter and network egress.

Preliminary recommendation: baseline 6 A100-equivalent GPUs for pilot reserve, with immediate autoscale triggers to add 2 GPUs at 400 rps sustained windows and +4 GPUs for bursts > 600 rps. Rationale: we biased up from the 4-GPU conservative note because of the burst behavior and our desire to avoid fallback.

Operational runbook items to include in MAP:
- Warmup job: seed most common prefixes and priming queries 30m before peak windows.
- Cache metrics: track kv-hit, cold-start rate; trigger warm pool if cold-start rate > 7% over 10m.
- Circuit breakers: maintain a short-term request queue and fail fast after 3s queue time; prefer degraded model routing over dropping.
- Network checklist: ensure private egress through their NAT with 10 Gbps headroom per sub-pool; verify MTU and peer routing for sustained bursts to avoid fragmentation.

Questions for infra: what are our quantized-model fallbacks latency-penalties? Also confirm warm pool spin-up time on their on-prem infra (target < 120s).

Will refine after we ingest their sample traffic.
