Hi Caitlin —

Appreciate the context. Attached is a concise options packet (ND-dedicated-options-v1.pdf) plus a pricing/onboarding worksheet (gpu-onboarding-worksheet.xlsx). Below is a summarized set of commitment bands and an example 6-month ramp choreography that aligns to the performance profile you described.

Summary (three commitment bands):

- Band Alpha (Cost-conscious starter)
  - Cluster: 8x A100 (or equivalent)
  - Minimum commit: 8 GPUs (soft floor)
  - Target steady throughput: ~40–70 rps short-chat; limited overnight batch windows
  - Recommended use: early pilot, small burst credits for test spikes
  - Typical unit rate: higher per-token compared to larger bands

- Band Beta (Balanced production)
  - Cluster: 32x A100 / mix H100 for peak lanes
  - Minimum commit: 32 GPUs (hard floor for SLA-backed lanes)
  - Target steady throughput: ~120–220 rps; overnight embedding concurrency supported
  - Recommended use: initial production with predictable SLOs
  - Notes: includes 20% warm-cache buffer to reduce cold-start token cost

- Band Gamma (Throughput-optimized)
  - Cluster: 128x H100 or 96x H100 + 32x cheaper-quantized lanes
  - Minimum commit: 96–128 GPUs (negotiated)
  - Target steady throughput: 400+ rps; large batch/embedding windows
  - Recommended use: sustained high-volume products; best unit economics

GPU floor and activation mechanics:
- We define two floor types: soft floor (flexible, can drop with limited notice) and hard floor (contracted, SLA-backed). Soft floors are charged on consumed hours but come with guaranteed spot-preemption protection; hard floors are billed as committed capacity.
- Burst lanes: every band includes an option for transient burst capacity (monthly burst pool or credits). If Nebula needs a 72-hour spike we can provision burst lanes in-region; credits get applied first then metered usage.
- Warm-up credits: to smooth onboarding, we offer a 2–4 week warm-up credit that offsets early overage while KV caches fill and batched workloads stabilize.

Example 6-month ramp (milestones mapped to commitment increases):
- Month 0 (contract execution): Provision Band Alpha with 8 GPUs; enable telemetry and shared tracing.
- Month 1: Baseline optimization, KV warm-cache, pilot traffic → target 50% of production traffic.
- Month 2: Move to Band Beta soft floor (32 GPUs) as month-on-month throughput target met; enable reserved lanes for critical routes.
- Month 4: Convert 32 GPUs hard floor to committed + add 10–15% buffer for peak days.
- Month 6: Optional conversion to Band Gamma or step-up to 64–96 GPUs depending on adoption (we can do staged increases every 4 weeks).

Technical and pricing tradeoffs (high level):
- Smaller floors reduce committed spend but increase headroom risk and per-token costs; ideal for proof-of-concept.
- Larger commitments require lead time but give ~30–45% unit cost improvement and priority capacity.
- Choice of GPU (A100 vs H100) affects both latency & cost; we can architect mixed fleets so latency-sensitive routes use premium H100 lanes and cheaper quantized lanes handle batch embeds.

Hi Caitlin — looping in a quick technical note ahead of the call.

- Staged tranches: We can support 2x16 GPU tranches under the same commercial commitment; billing can be aggregated into a single invoice cadence. Operationally we map tranches to node pools so you get clear telemetry per tranche.
- Mixed-cluster pattern you suggested (24 low-latency + 8 quantized) is fine. We recommend:
  - Low-latency lanes on H100-backed instances for critical chat routes (reduces 95/99 latency by ~15–25% vs A100 for long context).
  - Quantized lanes (int8/int4) on cheaper accelerators for embeddings/batch jobs. These lanes share a KV-cache invalidation policy to avoid cold-token penalties.
