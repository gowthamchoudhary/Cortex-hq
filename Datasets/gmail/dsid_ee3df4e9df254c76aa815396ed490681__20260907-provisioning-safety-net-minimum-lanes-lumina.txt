Thanks Ben — reviewed the forecast quickly and sketched a configuration that maps to our runtime economics. I uploaded a short draft (redwood_dedicated_proposal_lumina_v1.pdf). Key assumptions: 95th pct request size = 512 tokens; average realtime concurrency target = 60 qps sustained during business hours; permissive burst window for scheduled jobs (batch enrichment) is 1 hour/day.

Recommended (my view):
- Minimum GPUs (guaranteed): 6 GPUs with warm KV caching across 2 nodes (3 GPUs each). Rationale: keeps token latency under 120ms p50 for the 512-token tail while maintaining cache hit rates.
- Safety net / minimum lanes: reserve an extra 2 GPUs as an isolated "safety lane" (hot spare) to absorb node failures and sudden cold-cache spikes. This is billed as reserved but can be set to scale policies (only used on outage or scheduled bursts).
- Burst policy: Keep an elastic burst pool of +6 GPUs in the same region for predictable bursts; prefer short-lived spot-backed capacity for non-critical batch.
- Canary approach: 1 node (4 GPUs) in the customer's VPC for canary validation and smoke tests; roll traffic 10%/30%/100% over 48 hours.
- Ramp cadence: Week 0: provision canary; Week 1–2: validate and warm caches with shadow traffic; Week 3–5: staged ramp to baseline (add reserved lanes + apply autoscale); Week 6: full live cutover and metrics handoff to Lumina SRE.

Operational notes: KV cache priming is the biggest lever to reduce steady-state GPUs. If Lumina commits to a pre-warm window (i.e., 24–48 hours before peak campaign), we can drop the minimum guaranteed GPUs by ~20%. Also recommending a 3-month incremental review to re-calibrate quantization settings once load is stable.

Next action items for Sonia: confirm the business-hours concurrency and whether scheduled batch jobs will be staggered. Also loop in procurement for contract terms and whether Lumina requires a hard SLA for burst availability.
