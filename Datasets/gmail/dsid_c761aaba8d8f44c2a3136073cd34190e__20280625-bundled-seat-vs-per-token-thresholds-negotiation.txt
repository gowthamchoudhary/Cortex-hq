Hi Kevin — quick answers and two example scenarios below. Attached: redwood-pricing-matrix.xlsx and a PDF with the arithmetic examples.

Short answers:
- Allocation model: seat bundles carry an aggregate monthly token allowance scoped to the customer/organization (not pinned to individuals). If you also run host-level per-token routes (e.g., API endpoints billed per token), we reconcile consumption at the org level each billing period and consume from the bundle first, then apply per-token overage rates.
- Rounding rules: we round at the smallest billing unit, which for tokens is per-API-call token consumption aggregated per minute window for streaming and per-request for non-streaming. After aggregation we apply the billing quantum (1,000-token increments) for invoicing. Practically: per-call numbers are summed into a rolling minute bucket and then rounded to the nearest 1,000 tokens.
- KV-cache and prefix cache: cache hits are deducted before metering; a 100% cache hit results in no token charge. Partial streaming chunks are measured by the tokens delivered server-side (what the runtime emits) and are included in the same minute aggregation.

Examples (for contract text):
Scenario A — Bundled-preferred consumption
- Bundle: 20 seats, 500k tokens/seat = 10,000,000 bundled tokens/month.
- Usage: month-to-date consumed = 10,250,400 tokens across all routes.
- Metering: we apply minute aggregation and 1,000-token quantum rounding. The 10,250,400 -> rounded to 10,250,000 after nearest 1,000 rule. The bundle covers that; no overage charge.

Scenario B — Mixed routes, small excess
- Bundle same as above. External per-token route (ad-hoc API) consumes an extra 45,900 tokens during a burst. Aggregation rounding: 45,900 -> 46,000 (1,000-quantum up). The bundle covers first 10,000,000; excess 250,400 consumes 250,400 of the bundle; the 46,000 ad-hoc rounded amount creates an apparent excess only if total rounded consumption exceeds the bundle. Overages billed at agreed overage rate.

Negotiation note: We can offer a concession for SOWs — a 100k monthly floor that prevents micro-overage charges under that floor; above that floor normal rounding/quantum applies. If you want that in the SOW, we will mark it as a one-line concession and reflect it in the pricing matrix.

Kevin — quick clarifications inline, and suggested SOW clause at the bottom.

1) Rounding direction: Redwood's billing defaults to nearest-quantum rounding (>=500 rounds up). We avoid systematic upward bias; the minute aggregation plus nearest-quantum prevents per-call micro-billing. That said, for enterprise SOWs we can switch to 'floor-to-quantum' for the first 100k tokens each month (i.e., all rounded-down during the floor) so invoice exposure is conservative on your side for small overages.

2) Floor scope: applies per-billing-account. If you require it to apply across subsidiaries under a single master account, we can implement it as an org-level floor in our billing topology — we'll need to map your subsidiaries into a single billing account at purchase.

Proposed SOW language (suggestion you can paste):
"Redwood will apply a monthly aggregation floor of 100,000 tokens at the organization billing-account level. Consumption will be aggregated according to Redwood's standard metering (minute aggregation; 1,000-token quantum). For the first 100,000 tokens each month, Redwood will floor consumption to the nearest 1,000 tokens downwards (no overage will be billed within this floor). After the floor is exhausted, consumption will be billed using standard nearest-quantum rounding as defined in Redwood's pricing matrix."

If this looks acceptable I can drop it into the SOW and reflect the concession in the pricing appendix. Rafael — please confirm the operational changes we need to capture (account mapping + billing flags).
