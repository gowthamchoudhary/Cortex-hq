Quick update from runtime/infra:
- We confirmed a rapid drain in Orion's Dedicated GPU pool starting 07:40 PDT; one of their node groups had a scheduled kernel patch that kicked off concurrently with higher-than-normal seq-lengths from their jobs.
- Autoscaler observed pressure and made scale-up requests at 07:42 and 07:46, but several of the new instances failed health checks due to an initialization OOM in the model loader (large LLAMA variant + long sequence warmups). Those 1st-wave failures produced 5xx for the in-flight requests.
- Retry amplification: Orion’s client retries every 200ms for up to 5 attempts by default; because new instances were returning 5xx while the autoscaler was still provisioning, we observed a retry storm that pushed queue depth higher and caused the autoscaler to thrash.

Concrete actions executed (08:10–08:30 PDT):
1) Applied emergency burst: enabled Dedicated burst-capacity for Orion (temporary) and added 4 warm spare GPUs to their pool (expected stabilizing effect within 2–4 minutes).
2) Applied gateway-level short-circuit: temporarily reject identical repeat requests with 429+Retry-After if they exceed a short client-side hash window (to break the retry spiral).
3) Fallback routing: routing rule added to send non-critical routes to a smaller quantized variant (redwood-quant-small) hosted pool for graceful degradation of latency.
4) Reduced per-worker concurrency for affected model to reduce the chance of model-loader OOMs while new nodes finish warmup.

Next steps:
- I’m collecting the autoscaler event timeline + instance boot logs and will attach a condensed log bundle.
- Recommend we work with Orion to increase client-side backoff to 500ms, and to consider a jittered exponential backoff to avoid synchronized retries.
