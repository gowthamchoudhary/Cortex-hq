Thanks for the captures, Oliver — I looked through the pcap and a few things jump out immediately:

Quick findings:
- I see MSS negotiation around 1460 but multiple packets are being retransmitted with DF set and no ICMP fragmentation-needed messages visible in-path — that often points at MTU blackholing across an off-path tunnel/NAT.
- There are a handful of flows where SYNs return from different source IPs, which suggests asymmetric return path (NAT hairpin or SNAT on a different hop).
- We also see occasional SYN -> SYN/ACK -> delayed ACK patterns that align with the ~45s stall windows.

Oliver — appreciate the quick verification. This confirms MTU/path-MTU blackholing + SNAT asymmetry are both in play. Two parallel items we should address:

1) Immediate mitigation (low-risk): enable MSS clamping on the NAT/firewall and/or add an iptables rule on the app hosts to clamp MSS (we tested -M 1200 and it removed stalls). That fixes the symptom.

We need the transit team to ensure symmetric SNAT or preserve conntrack state across the NAT pool.

Thanks for the context. A few security/ops notes:

- If asymmetric SNAT makes flows appear to originate from unrecognized egress addresses, some firewall policies will drop stateful entries after a timeout or reclassify packets for deep inspection which increases latency — that matches your 45s stall behavior.
- From a policy standpoint we can relax inspection for the peer subnet (ExampleCorp peering range) for TCP ports 443/8443 to reduce stateful timeouts; this needs an approved change and audit entry.
- As an immediate low-risk change I support clamping MSS on the NAT (or host iptables if you prefer) and then re-evaluate once SNAT symmetry is restored.

Action items:
- Oliver / ExampleCorp: please confirm when your transit team can perform symmetric SNAT or pin SNAT addresses for the peering path.
- ExampleCorp: please apply the host-level iptables clamp as temp mitigation (we'll provide exact commands).
