Hi Sam — thanks for flagging this. Short answer up front: for AWS Marketplace purchases the invoice is issued by AWS (AWS Marketplace Digital Software) and typically shows AWS as the biller while listing the seller (Redwood Inference) in the line item. That means:

- VAT/tax treatment: For EU B2B buyers, reverse-charge can apply, but your VAT team needs to rely on the marketplace invoice showing a B2B sale plus your VAT ID. If the AWS invoice does not clearly indicate seller VAT or B2B status, we can request a seller settlement report and provide our VAT ID as supporting evidence.
- Legal entity variants: Depending on the marketplace and region, the seller name can be one of: "Redwood Inference, Inc." (US entity) or a local subsidiary/partner name if we list through a regional entity. For our AWS listing we are the seller as "Redwood Inference, Inc." (US) — we do not currently issue the primary invoice on AWS in most regions.
- W-9 / W-8: Because we are a U.S. company, we can provide a completed W-9 for your records if your tax team requires it to prove seller status for US-sourced charges. For non-US withholding concerns, we can provide W-8BEN-E if a non-US entity is involved — but in this listing our seller entity is US-based so W-9 is the typical doc.

Actionable items I can do right now:
1) Share a one-page marketplace-billing-guide that outlines AWS vs Azure vs GCP invoice responsibilities (attached: marketplace-billing-guide-AWS-Azure-GCP.pdf).
2) Provide a W-9 and a short seller statement confirming the seller name on our AWS listing is "Redwood Inference, Inc." and our US EIN. I will attach the W-9 template and a pre-filled statement.
3) If CloudRetail needs a seller invoice (a separate invoice from Redwood) for accounting, we will open a request with Marketplace Partner Ops to see if a seller-issued invoice or settlement report can be provided for the July charge — this sometimes requires buyer permission or a marketplace support ticket.

Notes on reverse-charge:
- Reverse-charge requires: buyer VAT ID, evidence that sale is B2B, and in some countries a seller VAT ID on the invoice. Since we are a US seller, we generally cannot provide an EU VAT number; that means many EU customers rely on the marketplace invoice + their VAT ID and use reverse-charge. If your tax team needs a seller VAT number specifically, we may need to explore if a local reseller/subsidiary invoice is possible (this is a longer conversation).
