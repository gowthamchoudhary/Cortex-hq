Thanks again for taking the demo this morning. Per our conversation, I wanted to put a short, concrete recap on the pricing questions we walked through so your procurement and engineering teams have a single reference. Fast read below + next steps at the bottom.

1) What we bill for (high level)
- Billed unit: tokens. Redwood bills generation and context tokens processed by the model.
- Requests: API calls (requests) are not billed as a meter by itself, but they matter for concurrency/throughput planning and may affect cost indirectly through higher required capacity.
- Characters: we show characters in some analytics dashboards to help you estimate client-side text sizes, but characters are not the billing primitive.

2) How tokens map to characters (rough guidance)
- English average: ~4 tokens per 15 characters (roughly 3–5 characters per token depending on language and punctuation).
- Practical rule: multiply token estimate by ~4 to get characters; we’ll provide a short conversion table.

3) Prompt caching vs billed tokens
- Cache behavior: if a request hits our prompt-prefix cache and we can reuse cached KV state, you still see a billed reduction because fewer decode tokens are generated.
- Important nuance: the billing system charges for the tokens actually processed by the model. If a full prompt is sent but the runtime short-circuits because of an exact cache hit (no model decode), billed tokens can be zero for the decode portion; however request-level overhead, routing, and any pre/post processing are not billed as tokens.
- Example: a 1,200-token prompt with an exact cache hit for the first 1,000 tokens that returns a cached continuation for the next 200 — billed tokens reflect only the model work performed. We’ll provide a worked example for Avery’s sample traffic.

4) Unit economics you asked about ($/1M tokens target)
- You mentioned a target of $X per 1M tokens for model inference during the call. We discussed multiple levers: model family (open vs tuned), quantization profile, and batching.
- For your current usage profile (high concurrency, many short calls), batching has limited upside; prefix KV caching and smaller model variants are the biggest levers.

5) Reporting & transparency
- We provide token-level breakdowns by route and by token type (prompt vs completion).
- We can export a one-week sample CSV keyed by request_id so your billing team can map to internal logs.

Quick note on caching nuance:
- Redwood supports two main cache modes relevant to your pattern:
  * Exact prompt-level caching (fastest, highest hit-rate when you have repeated identical prompts)
  * Prefix-aware KV caching: this allows reuse of internal KV state for common prefixes (i.e., the stable header) and only decodes the variable suffix. This often yields ~50–80% decoded-token reduction depending on how much of the prompt is stable.
