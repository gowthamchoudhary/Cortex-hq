Hi Olivia — great to see the forecast, thanks. Quick read and some initial thoughts: we can absolutely avoid a single large invoice by smoothing top-ups across the quarter and issuing monthly pro-rated credits tied to usage thresholds.

Two practical options to consider (short sketches):

- Option A — Smoothing + Tiered Buckets: You purchase an interim top-up that’s split across 3 months. If actual usage exceeds the forecasted tier in any month, the overage is billed at a reduced overage rate (tiered). This gives you predictable invoices and lower marginal costs during spikes.

- Option B — Weighted Discount Retainer: Commit to a $X monthly retainer (lower than full uplift) and we apply a weighted discount curve for incremental tokens. The discount increases once you pass 70% / 90% thresholds. This preserves predictability while giving progressive unit economics.

Olivia — thanks for the forecast, this is straightforward to model. A couple of pragmatic guardrails we recommend:

1) Establish a 3-month smoothing window tied to the forecast with a reconciliation at month-end 3. Any under-consumption converts to a carry-forward credit; over-consumption gets billed at the tiered overage rate.

2) Weighted discount: we suggest these breakpoints (example): 0–69% forecasted usage = base rate, 70–89% = 10% discount, 90%+ = 18% discount. We can customize the breakpoints to meet your procurement targets.

3) If co-terming is required, we can align the amendment to your existing renewal date and amortize the remaining months of uplift into the new term so there’s no double-billing.
