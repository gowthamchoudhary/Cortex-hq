Samantha — thanks for the clear questions and the attachment. TL;DR: standard PO terms and approvals are fairly predictable, but we can make short-term pilot exceptions with some guardrails. High-level answers below and a suggested next step.

1) Term options
- Standard: 12-month and 36-month private offers (most common).
- Pilot exception: we can do 6-month private offers in special cases (pilot + conversion plan). Pricing for 6-month pilots is typically at a higher effective rate (we do not apply the same volume discount you’d see on a 12-month PO). We generally treat 6-month as a time-limited subscription with a ~=10–20% premium depending on usage profile and seat/throughput commitments.
- Rationale: shorter terms undermine forecasting and our Marketplace pricing floor; a premium offsets additional scheduling and billing overhead.

2) Buyer approvals and signoffs (typical US practice)
- <$50k ARR / one-off: self-service or Sales Ops signoff; minimal Marketplace paperwork.
- $50k–$250k ARR: requires Sales lead + Finance approval; Redwood Legal may review standard SLA/terms if there are custom data or indemnity asks.
- >$250k ARR or any multi-region deployment: requires Legal signoff + PartnerOps (Marketplace team) involvement. AWS co-sell on offers above this band usually requires additional AWS buyer validation and a separate seller-guided PO flow.

3) AWS-specific notes
- If the buyer wants to route through AWS Discounting/Private Offer approvals (co-sell), AWS will typically ask for a seller-provided pricing justification and deal registration; this can add 3–7 business days.
- For buyers in regulated sectors we sometimes need an extra compliance attestation before listing the PO (e.g., data residency constraints).

Samantha — adding a couple of practical constraints from PartnerOps / Marketplace standpoint so you have a single source of truth:

- Approval lane: in practice, if your expected spend converts to a >$200k ARR commitment, we must run the offer past our Marketplace compliance team and AWS co-sell approvals. That adds an internal 2–4 business day review window on our side.
- Doc flow: for 6-month pilots we push for a PO + a conversion MOU that outlines how pricing would be handled on conversion. Saves time at renewal.
