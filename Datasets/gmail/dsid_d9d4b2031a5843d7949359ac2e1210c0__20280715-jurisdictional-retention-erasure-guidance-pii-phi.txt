Summary answers:
- Per-jurisdiction retention: Yes. In Redwood Private you can map retention policies to data partitions (region/country tags). We support per-bucket lifecycle rules plus application-level records indexing that drives selective erasure.
- Deletion guarantees: We delete from persistent object stores (customer-managed or Redwood-managed) and purge KV caches; we provide an erase confirmation and audit trail. - Redaction vs tokenization: We offer both patterns. Automated redaction (regex + ML PII detector) removes or masks tokens at ingest; tokenization/pseudonymization replaces identifiers with reversible tokens (if you keep the mapping store) or irreversible hashes. Trade-offs: redaction can reduce model utility for downstream NLP; tokenization can preserve some linking but creates a secondary sensitive mapping that requires its own retention controls.

Hi Anita/Vanessa — quick technical breakdown and constraints.

1) Per-jurisdiction retention and enforcement
- How we implement: Redwood Private supports metadata tagging at ingest (country, facility, data classification). Retention policies are attached to those tags. The platform enforces object lifecycle rules (S3 lifecycle or equivalent), a metadata index that drives selective deletion, and a post-delete verification step that checks object manifests and KV caches.
- Backups/snaps: Important caveat — snapshot/backups outside the immediate object store (e.g., periodic backups, cross-region replication) must also be included in the retention/removal plan. We can:
  - Disable cross-region replication for tagged PHI buckets, or
  - Apply the same lifecycle policies to replicated copies, and
  - Provide a documented purge job for known snapshots.

2) Transient runtime caches and model KV caches
- We purge KV caches after request completion according to configurable retention windows. For strict deletion requirements we can set the KV retention to 0 or only retain encrypted tokens. Note: zero-KV retention can increase cold-start latency for generation-heavy workloads.

3) Redaction vs tokenization trade-offs
- Redaction (masking/removal): Pros: reduces re-identification risk, simpler to audit. Cons: may remove signal that certain models rely on (e.g., named-entity co-reference), which can degrade model outputs.
- Pseudonymization/tokenization: Pros: preserves structure for downstream analytics; reversible tokens allow re-linking. Cons: mapping store is a sensitive asset and needs its own retention + access controls (HSM-backed keys recommended).

4) Evidence & auditability
- We can produce an audit package with timeline of delete operations, manifest hashes, and signed confirmations. For SOC2/ISO audits we can attach logs; we include an example SOC2 evidence stub in the attachments.

5) Legal hold and exceptions
- If MedixCare ever issues a legal hold, deletions can be paused for the hold scope. That means our deletion confirmation will show a pending exception for specific record ids. Legal holds are an explicit exception in the delete workflow and must be coordinated via account team + legal.

Recommended defaults for MedixCare pilot:
- Ingest: apply automated PII detection + reversible tokens for patient identifiers for 30 days, then irreversible hash after approval.
- Retention: per-country lifecycle with automated purge at 5 years (Country B) / 7 years (Country A) per your policy.
- Keys: customer-managed KMS for private deployments.
