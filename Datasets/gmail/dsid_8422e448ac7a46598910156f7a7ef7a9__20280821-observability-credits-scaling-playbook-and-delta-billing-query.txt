Hi Nina — thanks for sending the estimate and for the thoughtful questions. Short answer: yes, we do have both operational and contractual approaches to avoid surprise overages. Longer answer below and a proposed next step.

High-level options we offer:

1) Credit smoothing window (recommended for pilots): we apply rolling credits across a configured billing window (30/60/90 days). Short surges are absorbed by credits and amortized so the invoice reflects smoothed usage.
2) Surge cap + alerting: a hard cap that triggers rate-limiting or fallback to a cheaper tracing tier; alerts fire early and we notify both SRE and account teams.
3) Contractual overage buffer: include a committed baseline with a percentage buffer (e.g., baseline + 20%) before any overage billing is applied.

For pilots we commonly combine (1) + (3): a modest committed baseline, a 60-day smoothing window, and one-time pilot credits to cover incident-mode usage. I can share a short playbook that describes configuration, invoicing behavior, and the exact accounting treatment for credits.

Hi Nina — adding the SKU & mechanics detail below so finance can map it to their billing system. I also attached a short SKU summary PDF.

SKU mapping (summary):
- Retention: billed by GB-month retained per retention bucket (hot/warm/cold). We offer commit slabs (e.g., 1TB, 5TB) with tiered unit pricing. Overages above slab are billed at the slab unit rate unless you opt-in to smoothing.
- Tracing ingestion: billed by trace-ingest units (million spans). We provide burst credits for incident windows (configurable amount).
- Advanced dashboards: seat + compute mix (monthly). Dashboards are mostly flat-monthly for licensed seats; heavy dashboard queries count against the trace/ingest quota depending on query type.

Smoothing mechanics (how it looks on the invoice):
- We track daily usage and compute a rolling average over the configured window. If daily usage exceeds baseline, the excess can be absorbed into the smoothing buffer and amortized across the window; the invoice line shows (net billed usage) + a "smoothing adjustment" line.
- If total usage for the window exceeds committed + buffer, the remaining delta shows as an overage line with the agreed overage rate.

Pilot credit proposal (example):
- 60-day pilot: $12k credit applied to observability add-ons.
- 30-day smoothing window default; Opslyte may opt for 60-day smoothing during incidents.
- One-time setup fee waived for pilots that convert to a 12-month committed path.
