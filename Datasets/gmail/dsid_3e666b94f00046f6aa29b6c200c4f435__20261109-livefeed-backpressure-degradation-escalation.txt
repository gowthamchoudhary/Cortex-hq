Thanks for the ping, Priya. Quick summary of what I'm seeing in our ingress logs for EdgeScale:

- There is a small but real increase in 502/connection-reset events on the streaming endpoints originating in us-east-1 over the last 36h.
- Correlated proxy logs show repeated "upstream timed out" and "client prematurely closed connection" around the flush boundaries.
- We see frequent small flushes (many small chunks) followed by a FIN/RST before the expected final chunk.

Hypothesis: under certain workload shapes we're hitting a proxy buffer/flush pathology combined with our dynamic batching. The serving runtime appears to flush partial frames early (to reduce latency), and when paired with a client or proxy that expects a different chunk cadence we end up with a premature closure mid-response.

Immediate mitigations I recommend (can be applied quickly):
1) Traffic manager: create a route override for EdgeScale that sends traffic to the conservative pool (non-streaming endpoint) while we investigate. This preserves correctness at the cost of slightly higher latency.
2) SRE: increase nginx proxy buffer and upstream_keepalive_timeout on the affected ingresses in us-east-1 as a canary.
3) Serving: temporarily disable micro-flush behaviour for the EdgeScale model variant (avoid very-small-chunk flushes).
4) Ops: add server-side keepalive heartbeats to reduce perception of "end of stream" at client parsers.

I'll open a short-lived PR with the buffer change and a serving flag toggle; request SRE to deploy to a single canary host within the hour. If we see the resets drop, expand to the pool.
