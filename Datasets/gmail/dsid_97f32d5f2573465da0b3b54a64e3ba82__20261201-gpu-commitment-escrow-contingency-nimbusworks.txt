Hi Elena — appreciate the clear asks. Attached is a short options memo and an XLS with scenario math (scaling-scenarios.xlsx). Summary below — three discrete, procurement-friendly options with clear triggers and an escrow-style buffer we typically call a "commitment escrow."

Option A — Baseline Soft-Floor (lowest upfront)- Cluster: 8x A100 equivalent (shared lane)- Min guaranteed GPUs: 4 (soft guarantee, 95% availability SLA)- Ramp: month 1 = 40% committed baseline / month 2 = 70% / month 3 = 100%- Escrow: 10% credit held to offset burst fees if usage spikes beyond commit during onboarding- Best for teams optimizing cash and allowing modest headroom

Option B — Anchored Minimum (recommended for production)- Cluster: 12x A100 equivalent (dedicated partition)- Min guaranteed GPUs: 8 (hard guarantee, SLA-backed)- Ramp: month 1 = 60% / month 2 = 85% / month 4 = 100%- Escrow: 5% of monthly commit kept as operational buffer; auto-release after 90 days if no overage- Includes warm-start prewarmed nodes for <50ms cold-start degradation

Option C — High-availability Slate (peak-critical workloads)- Cluster: 20x A100 equivalent (redundant across AZs)- Min guaranteed GPUs: 12 (hard guarantee + hot failover)- Ramp: tranche-based (50% activate at signing, remainder staged 30/60/90 days)- Escrow: 15% credit to cover sudden burst and cross-AZ failover costs- Recommended if you need predictable throughput under spikes and regional redundancy

Technical notes (quick):

- Minimum guaranteed GPUs define billing floor and prewarming policy; autoscale above the floor is allowed but billed per-token or per-GPU as configured.
- Warm-start credits reduce tail latency during ramp periods — we include a measured warmup window to avoid cold-cache penalties.
- For seasonal spikes we recommend a burst pool with explicit burst caps and a priced fallback to compatible quantized model variants.

Adding a couple of operational constraints from infra/serving: 

- Minimums are enforced at the scheduler level as a "reserved pool". If you request a hard 8-GPU minimum, those GPUs are pinned and cannot be used by other tenants. Warm-start behavior happens on the pinned pool and KV caches are preserved across hot restarts.
- If Nimbus expects large sequence-length variance (RAG with long contexts), recommend adding +1 GPU per 4 baseline GPUs to preserve throughput at tail latencies. The XLS shows per-seq-length delta — faster models can reduce footprint but increase cost per token.
- Canary policy: we will include a canary lane (10% of traffic) for any model rollouts for first 14 days, with automatic rollback thresholds if token quality drops by measured delta.
