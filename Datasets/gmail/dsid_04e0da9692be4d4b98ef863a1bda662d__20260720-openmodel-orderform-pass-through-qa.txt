Thanks for the ping — quick read-through attached (RI_suggested_redlines.docx). High-level thoughts:

1) Pass-through indemnity for OSS/model licensors — not acceptable as a blanket pass-through. Recommend:
   - Limit "pass-through" so Redwood will only assume indemnity obligations to the extent Redwood has a contractual indemnity from the upstream licensor or vendor (i.e., we won't absorb claims for third-party license breaches unless we can recover from that third party).
   - If upstream indemnities are unavailable, propose a narrow carve-out: Redwood will be responsible for direct damages caused by Redwood's distribution of a model that late-discovered infringes a third party's IP, but only where Redwood had actual knowledge of the infringement or willful misuse.

2) Damages cap / consequential damages — recommend keeping the customer-facing cap at 12 months of fees with explicit exclusion for statutorily required liabilities and fraud/willful misconduct. For OSS-related license breach, propose capping recoverable direct damages to the lesser of (a) order form fees for preceding 12 months or (b) a fixed sub-cap for OSS violations (suggest $250k) to reduce open-ended exposure.

3) Wording suggestions — included tracked changes for MSA Section 9 (Liability) and added an order-form clause: "Open Model Licensing; Third-Party Dependencies" that:
   - requires customer to identify the upstream vendor and provides an option for Redwood to decline support for particular upstream model versions;
   - disclaims indirect/consequential damages;
   - ties any pass-through indemnity to actual contractual rights against upstream vendor.

Note: We should expect CloudRetail procurement to push back on any per-claim OSS sub-cap. If they do, recommend we trade the sub-cap for a requirement that CloudRetail maintain upstream indemnities from their vendors and list them on the order form.

Maria/Team — quick recommendation from legal ops after reviewing both options:

- We should offer both options but push for Option B as the default. Option B avoids open-ended pass-through exposure while giving the customer concrete remediation certainty.
- If customer insists on Option A, add the following guardrails: (i) pass-through only applies where Customer presents a valid assignable claim against a named upstream vendor; (ii) Redwood's obligations are limited to direct damages and remediation costs and are subject to the Order Form cap; (iii) Redwood may limit support for a given upstream model if we determine (in good faith) that the vendor relationship or license creates unacceptable legal/operational risk.
- Proposed compromise for OSS liability sub-cap: instead of a fixed $250k floor, tie any OSS-specific recovery limit to the lesser of (a) 12 months of order-form fees or (b) $500k for enterprise deals. This gives us predictability while being more palatable to the customer for larger deals.

If procurement accepts Option B, we can sign-off quickly. If they choose Option A, expect back-and-forth on the gate/assignment mechanics.
