Lucy — quick answers, high level:

- Switching the soft-failover from 30 -> 15 minutes increases the effective reserved headroom we must hold for cross-region capacity or accept more frequent model fallbacks. Practically, that shifts the credit trigger: instead of crediting after a sustained 30m regression we’d credit after 15m. That increases credit exposure ~1.6x in our back-of-envelope, because short outages become billable sooner.

- Queueing priority: we can support per-tenant route priority and preemption rules in the reserved pool. Implementation outline: per-tenant priority tag, preemption thresholds, and a small dedicated fast lane for 'real-time' tokens. This is what the queueing-diagram tries to illustrate.

- Emergency burst reservation (20% reserve for patient workflows): yes, we can reserve a protected fraction of the burst bucket, but that reduces the aggregate burst capacity for other workloads and would be reflected in the pricing appendix.

Hi all — technical addendum below (excerpted for thread):

On preemption & KV cache:
- Preemption threshold: we define a tenant eviction threshold at 85% sustained utilization over a 5-minute rolling window. At that point lower-priority jobs are preempted and queued.
- Cache warming: when a request is routed to a fallback model variant, KV cache warming occurs asynchronously. Cold starts for large context pins may add 40–120ms per request for the first N keys; we recommend an optional warm-start prefetch for mission-critical flows.
- Headroom accounting: burst budgets are tracked as token-credits per-minute. Emergency reservation (20%) will be enforced at the token-accounting layer and visible in the Console as a reserved-burst balance.

Operational notes:
- Observability: we'll ship per-route 95/99/99.9 latency metrics to your Grafana instance via the metrics endpoint.
- Credits: crediting will be triggered by sustained 95th percentile latency > agreed threshold for >= 15 minutes (if you accept the 15-minute window). Credits are calculated on per-hour reserved spend.

If Elara signs off on the 15-minute soft-failover we’ll: (a) mark the SLA addendum accordingly, (b) update the pricing appendix for the added headroom reserve, and (c) enable the protected-burst flag on provision.
