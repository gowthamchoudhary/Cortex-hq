Key differences (high level) between hyperscaler reserved VMs and Redwood Dedicated
- Reservation semantics: hyperscalers reserve instance types (VM+GPU) in a region; preemption risk depends on SKU and purchase model. Redwood Dedicated reserves physical GPU capacity inside our Dedicated pool — we guarantee that capacity for the duration of the contract and support live migrations within our pool for hardware maintenance without dropping customer traffic.
- SLA and uptime: our Dedicated SLA covers model-serving availability (we commit to 99.9% per-month for provisioned endpoints) and include automatic fallback routing to a compatible hosted model profile with documented latency degradation steps. Hyperscaler reserved VM SLA is typically for the cloud infrastructure and may not cover application-level inference availability or managed fallback behavior.
- Operational runbook: Redwood provides an ops playbook for on-call routing, KV-cache warm-up, and failover policies. We can run replay tests with KV-cache priming to get realistic p95 numbers. Hyperscalers generally require the customer to manage orchestration for inference reliability; some marketplace partners add orchestration but it varies.
- Cost and predictability: hyperscaler committed discounts can look cheaper for raw GPU hours but may not include the orchestration, caching, and routing features that reduce effective token cost. We’ll include a modeled TCO slide for the 6-week trial.
