Team — I reviewed the logs Maria sent and matched them against our control plane telemetry. Summary:
- Observed service outage window (error responses + dropped requests): 2027-11-02 03:18:00 UTC through 2027-11-02 07:32:00 UTC = 254 minutes of degraded/unavailable service.
- Additional 45 minutes (07:32–08:17 UTC) where API responses were returning 500s < 5% and latency > 2x normal; we treat that as partial degradation for credit weighting.

SLA reference: our standard Hosted SLA (Section 4.2) defines Availability = (total minutes in month - downtime minutes) / total minutes in month. For credit eligibility we use the following banded schedule (monthly availability):
- >= 99.9%: no credit
- 99.0% to 99.9%: credit = 10% of monthly committed fees * (minutes_outage / total_minutes_in_month)
- 95.0% to 99.0%: credit = 25% of monthly committed fees * (minutes_outage / total_minutes_in_month)
- < 95%: credit = 50% of monthly committed fees (capped)

Catalyst's committed monthly spend: $48,000 (from Deal DL-2027-089). Total minutes in November: 30 days = 43,200 minutes. Using conservative approach we treat the 45 minutes partial degradation as 22.5 minutes equivalent. Total credited minutes = 254 + 22.5 = 276.5 minutes.

Preliminary calc: credit = 10% * $48,000 * (276.5 / 43,200) = 0.10 * 48,000 * 0.006403 = $30.74 (rounded).

This seems low because the banded schedule multiplies the minutes proportionally; finance/legal, is there precedent for issuing a flat minimum credit for outages over X hours?
