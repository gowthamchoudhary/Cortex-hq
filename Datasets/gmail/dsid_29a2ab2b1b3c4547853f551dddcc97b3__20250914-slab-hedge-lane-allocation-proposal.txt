Carlos / Jordan / team —

Thanks for the traffic histogram. I ran a quick simulation using the attached spreadsheet (acme_capacity_model_v1.xlsx). Below are the key recommendations and the math we used, expressed as conservative targets so your team has headroom for unknowns: 

Summary recommendation (short):
- Baseline slab: 6 tiles (each tile approximates an A100-equivalent VM) dedicated to the production model.
- Warm spare tiles: 2 tiles kept warm (prefetch + KV cache primed) as a slab hedge.
- Burst pool: 2 flexible tiles that can be multiplexed across tenants; admission control gates these during 250 qps spikes.
- Total committed tiles: 10 (6 baseline + 2 warm + 2 burst).

How we got there (math, simplified):
- Measured per-token cost (in our internal benchmark for your model family): ~0.7ms per token on tile.
- Average tokens/request = 500 tokens total (350 in + 150 out).
- Per-request token processing time = 500 * 0.7ms = 350ms.
- With multi-request multiplexing and batching efficiency of 1.8x at steady state, effective per-request time -> 350ms / 1.8 ≈ 195ms.
- To achieve 90 qps sustained: needed concurrent capacity ≈ 90 * 0.195s ≈ 17.6 concurrent-equivalents.
- Each tile can support ≈ 2.1 concurrent-equivalents under the expected batching patterns -> tiles_needed ≈ 17.6 / 2.1 ≈ 8.4 -> round up to 9.

We choose 6 baseline + 2 warm (cache primed, faster p95) + 1 burst = 9 — but because you want 3-minute 250 qps windows and limited cold-start tolerance, we recommend adding one more flexible tile (total 10) to avoid oscillation and provide ~12–15% extra headroom for unexpected noise.

Networking considerations:
- Keep egress under 18 Gbps: with 150 outbound avg tokens and protobuf framing, estimated egress ≈ 12.5 Gbps at 90 qps. During 250 qps spikes, burst egress hits ≈ 35 Gbps unless truncated — so we recommend token chunking + streaming rate caps and an egress throttle policy in the router to guarantee the 18 Gbps transit limit (drop/trim non-essential telemetry).

Operational runbook (short):
- Warm pool prefetch: pre-warm KV caches for top-10 prefixes hourly; maintain a periodic replay to prevent eviction.
- Admission control thresholds: set soft queue threshold at 75% of tile concurrency, hard throttle at 95%. Soft threshold triggers fallback to quantized model (p95 target relax), hard threshold returns 429s for non-critical calls.
- Failover policy: start model fallback after 30s of sustained soft-threshold violations; graceful draining of requests to burst tiles with priority to latency-sensitive routes.
