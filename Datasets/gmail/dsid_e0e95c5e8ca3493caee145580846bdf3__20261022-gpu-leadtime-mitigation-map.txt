Raj — thanks for the note and the ETA summary. High level thoughts before we formalize the MAP:

1) Staged reservation (recommended): commit to a 25–40% initial pool (reserved capacity) to hit an early pilot, with pre-agreed scale milestones tied to shipment windows (30/60/90 days after first ship). That lets Nimbus validate workloads and SLOs while avoiding a single large upfront capex.

2) Buffering options we can include in the MAP:
   - Burst credits: inexpensive short-term burst capacity on hosted nodes; immediate but subject to availability.
   - Held standby pool: reserved cold GPUs (lower utilization) billed at a lower hourly rate but guaranteed capacity — good for critical SLOs.
   - Cross-region fallback: route non-latency-critical traffic to alternative regions during shortages.

3) Queueing and preemption policy: define which workloads are preemptible (batch/async) vs. non-preemptible (prod inference). Include a sizing spreadsheet so procurement can see incremental cost vs. risk.
