Karthik — quick read and proposed approach below. See attached redline (Redline_IP_Responses_20260720.docx) for full text. Key positions: 

1) Scope of Redwood indemnity: agree to indemnify Customer for third-party claims alleging that Redwood’s unmodified Service (including models and hosted runtime) infringes, PROVIDED THAT:
   - Customer notifies Redwood promptly and cooperates;
   - Redwood has sole control of defense and settlement (we can engage counsel at our expense);
   - Any settlement must not (a) require Redwood to admit liability, (b) impose ongoing obligations (e.g., royalty/license grants) without Redwood’s express consent, or (c) require Customer to pay amounts beyond money damages apportioned to Redwood.

2) Carveouts we will preserve: claims arising from (a) Customer Data, (b) Customer modifications or combination of the Service with Customer systems where infringement is caused by the modification/combination, (c) third-party components where indemnity obligations are limited by upstream licenses.

3) Settlement authority & notice windows: counter-propose that Customer may not settle a claim on Redwood’s behalf. If Customer proposes a settlement, Redwood must consent; if consent is withheld, parties will engage in good-faith negotiation for 30 days before either can unilaterally settle an element that would bind the other. The 10-business-day auto-settle is not acceptable.

4) Escrow / holdback: decline 10% holdback on hosted SaaS. Suggest instead either: (a) no escrow for hosted API, or (b) a 1% holdback capped at $50k for delivered software/privileged IP where the customer receives software assets. If Globex insists on escrow for SaaS, require it to be narrowly tailored (short release window, escrow agent with explicit dispute release mechanics).

5) Remedies & caps: preserve our general liability cap and contractually exclude equitable remedies that force recurring obligations. We should also make clear indemnity payouts are subject to the general commercial cap/exclusions (e.g., exclusions for consequential damages).

Deliverable: I’ve prepared a clean reply + tracked redline calling out these changes. Recommend we offer the below compromise to close quickly: Redwood control of defense + settlement restrictions (as above) + 1% holdback for SaaS capped at $50k, or 10% holdback only for on-prem perpetual software deliveries. If they accept, procurement can move to DocuSign.
