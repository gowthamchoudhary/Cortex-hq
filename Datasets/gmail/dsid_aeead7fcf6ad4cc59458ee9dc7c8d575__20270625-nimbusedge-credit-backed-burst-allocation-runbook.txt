Rishi / team — quick sizing notes (high level):

Assumptions:
- Baseline committed: 32 80GB GPUs (NVIDIA H100-equivalent). Typical seq length 512, avg tokens/sec per GPU: ~1200 for their model family.
- Peak scenarios: 10% (3 GPUs), 50% (16 GPUs), 200% (64 GPUs) additional over baseline.

Estimated implications:
- 10% burst: handled entirely in-cache with negligible latency change; recommend 1:1 mapping to credits (1 credit unit = 1 GPU-minute at baseline perf).
- 50% burst: recommend 50% of burst served via pre-warmed preemptible slab (24 GPUs preemptible), remaining  (if any) via short-lived ephemeral nodes; expected 95th percentile latency increase ~15–25% under sustained 15-min windows.
- 200% burst: requires broad scale — propose credit cap + automated throttling to maintain 99.9% control-plane response. Use regional failover to secondary region for non-low-latency tiers (embeddings/async batch).

Autoscale policy (suggested):
- Scale-up trigger: queue depth > 50 requests/queue OR average utilization > 75% for 2 minutes.
- Preemptible backfill: activate up to 40% of requested burst as preemptible by default (fast spin-up, lower cost).
- Hard cap: do not exceed baseline + credit_pool + preemptible_max. If cap reached, circuit-breaker throttles by priority class (foreground low-latency traffic first).
- Credit accounting: 1 credit = 60 GPU-seconds at baseline throughput; monthly replenish X credits (negotiable).

Cost delta: estimate incremental cost for 50% burst (24 GPUs preemptible for 15 mins daily over month) ~ +12% unit cost vs baseline. Exact numbers in attached capacity-model-approx.csv.
